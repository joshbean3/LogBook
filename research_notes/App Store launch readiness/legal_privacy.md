# Legal, privacy and third-party terms for Logbook (US individual developer, health data, third-party AI)

Researched 2026-11-10. Fact-checked against primary sources 2026-11-10: checked claims carry "(verified 2026-11-10)", "(corrected 2026-11-10: ...)" or "(unverified ...)" in the Requirements table. Rows with no mark were not re-checked. This is research, not legal advice. Items marked LAWYER are the ones where a licensed attorney is genuinely needed.

Quotations are verbatim from the fetched pages unless marked "summary". Where a page could not be read (403, paywall, script-rendered), that is said in Gaps and nothing is asserted from it.

Source type labels used throughout:
- **Apple rule**: App Review Guidelines, Developer Program License Agreement (DPLA), Apple standard EULA and minimum EULA terms
- **Apple guidance**: developer documentation, App Store Connect Help, support pages, marketing guidelines
- **Law or regulator**: statute or rule text, or a page published by the regulator (FTC, state attorney general, CPPA, legislation.gov.uk, EU regulation text)
- **Official vendor docs**: Strava, Anthropic, Fly.io legal and developer pages
- **Practitioner report**: law firm or trade summaries. Used only where no primary source could be read, and labelled
- **Repo**: something seen in /home/user/LogBook during this research. A spot check, not an audit

## 1. What must the privacy policy say, by Apple's rules and by law?

### Takeaway
One hosted policy can satisfy Apple 5.1.1(i), HealthKit, CalOPPA and Strava if it is written to a GDPR-style outline and names Anthropic, Strava and Fly.io with retention periods. Washington and Nevada need an additional, separate "Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy" page. The current in-app promise "never sold or shared" is not literally true while data goes to Anthropic and should be reworded.

### Cited Findings
- Apple 5.1.1(i): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner." The policy must "Identify what data, if any, the app/service collects, how it collects that data, and all uses of that data"; "Confirm that any third party with whom an app shares user data ... will provide the same or equal protection of user data as stated in the app's privacy policy and required by these Guidelines"; and "Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data." [Apple rule] [G#5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.1)
- Apple 5.1.3(i): "You must disclose the specific health data that you are collecting from the device." [Apple rule] [G#5.1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.3)
- HealthKit documentation: "You must also provide a privacy policy for any app that uses the HealthKit framework." Apple points to the ONC "Personal Health Record model (for non-HIPAA apps)" as a model and adds "These models aren't intended to replace a web-based privacy policy". "You must clearly disclose to the user how you and your app will use their HealthKit data." [Apple guidance] [Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)
- Strava API Policy 7.3: "You must provide a lawful privacy policy for your Developer Application that meets the requirements of the GDPR" (and UK GDPR per the sibling note's reading), reachable through prominent links, which must not "modify, conflict with, or supersede the Strava Privacy Policy". 6.5: "Strava may monitor and collect Usage Data and may use Usage Data for any business purpose", and "You agree to include a statement to this effect in your privacy policy." [Official vendor docs] [SP](https://www.strava.com/legal/api_policy)
- CalOPPA, as described by the California Attorney General: the policy must be "conspicuously" posted and, for an online service, "reasonably accessible ... for consumers of the online service"; a website policy may be adequate for an app only if a link is "reasonably accessible" within the app; an operator is in violation only if it fails to post within 30 days after being notified; penalties can reach $2,500 per violation through the Unfair Competition Law. Required contents per the summaries: categories of personal information collected, categories of third parties, any review and correction process, effective date, how changes are communicated, response to Do Not Track signals. [Law or regulator, via AG documents and search summaries; statute page returned 403] [CA AG, Privacy on the Go](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press_releases/privacy_on_the_go.pdf); [BPC 22575](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22575)
- FTC health privacy guidance (August 2024): "Health information" is anything that "conveys information or enables an inference about a consumer's health"; "take a broad view of what constitutes health data and handle it accordingly". Businesses must check what they say, imply and omit "to make sure you don't create a deceptive or misleading impression". "disclosing consumers' health information for advertising without their affirmative express consent may be an unfair practice." The page names BetterHelp, GoodRx and Premom. [Law or regulator] [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/collecting-using-or-sharing-consumer-health-information-look-hipaa-ftc-act-health-breach)
- Washington: the Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy "must be a separate and distinct link on the regulated entity's homepage" and "may not contain additional information not required under the My Health My Data Act." "Homepage" for an app is the app's platform or download page and includes a link within the app such as in settings or an about section (summary of RCW 19.373.010(16)). [Law or regulator] [WA AG FAQ](https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy); [RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010)
- Nevada SB 370 sec. 21: the policy must "clearly and conspicuously" establish (a) categories of consumer health data collected and how used, (b) categories of sources, (c) categories shared, (d) categories of third parties and affiliates, (e) purposes, (f) manner of processing, (g) how to submit a request, (h) any process to review and request changes, (i) how consumers are told of material changes, (j) "Whether a third party may collect consumer health data over time and across different Internet websites or online services", (k) effective date. It must be linked "conspicuously on the main Internet website" or otherwise provided clearly. [Law or regulator] [NV](https://archive.leg.state.nv.us/Session/82nd2023/Bills/SB/SB370_EN.pdf)
- Repo: `PRIVACY_PROMISE = 'Your data is yours. It is never sold or shared.'` (apps/web/src/features/auth/PrivacyFacts.tsx). The same file lists what is "Sent to Claude, by Anthropic". No hosted policy URL, terms URL or mailto link was found by grep in apps/web/src. [Repo]

### Inferences
- Policy outline that covers every source above (each heading short, plain words):
  1. Who we are and how to reach us (name or entity, email, postal address if required by Strava's GDPR-grade rule).
  2. What we collect, by source: account (email, name, password hash); what you enter (plan, sessions, habits, to-dos, goals, journal, learned); Apple Health types, listed one by one; Strava data, listed; files and photos you give the assistant; assistant conversations; device and log data; notification settings (local only).
  3. Why we use it (run the app, compute scores, answer the assistant, reminders, security). One line that nothing is used for ads, tracking or sale.
  4. Who receives it: Anthropic (AI answers, only what you allow, never Strava data), Fly.io (hosting), Strava (when you connect; Strava may collect and use data about your use of its API), email provider if any. State that each is bound to equal protection.
  5. Your choices: the consent switches, disconnect Health sync, disconnect Strava, turn off reminders; how to withdraw each.
  6. Retention table (section 12) and deletion: in-app delete, what happens at processors and in backups.
  7. Your rights and how to ask: access, copy, correction, deletion, response time (45 days fits Washington and Nevada for access requests; Nevada deletion requests must be completed within 30 days, NRS 603A.515, corrected 2026-11-10).
  8. Security in one paragraph.
  9. Children and minimum age.
  10. Changes to this policy and effective date.
  11. State sections: link to the Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy; California Do Not Track line ("the app does not track across sites and does not respond to DNT signals"; verify wording with counsel).
- The Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy should be a short stand-alone page that repeats only the health-data facts: categories, sources (you, Apple Health, Strava), purposes, what is shared and with which categories of third parties (none sold; processors listed), and how to exercise rights and appeal.
- The "never shared" promise is the single most likely sentence to be read as deceptive, because Apple's 5.1.2(i) itself describes sending data to third-party AI as sharing. A narrower promise is both true and still strong.

## 3. Does the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (as revised in 2024) cover Logbook, and what counts as a breach?

### Takeaway
Probably yes. The FTC's own example says a fitness app that can pull from the user and a wearable is "likely a vendor of personal health records". A breach includes any unauthorized disclosure, not only a hack. The duties are notice to users, the FTC and sometimes media within 60 days.

### Cited Findings
- The FTC announced the final rule on April 26, 2024; it "clarifies that the HBNR applies to health apps and similar technologies that are not covered by HIPAA", revises "PHR identifiable health information", adds "covered health care provider" and "health care services or supplies", makes clear a breach includes unauthorized acquisition "resulting from a data security breach or an unauthorized disclosure", clarifies "multiple sources", expands notice content (naming third parties that acquired the data), and takes effect 60 days after Federal Register publication. The FTC's rule page lists the Federal Register notice dated May 30, 2024. [Law or regulator] [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-finalizes-changes-health-breach-notification-rule); [FTC rule page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/health-breach-notification-rule)
- 16 CFR 318.2 (summary of the text as fetched): "Health care services or supplies" includes "any online service such as a website, mobile application, or internet-connected device" that provides tools to track "diseases, conditions, treatments, medications, vital signs, fitness, fertility, sleep, mental health, genetic information, diet". A "personal health record" is "an electronic record of PHR identifiable health information on an individual" with the technical capacity to draw from multiple sources and managed by or primarily for the individual. "Breach of security" is "acquisition of such information without the authorization of the individual"; unauthorized access is presumed to be acquisition unless reliable evidence shows otherwise. "Unsecured" means not protected by a technology specified in HHS guidance. [Law or regulator, text via Cornell LII] [16 CFR 318.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/318.2)
- FTC compliance guidance (July 2024, edited January 2025): a fitness app that can take data from the user and a wearable is "likely a vendor of personal health records", even if some users never connect a tracker. A breach "is not limited to cybersecurity intrusions"; sharing health data with a platform without consent counts. Notice to individuals "without unreasonable delay" and "within 60 calendar days after the breach is discovered"; FTC notice at the same time for 500 or more people, otherwise "within 60 calendar days following the end of the calendar year"; "prominent media outlets" when 500 or more residents of a state are affected; civil penalty "up to $53,088 per violation". [Law or regulator] [HBNR guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-ftcs-health-breach-notification-rule-0)

### Inferences
- Effective date: 60 days after a May 30, 2024 publication computes to July 29, 2024, which matches the FTC's reference to "the July 2024 amendments". The Federal Register citation itself was not retrieved.
- Logbook holds sleep, heart rate, HRV, workouts and journal text per identified user, drawn from Apple Health, Strava and the user. That fits the FTC's own example closely.
- Practical meaning: any path by which health data reaches a party the user did not authorize is a reportable event. That includes a misconfigured log shipper, an error tracker capturing request bodies, an AI call made with consent off, or a bug that shows one user's data to another.
- Encryption at rest and in transit matters twice: it is expected security, and data that meets the HHS standard for "secured" falls outside the rule's trigger for theft of storage media. It does not help when the app itself discloses data.

### Gaps
- The eCFR and Federal Register sites blocked automated reading; rule text came from Cornell LII and FTC pages. Sections 318.3 to 318.7 were not read verbatim. (Corrected 2026-11-10: the eCFR renderer endpoint https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=318 is readable; 318.2, 318.3, 318.4 and 318.5 were read there and match the findings above. The penalty figure was checked in 16 CFR 1.98(d): $53,088.)
- The HHS guidance that defines "unsecured" (which encryption counts) was not read.

## 4. Do Washington's My Health My Data Act and Nevada's SB 370 cover a small developer outside those states?

### Takeaway
Probably yes for both, once a resident of the state uses the app. Neither law has a revenue or user-count floor. Washington adds a private right of action through its Consumer Protection Act; Nevada does not. Both need a dedicated consumer health data privacy policy, opt-in consent, and 45-day responses to requests (corrected 2026-11-10: Nevada deletion requests have a shorter 30-day limit, NRS 603A.515).

### Cited Findings
- Washington "consumer health data" is "personal information that is linked or reasonably linkable to a consumer and that identifies the consumer's past, present, or future physical or mental health status", including "Bodily functions, vital signs, symptoms, or measurements of the information described in this subsection (8)(b)", "Biometric data", and information "derived or extrapolated from nonhealth information". [Law or regulator] [RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010)
- "Regulated entity" means any legal entity that conducts business in Washington or provides products or services targeted to Washington consumers and "alone or jointly with others, determines the purpose and means of collecting, processing, sharing, or selling" consumer health data. "Small business" is a regulated entity that "Collects, processes, sells, or shares consumer health data of fewer than 100,000 consumers during a calendar year" (or meets a second, revenue-share test). "Person" includes "natural persons, corporations, trusts, unincorporated associations, and partnerships". [Law or regulator] [RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010)
- "Consent" is "a clear affirmative act that signifies a consumer's freely given, specific, informed, opt-in, voluntary, and unambiguous agreement"; it cannot come from acceptance of "a general or broad terms of use agreement", from hovering, muting, pausing or closing content, or from deceptive design. [Law or regulator] [RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010)
- Collection is allowed with consent for a specified purpose or to the extent necessary to provide a product or service the consumer requested; sharing needs consent "separate and distinct from the consent obtained to collect" or necessity for a requested service. "Share" excludes disclosures to a processor consistent with the purpose. A processor may act only under "a binding contract". Rights: confirm and access, including a list of third parties; withdraw consent; delete, reaching affiliates, processors and third parties; 45 days plus one 45-day extension; an appeal process. Security must meet the "reasonable standard of care" in the industry. (Summary of .030 to .060.) [Law or regulator] [MHMD](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373&full=true)
- Washington AG: "Small businesses, as defined in the Act, must comply with sections 4 through 9 beginning June 30, 2024." "An entity that only stores data in Washington is not a regulated entity." "an app that tracks someone's digestion or perspiration is collecting consumer health data." Any violation is a per se violation of the Consumer Protection Act, "enforced by the Attorney General as well as through private action." [Law or regulator] [WA AG FAQ](https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy)
- Nevada SB 370: "Regulated entity" means "any person who: 1. Conducts business in this State or produces or provides products or services that are targeted to consumers in this State; and 2. Alone or with other persons, determines the purpose and means of processing, sharing or selling consumer health data." "Consumer" is "a natural person who has requested a product or service from a regulated entity and who resides in this State or whose consumer health data is collected in this State." "Consumer health data" is information "that a regulated entity uses to identify the past, present or future health status of the consumer", including "Bodily functions, vital signs or symptoms" and inferred data; it excludes information used to "Provide access to or enable gameplay by a person on a video game platform". [Law or regulator] [NV](https://archive.leg.state.nv.us/Session/82nd2023/Bills/SB/SB370_EN.pdf)
- Nevada enforcement and date: a violation "constitutes a deceptive trade practice"; the provisions "Do not create a private right of action"; "This act becomes effective on March 31, 2024." Security policies must "Satisfy the standard of care in the industry" and "Be reasonable, taking into account the volume and nature of the consumer health data at issue." Requests are answered within 45 days of authentication (verified 2026-11-10 in NRS 603A.510; corrected: deletion requests are separate, "Not later than 30 days after authenticating a request", with processors and third parties notified, and backups allowed up to 2 years, NRS 603A.515, https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-603A.html). [Law or regulator] [NV](https://archive.leg.state.nv.us/Session/82nd2023/Bills/SB/SB370_EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- Sleep stages, heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, blood oxygen, wrist temperature and recovery scores are "vital signs" and "measurements" in the Washington sense and "bodily functions, vital signs" in Nevada's. Journal text and training status (sick, injured) can also reveal health status.
- Anthropic and Fly.io fit the "processor" role if they act only under contract for Logbook's purposes. Anthropic's DPA says "Customer is the controller and Anthropic is Customer's processor" (section 9). Sending a health summary to Claude to answer the user's own question is then processing, not "sharing", but the separate consent Logbook already uses is still the safer design and is what Apple requires anyway.
- Nevada's wording "that a regulated entity uses to identify ... health status" is narrower than Washington's; Logbook plainly uses the data that way (recovery and sleep scores).
- Washington's private right of action is the largest legal exposure for a solo developer in this whole list: any Washington user can sue under the Consumer Protection Act for a missing health data policy or missing consent.

### Gaps
- Whether an individual sole proprietor is a "legal entity" under RCW 19.373.010(23), and whether a nationally available free app "conducts business in Washington", are legal questions. LAWYER. The cautious reading is yes.
- Fly.io's data processing agreement was not fetched; whether it is a "binding contract" in the Washington sense is unverified.
- Sections .020 and .040 were read through a summarizing fetch, not line by line; the policy's exact required items should be copied from the statute when drafting.

## 5. If the app is distributed in the EU and UK, what do GDPR and UK GDPR require?

### Takeaway
Listing the app on EU or UK storefronts would probably bring GDPR and UK GDPR into play, with explicit consent for health data, a representative in each, a DPIA and 72-hour breach reporting. For a solo developer the proportionate move is to launch in the US only and add those storefronts after a dedicated compliance pass.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 3(2): the Regulation applies to processing of personal data of data subjects in the Union "by a controller or processor not established in the Union, where the processing activities are related to: (a) the offering of goods or services, irrespective of whether a payment of the data subject is required, to such data subjects in the Union; or (b) the monitoring of their behaviour". [Law or regulator] [GDPR Art. 3](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/)
- Recital 23 (summary): mere accessibility of a website or contact details from the EU is not enough; using a Member State's language or currency with the possibility of ordering, or mentioning EU customers, may show intent to offer services there. [Law or regulator] [Recital 23](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-23/)
- Art. 9(1): processing of "data concerning health" "shall be prohibited" unless a 9(2) condition applies; 9(2)(a): "the data subject has given explicit consent to the processing of those personal data for one or more specified purposes". [Law or regulator] [GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)
- Art. 27: where Art. 3(2) applies "the controller or the processor shall designate in writing a representative in the Union", except for processing that is "occasional, does not include, on a large scale, processing of special categories of data" and is unlikely to result in a risk. [Law or regulator] [GDPR Art. 27](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-27-gdpr/)
- UK GDPR Art. 27 remains in force with the same structure (representative in the United Kingdom); the page shows a consequential amendment dated September 30, 2026 under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 and no outstanding changes. [Law or regulator] [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/27)
- Art. 35(1) and (3)(b): a DPIA is required for processing "in particular using new technologies" that "is likely to result in a high risk", in particular "processing on a large scale of special categories of data referred to in Article 9(1)". [Law or regulator] [GDPR Art. 35](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-35-gdpr/)
- Art. 33(1): notify the supervisory authority "without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after having become aware of it" unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk. [Law or regulator] [GDPR Art. 33](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-33-gdpr/)
- Art. 8(1): for consent-based online services offered to a child, processing is lawful "where the child is at least 16 years old"; "Member States may provide by law for a lower age ... not below 13 years." [Law or regulator] [GDPR Art. 8](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-8-gdpr/)
- Apple 5.1.1(ii): "Apps that collect data for a legitimate interest without consent by relying on the terms of the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation ("GDPR") or similar statute must comply with all terms of that law." [Apple rule] [G#5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.1)
- Anthropic's Data Processing Addendum (effective February 24, 2025): "Customer is the controller and Anthropic is Customer's processor"; EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Modules Two and Three), a UK Addendum and a Swiss Addendum apply to transfers; subprocessors are listed at anthropic.com/subprocessors; Anthropic notifies the customer of a security breach "without undue delay, but in any event within 48 hours"; it assists with data subject requests. The DPA is incorporated into the Commercial Terms by reference. [Official vendor docs] [Anthropic DPA](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/data-processing-addendum); [ACT](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)

### Inferences
- Choosing EU countries in App Store Connect availability, with an App Store page shown in local storefronts, is a deliberate offer of a service to people there. Continuous syncing of health data is not "occasional", so the Art. 27 exemption is unlikely to hold.
- Lawful bases for a later EU launch would be: contract (Art. 6(1)(b)) for the account and core features; explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)) for all health data, including the server sync itself and each AI use. Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent and must stop processing.
- The existing two-switch consent model, the "Sent" and "Never sent" lists and in-app deletion are a good base. What is missing for the EU is paperwork: representative, DPIA, records of processing, transfer wording, rights procedure.
- A US-only launch also sidesteps EU AI Act duties and reduces the DSA question to a declaration.

### Gaps
- GDPR Chapter III (rights of access, erasure, portability and the rest), Art. 13 notice contents, Art. 28 processor terms and Art. 30 records were not read in this pass. They apply if the EU or UK is served.
- EDPB guidelines on territorial scope and on what "large scale" means were not read; the ICO's special category guidance returned 403.
- Whether Apple distribution in a storefront alone meets the "offering" test has no authority cited here. LAWYER before any EU or UK launch.
- Canada (PIPEDA) and other non-US storefronts were not researched. If Canada is included at launch, that needs its own check.

## 6. COPPA, minimum age and age assurance

### Takeaway
COPPA does not apply to a general audience app without actual knowledge of under-13 users, but the developer must act if it learns of one. Logbook needs a stated minimum age. 18+ is the simplest legal position for health data plus AI; 13+ keeps a wider audience but triggers Anthropic's minors safeguards. State app store age laws may add a duty to read Apple's age range signal.

### Cited Findings
- COPPA covers services "directed to children under 13" and general audience operators with "actual knowledge" they collect personal information from children under 13. Operators are not required to ask ages. A neutral age screen is acceptable; a checkbox reading "I am over 12 years old" is not neutral. If an operator learns a specific user is under 13 it must meet notice and parental consent requirements or delete the child's information. "The COPPA Rule was amended on April 22, 2025." (FAQ, partly summary.) [Law or regulator] [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)
- FTC, January 16, 2025: the amended rule requires separate parental consent for third-party disclosures, says "operators cannot retain the information indefinitely", and adds biometric and government identifiers to "personal information"; effective 60 days after Federal Register publication, with compliance one year after publication for most provisions. [Law or regulator] [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data)
- Apple 5.1.4: apps that "collect, transmit, or have the capability to share personal information ... from a minor must include a privacy policy and must comply with all applicable children's privacy statutes." [Apple rule] [G#5.1.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.4)
- Strava API Policy 5.15 applies extra duties if the app "is directed to or knowingly collects data from children under thirteen (13)"; "Strava's service is not directed to children under thirteen (13)." [Official vendor docs] [SP](https://www.strava.com/legal/api_policy)
- Anthropic Usage Policy: a minor is "any individual under the age of 18 years old, regardless of jurisdiction"; "Products serving minors" must follow the additional guidelines in Anthropic's support article "and all applicable laws". The article lists age verification, content moderation and filtering, monitoring and reporting, a public statement of COPPA compliance, AI disclosure, and periodic audits by Anthropic. [Official vendor docs] [AUP](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup); [Anthropic support article](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9307344-responsible-use-of-anthropic-s-models-guidelines-for-organizations-serving-minors)
- Apple age assurance: "developers are responsible for their own age restrictions. In certain regions, where legally required, Apple uses age assurance methods to confirm an Apple Account holder's age and shares age categories with you through the Declared Age Range API. In those regions, you must check the age of the people using your app." For significant updates, "Until the parent provides consent, the child must be prevented from accessing the significant update". On revocation "Apple will prevent the app from launching". For obligations, "consult your legal counsel." [Apple guidance] [Age assurance](https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance)
- submission_requirements.md (SUB-29, SUB-40) records Apple's age rating tiers (4+, 9+, 13+, 16+, 18+), the rule that a EULA minimum age above the computed rating forces an override, and Apple news naming Texas (new Texas Apple Accounts from June 4, 2026), Utah, Louisiana, Brazil, Australia and Singapore for age assurance. [Apple rule and guidance, via sibling note]
- Repo: the signup screens ask no age or birth date. [Repo]

### Inferences
- By computation from the FTC FAQ's April 22, 2025 date, the amended COPPA rule took effect around June 23, 2025 with general compliance due around April 22, 2026. These dates were not seen on a primary page.
- Option A, 18+: terms and policy say adults only; the age rating must be overridden to 18+; no Anthropic minors safeguards needed; cleanest under Washington, GDPR Art. 8 and state minor-protection laws. Cost: smaller audience and the 18+ badge.
- Option B, 13+: rating 13+; must add Anthropic's safeguards and take a position on state laws about minors and AI; GDPR Art. 8 would require parental consent for 13 to 15 year olds in some EU states.
- Either way: no age checkbox; one sentence at signup ("Logbook is for people 18 and older" or "13 and older"); a support procedure to delete accounts of children under 13.

### Gaps
- Which US states' app store age laws are in force, enjoined or pending on 2026-11-10 was not verified from primary sources; Apple's page names no states. LAWYER. (Corrected 2026-11-10: Apple's news post of June 3, 2026, https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=sg176nne, confirms Texas SB 2420 applies to new Apple Accounts in Texas from June 4, 2026 after the injunction was lifted. Utah and Louisiana remain unverified from a primary source.)
- Whether Anthropic permits under-18 users at all in API-powered products is not stated on the page read; it describes safeguards, not permission.

## 7. Terms of service and EULA: Apple's standard EULA or a custom one?

### Takeaway
Apple's standard EULA applies automatically and is enough to license the app, but it says nothing about accounts, acceptable use, AI output, health disclaimers or the service behind the app. The recommended set is: Apple's standard EULA for the binary, plus the developer's own Terms of Service accepted at signup. A custom EULA is optional and, if used, must carry Apple's ten minimum terms and the developer's name and address.

### Cited Findings
- Standard EULA: apps are licensed, not sold; the licence is subject to the Standard EULA or "a custom end user license agreement between you and the Application Provider". It grants "a nontransferable license to use the Licensed Application"; external services are used "at your sole risk" and data shown, including medical information, "is for general informational purposes only and is not guaranteed by Licensor"; the app is provided "AS IS"; liability is capped at "fifty dollars ($50.00)"; California law governs, with exceptions for some non-US users. It contains no AI terms and no dedicated medical disclaimer. [Apple rule] [Standard EULA](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/dev/stdeula/)
- Minimum terms for a custom EULA: (1) "the EULA is concluded between You and the End-User only, and not with Apple"; (2) licence "limited to a non-transferable license to use the Licensed Application"; (3) "Apple has no obligation whatsoever to furnish any maintenance and support services"; (4) on warranty failure "Apple will refund the purchase price"; (5) "The EULA may not limit Your liability to the End-User beyond what is permitted by applicable law" and the developer handles product claims; (6) the developer handles IP claims; (7) the user represents they are not in an embargoed country or on a restricted list; (8) "You must state in the EULA Your name and address, and the contact information"; (9) the user "must comply with applicable third party terms of agreement"; (10) "Apple, and Apple's subsidiaries, are third party beneficiaries of the EULA." [Apple rule] [Minimum terms](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/dev/minterms/)
- Anthropic Commercial Terms D.3: the customer "must notify its Users, that factual assertions in Outputs should not be relied upon without independently checking their accuracy". [Official vendor docs] [ACT](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- Washington consent cannot be obtained through "A consumer's acceptance of a general or broad terms of use agreement". [Law or regulator] [RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010)

### Inferences
- Terms of Service contents worth having at launch (short, plain): who can use the app (minimum age); the account and its security; what the app is and is not (not medical advice; AI can be wrong; no emergency use); acceptable use (no misuse of the assistant, no uploading others' data); third-party services (Apple Health, Strava, Anthropic) and their own terms; licence to user content limited to running the service; ending or deleting the account; warranty disclaimer and liability limit to the extent law allows; governing law and venue; how terms change; contact.
- Health and AI consents must stay out of the terms and remain separate in-app choices, because Washington, GDPR and Apple all reject consent bundled into general terms.
- Acceptance should be an affirmative act at signup with links to both documents ("By creating an account you agree to the Terms and have read the Privacy Policy"), and the accepted version and time should be stored.
- An individual who writes a custom EULA must print a postal address in it. That and Apple 5.1.1(ix) point the same way: form an LLC. (Corrected 2026-11-10: Anthropic's terms also name "you" as a possible Customer, so they are not a third reason.)

### Gaps
- Enforceability of liability caps, arbitration clauses and class waivers for a health-adjacent consumer app varies by state and was not researched. LAWYER to draft or review the Terms; this is the document where a lawyer adds the most value.
- No authority was read on whether an App Store app needs Terms beyond the Apple EULA; this is practice, not a rule.

## 8. Medical and fitness disclaimers

### Takeaway
No rule read here forces a specific disclaimer on a fitness app, but Apple expects medical apps to tell users to check with a doctor, Apple's standard EULA already treats medical information as "general informational purposes only", Anthropic bars AI medical recommendations without human review, and FDA's wellness policy turns on claims. A short, visible "not medical advice" line plus tight wording of health scores covers all four.

### Cited Findings
- Apple 1.4.1: "Medical apps that could provide inaccurate data or information, or that could be used for diagnosing or treating patients may be reviewed with greater scrutiny." "Apps must clearly disclose data and methodology to support accuracy claims relating to health measurements". "Apps should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions." [Apple rule] [G#1.4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.4.1)
- FDA issued a revised final General Wellness guidance on January 6, 2026, superseding the 2019 version; it addresses wearables that report physiologic values (including heart rate variability and oxygen saturation) and makes general wellness status depend on how the product is advertised and promoted; firms advise keeping claims to general wellness and away from specific diseases or conditions. [Practitioner report] [Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products); [Faegre Drinker](https://faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/1/key-updates-in-fdas-2026-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance)
- Anthropic Usage Policy, medical high-risk area: "diagnosing a condition; recommending, starting, stopping, or adjusting a treatment or medication, including dose", plus interpreting symptoms or test results and assessing or treating mental health conditions; exclusions include "General information or educational content" and "Wellness content, such as education on sleep, stress, nutrition, or exercise." [Official vendor docs] [AUP](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)
- submission_requirements.md (SUB-28): new apps in Health & Fitness distributed in the US, UK or EEA must declare regulated medical device status (since March 26, 2026). [Apple rule, via sibling note]
- Repo: grep for "not medical", "medical advice", "doctor", "physician" in apps/web/src and apps/api/src/services/assistant.ts found only an emoji keyword rule; no disclaimer text. [Repo]

### Inferences
- Where the disclaimer should appear: Terms; one line on the plan-build beat of onboarding; the (i) explainer on Recovery, Strain, Sleep and each vitals tile ("This is a fitness estimate from your own data. It is not a medical reading."); the assistant's first-run sheet; and inside the model's instructions so answers carry it when relevant.
- Wording of scores matters more than the disclaimer. "Higher than your normal" is wellness. "May indicate illness" is a medical claim under both Apple 1.4.1 and FDA's approach.
- Training status values "Sick" and "Injured" are user-entered labels and fine, as long as the app does not infer them from vitals.
- A "start an exercise program" consult-a-physician line is conventional in fitness products; no primary source requiring it was found, so it is listed as good practice only.

### Gaps
- The FDA guidance document itself (fda.gov/media/90652) was not read; statements about it are from law firm summaries.
- No case law or regulator statement was found on liability for AI training advice that leads to injury. LAWYER input belongs in the Terms' assumption-of-risk and liability sections.

## 9. AI-specific duties: Apple 5.1.2(i), Anthropic's terms and policies, state and EU AI rules

### Takeaway
Three layers apply. Apple: name the AI provider, say what is sent, and get explicit permission before any personal data goes. Anthropic: tell users they are talking to AI, tell them outputs can be wrong, keep the coach out of medical recommendations, and describe retention accurately (30 days by default, not zero). Law: several states now require chatbot disclosure and bar AI from posing as a licensed health professional. Logbook's consent switches already cover most of the Apple layer; the "Remember when" and attachment flows sit outside them.

### Cited Findings
- Apple 5.1.2(i): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." 5.1.2(ii): "Data collected for one purpose may not be repurposed without further consent unless otherwise explicitly permitted by law." [Official vendor docs] [Anthropic privacy center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)

Sources disagree on some dates and counts. ### Inferences
- Consent coverage gap: the journal entry sent daily for "Remember when" is personal, often sensitive, and is sent on a schedule, not at the user's request. - Attachments are user-initiated, which is strong evidence of intent, but the first attach should still show a one-time line: "This file goes to Claude, by Anthropic, to answer you."
- The four duties to users can be met with very little UI: an "AI" label on the assistant; one line "It can be wrong. Check anything important."; the consent screens that already name Anthropic; and model instructions that refuse medical recommendations.
- Policy wording on Anthropic should be exact: "not used to train", "deleted within 30 days", "kept longer only if flagged for abuse or required by law". If ZDR is obtained later, update the sentence.
- The individual-versus-entity wording in the Commercial Terms is a contract formation question, not a product question. (Corrected 2026-11-10: the terms read "you or the organization ... that you represent", so an individual developer can contract; the open point is only the line "Services under these Terms are not for consumer use", which is about how the API is used, not who signs.)
- The Usage Policy page showed an effective date two days after the research date, so a new version takes effect on November 12, 2026. The text read is that version; the outgoing version was not compared.

### Gaps
- Statute texts for Maine, Utah, California AB 489 and SB 243, New York and others were not read; all state AI findings are from secondary sources, which conflict on some dates. LAWYER for a state-by-state read if the assistant's behaviour or branding moves toward companionship or health advice.
- Whether the configured model is ZDR-eligible and whether the beta feature in use is covered were not confirmed; the Covered Models list read names only two other model families.
- Anthropic's trademark and brand guidelines for saying "Claude" or "Powered by Claude" in an app were not fetched. Factual naming in consent copy is required by Apple's rule; logo use needs a check.
- Apple has not said whether a general AI provider "provide[s] a health or fitness service to the user" for the HealthKit third-party rule (also noted in review_guidelines.md).

[Apple rule] [G#5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.2); [G#5.1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.3)

## 12. Security expectations, breach response, retention and deletion

### Takeaway
No statute read here prescribes exact controls for an app like this; the standard is "reasonable" security for health data, which regulators describe as encryption in transit and at rest, salted slow password hashing, least privilege and tested authentication. Logbook meets the password point and gets volume encryption from Fly.io by default. What is missing is written: a breach runbook with four clocks, and a retention table the policy can quote.

### Cited Findings
- FTC best practices for mobile health apps (December 2022): "Do you use strong encryption at rest and in transit?"; "Don't store passwords in clear text."; "Add salt – random data – to hashed passwords and consider using slow hash functions."; "Make sure your app doesn't access consumer information it doesn't need."; "Invest resources in the design, implementation, and testing of authentication."; "get users' affirmative express consent before you collect or share that data." [Law or regulator] [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/mobile-health-app-developers-ftc-best-practices)
- Washington requires security that meets the "reasonable standard of care" in the industry and access limited to what is necessary; Nevada requires policies that "Satisfy the standard of care in the industry" and are "reasonable, taking into account the volume and nature of the consumer health data at issue." [Law or regulator] [MHMD](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373&full=true); [NV](https://archive.leg.state.nv.us/Session/82nd2023/Bills/SB/SB370_EN.pdf)
- Fly.io: "Volumes are, by default, created with encryption-at-rest enabled"; Fly takes "daily block-level snapshots of volumes", kept five days by default (1 to 60 configurable); "Each volume exists on one server in a single region. It is not network storage."; "Always provision at least two volumes per app."; snapshots should not be the primary backup. [Official vendor docs] [Fly volumes](https://docs.fly.io/volumes/overview)
- Anthropic's DPA lists AES-256 at rest and TLS 1.2+ in transit and a 48-hour breach notice to the customer. [Official vendor docs] [Anthropic DPA](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/data-processing-addendum)
- Breach clocks read in this research: Strava 24 hours ([SP](https://www.strava.com/legal/api_policy) 8.3); GDPR 72 hours to the authority ([Art. 33](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-33-gdpr/)); FTC Health Breach Notification Rule 60 calendar days to individuals and the FTC ([HBNR guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-ftcs-health-breach-notification-rule-0)); state laws in every state ([FTC breach guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/data-breach-response-guide-business)). The DPLA requires notifying users of a breach "in accordance with applicable law" (quoted in review_guidelines.md).
- Account deletion: "Offer to delete the entire account record, along with associated personal data."; "only offering to temporarily deactivate or disable an account is insufficient"; "If you're legally required to retain some user information, let people know what information will and won't be retained."; apps not in highly regulated industries "should not require people to make a phone call, send an email, use a chat support flow"; "If the deletion request will take additional time to complete, let them know." [Apple guidance] [Offering account deletion](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/)
- Repo: scrypt with random salt and `timingSafeEqual` in apps/api/src/auth.ts; fly.toml has a `[[mounts]]` volume; one SQLite database (better-sqlite3); Strava tokens in plain columns. [Repo]

### Inferences
- Suggested retention table for the policy (developer to confirm each value against the code):

| Data | Kept | Deleted when |
|---|---|---|
| Account, plan, habits, goals, journal, learned | While the account exists | Account deletion, at once |
| Apple Health samples on the server | While Health sync is on (consider a rolling window) | Turning sync off with "delete synced data", or account deletion |
| Strava activities | No more than 7 days after fetch | Disconnect, Strava-side revoke, or account deletion, at once |
| Strava tokens | While connected | Disconnect or account deletion |
| Assistant conversations and uploaded files | While the account exists, or until the user deletes a chat | Chat delete or account deletion |
| Copies at Anthropic | Up to 30 days (longer only if flagged or required by law) | Automatic |
| Server backups (Fly snapshots) | 5 days by default | Automatic roll-off |
| Server logs | Short fixed period (set one, for example 14 or 30 days) | Automatic |

- Breach runbook, one page: who decides (the developer); first hour (rotate secrets, revoke sessions, snapshot evidence); clocks (Strava 24 h if Strava data or token; GDPR 72 h if EU or UK users; state laws per residence; FTC rule 60 days); user notice template (what happened, what data, what we did, what you can do, contact); where to file (FTC breach form, state attorney general portals, legal@strava.com and developers@strava.com); call a lawyer.
- Single volume on a single host is a durability risk as well as a security one. Losing all user data is not a notifiable breach under the rules read, but it would make deletion and access promises moot. An off-site encrypted backup with a stated retention is worth adding, and its retention then belongs in the table.
- Low-cost hardening that maps to the standards above: encrypt Strava refresh tokens with a key from Fly secrets; make the webhook verify token a secret; keep request bodies and prompts out of logs; rate-limit login and reset; require re-auth before delete and export; enable 2FA on Apple, Fly.io, Anthropic, Strava and the domain registrar.

### Gaps
- No full security review was done; rate limiting, session handling, password reset flow, logging contents and backup setup were not inspected.
- Fly.io's DPA, subprocessor list and data region for app `josh-logbook` were not checked.
- The HHS guidance defining "secured" data for the FTC rule was not read.
- State breach laws differ in triggers, deadlines and attorney general notice; no state-by-state table was built. LAWYER at incident time.

## 13. EU DSA trader declaration and encryption export controls

### Takeaway
Both are short App Store Connect tasks. Every developer must declare trader or non-trader status; a free, unmonetized hobby app can reasonably declare non-trader, and a planned business should declare trader with a non-home address. The export key is already set correctly for an app that uses only OS-provided HTTPS; the only open point is the year-end self-classification report.

### Cited Findings
- Apple quotes the DSA definition of trader: "any natural person, or any legal person irrespective of whether privately or publicly owned, who is acting, including through any person acting in his or her name or on his or her behalf, for purposes relating to his or her trade, business, craft or profession." Factors: revenue from the app ("especially if you're transacting in large volumes"), commercial practices, VAT registration, development in connection with a trade or profession; a hobbyist who "developed your app with no intention of commercializing it" may not be a trader. For non-traders, "consumers in the EU will be informed that consumer rights stemming from applicable consumer protection laws won't apply to contracts between you and them." Individual traders provide an address or P.O. box, phone number and email, which are published on EU product pages. You "still need to declare a trader status." Apple "does not constitute legal advice". [Apple rule relaying law] [DSA trader requirements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements/)
- submission_requirements.md (SUB-06) records Apple's statement that apps without trader status are removed from the EU App Store (since February 17, 2025). [Apple rule, via sibling note]
- Export: "If you distribute your app outside the U.S. or Canada, your app is subject to U.S. export laws, regardless of where your legal entity is based." Set `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` to `NO` "if your app—including any third-party libraries it links against—doesn't use encryption, or if it only uses forms of encryption that are exempt from export compliance documentation requirements." "Typically, the use of encryption that's built into the operating system—for example, when your app makes HTTPS connections using URLSession—is exempt from export documentation upload requirements, whereas the use of proprietary encryption is not." "If your app uses exempt forms of encryption, you might alternatively be required to submit a year-end self-classification report to the U.S. government." [Apple guidance] [Complying with encryption export regulations](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/complying-with-encryption-export-regulations)
- Documentation table: encryption "limited to that within the Apple operating system": "No documentation required in App Store Connect."; "an industry standard algorithm, not provided within the Apple operating system": French encryption declaration, "only required if you're distributing your app on the App Store in France"; proprietary algorithms: CCATS plus the French declaration. [Apple rule] [Export compliance documentation](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/export-compliance-documentation-for-encryption)
- Apple: "it's your responsibility to review the Export Administration Regulation to determine whether your app's use of encryption requires a formal classification ... you're responsible for all liabilities associated with misinterpretation of export regulations or claiming exemption inaccurately." [Apple guidance] [Overview of export compliance](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/overview-of-export-compliance)
- Apple's standard EULA and minimum terms both carry the export and embargo representations for end users. [Apple rule] [Standard EULA](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/dev/stdeula/)
- Repo: Info.plist has `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` set to false. [Repo]

### Inferences
- A US-only launch makes the trader choice low stakes: nothing is published in the US storefront. The declaration is still asked.
- If Logbook will become a paid product, declare trader from the start with an LLC address or P.O. box, so a later change does not expose a home address.
- A US-only (or US and Canada) launch also reduces export exposure, since Apple frames the duty around distribution "outside the U.S. or Canada".
- Web Crypto calls or a bundled JS crypto library for anything beyond TLS would need a fresh look at the key's value.

### Gaps
- The BIS regulations and the annual self-classification rule were not read; whether an HTTPS-only app distributed abroad must file is unresolved here (also flagged as SUB-20). A short check with counsel or the BIS page settles it.
- Apple's exact non-trader notice wording to EU users was not shown on the page.

## 14. Where is a lawyer genuinely needed?

### Takeaway
Most of this list is engineering and plain writing the developer can do. A lawyer earns the fee on six items: entity formation, the Terms of Service, the Washington and multi-state health data position, the Strava combination question if the product depends on it, the minimum age and state age-law decision, and any EU or UK launch.

### Cited Findings
- Apple 5.1.1(ix) says sensitive-data apps "should be submitted by a legal entity". [Apple rule] [G#5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.1)
- Anthropic's Commercial Terms are made with "you or the organization, company, or other entity that you represent" (corrected 2026-11-10: an individual can be the Customer). [Official vendor docs] [ACT](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- Washington violations are enforceable "through private action". [Law or regulator] [WA AG FAQ](https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy)
- Apple on age laws: "consult your legal counsel." Apple on DSA: material "does not constitute legal advice". Apple on export: "you're responsible for all liabilities". [Apple guidance] [Age assurance](https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance); [DSA](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements/); [Export](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/overview-of-export-compliance)
- Strava may "terminate your access ... immediately", may audit, and requires indemnity. [Official vendor docs] [SA](https://www.strava.com/legal/api)

### Inferences
- Needed before launch (one or two sessions with a privacy or startup lawyer):
  1. Form an LLC and move the Apple, Anthropic, Strava, Fly.io and domain accounts to it. Reasons: Apple 5.1.1(ix), address publication (custom EULA, DSA), and personal liability for health data and training advice.
  2. Draft or review the Terms of Service: liability limits, assumption of risk for training, AI and medical disclaimers, governing law, dispute terms, minimum age.
  3. Review the privacy policy and the Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy against Washington and Nevada, confirm whether Connecticut or other states add duties, and confirm the consent flows meet Washington's definition.
  4. Decide 13+ versus 18+ and whether state app store age laws require the Declared Age Range API.
- Needed only if the trigger happens: a security incident (multi-state notice); a wish to make health claims beyond wellness (FDA); EU or UK launch (representatives, DPIA, transfers, AI Act); a paid tier (auto-renewal laws, Strava 5.8, DSA trader); Strava refusing or conditioning the capacity increase.
- Not needing a lawyer: Strava cache, deauthorization and brand fixes; the AI label and disclaimers; consent gating for every Anthropic flow; the retention table; export key; DSA declaration for a US-only launch; Apple Health naming.

### Gaps
- No fee or time estimates were researched.
- No source was found that states how regulators have treated solo developers of small health apps in practice; enforcement examples read (BetterHelp, GoodRx, Premom) involve companies sharing data for advertising, which Logbook does not do.
