## 1. Which version of the guidelines applies, and what changed recently?

### Takeaway
The governing text is the October 16, 2026 revision. The changes since late 2025 that touch Logbook are: third-party AI named in 5.1.2(i) (Nov 13, 2025), 4.1(c) on other developers' brands (Nov 13, 2025), the new 2.5.19 on hidden or dormant features (Oct 16, 2026), and a run of age rating questionnaire changes (Jul 2025, Jul 2026, Sep 2026, Nov 2026).

### Cited Findings
- The guidelines page footer reads "Last Updated: October 16, 2026". Apple rule. [G](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (verified 2026-11-10)
- Oct 16, 2026 changes: "1.2: Clarified app content and app types that are subject to this guideline..."; "2.3.14: New guideline covering creative assets."; "2.5.19: New guideline clarifies long-standing policy on hidden, dormant, or undocumented features, formerly covered in Guideline 2.3.1."; 3.1.1(a) entitlement wording; Section 5 trafficking wording; Before You Submit and After You Submit gained documentation links. Apple guidance. [Apple news Oct 16, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=92th7e5i) (verified 2026-11-10)
- Apple's framing of 2.5.19: it targets "apps misrepresenting what they do during the App Review process and then changing after approval, for example, adding features or functionality that weren't included in the version of the app that was submitted to App Review", with removal from the store and the Developer Program as the penalty. Apple guidance. [Apple news Oct 16, 2026 (second post)](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=vvzx6cul) (verified 2026-11-10)
- Jun 8, 2026 changes: Introduction (kid and teen safety guidance), 1.2 (developer responsibility paragraph), 4.3(a) and 4.3(b) (basis and examples), 4.5.3 (Live Activities may not spam). - Age ratings: new 13+, 16+, 18+ tiers and new required questions (in-app controls, capabilities, medical or wellness topics, violent themes); answers were due by Jan 31, 2026. [Apple news Jul 9, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=tlur8uvi), [Apple news Jun 8, 2026 Time Allowances](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=0d2gpmml) (verified 2026-11-10)
- From Nov 16, 2026 the questionnaire adds Shared Location and Dating and Relationships descriptors; declarations are needed when submitting an update or changing the questionnaire, and for all apps by Jan 12, 2027. Apple guidance. [Apple news Nov 2, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=yxkh08us) (verified 2026-11-10)
- Build toolchain: "Since April 28, 2026 Apps uploaded to App Store Connect must be built with Xcode 26 or later using an SDK for iOS 26..."; "Since September 9, 2026 iOS and iPadOS apps uploaded to App Store Connect must target iOS 13 or later." Apple guidance. [Upcoming Requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/) (verified 2026-11-10: the Upcoming Requirements page lists no newer Xcode or SDK minimum and no future-dated entry)

Apple guidance about a law. [Apple news Jun 3, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=sg176nne) (verified 2026-11-10)

### Inferences
- Any older checklist that predates Nov 13, 2025 will miss the third-party AI wording, which is the single most relevant recent change for an app whose assistant calls Anthropic from the server.
- 2.5.19 makes feature flags, dev galleries and switched-off integrations a review risk if they ship in the binary but are not described to App Review.

### Gaps
- The Upcoming Requirements page lists Xcode 26 as the minimum. Developer news on Nov 3, 2026 mentions Xcode 27.3 betas, so a newer minimum may be announced. I found no announcement of a date for an Xcode 27 minimum.
- What Texas SB 2420 (and similar state laws) require of an individual developer of a non-kids fitness app was not researched here. It is law, not a review guideline.

## 2. Safety (Section 1): what applies to a training, health data and AI coach app?

### Takeaway
1.4.1 is the live one: Logbook must disclose data sources and method behind any health score, must not claim to measure anything with phone sensors, and "should remind users to check with a doctor". The spot check found no such reminder anywhere in the app. 1.5 (contact) and 1.6 (security) apply to every app. 1.2 and 1.3 do not apply as long as nothing is shared between users and the app is not aimed at children.

### Cited Findings
- 1.4 Physical Harm: "If your app behaves in a way that risks physical harm, we may reject it." Apple rule. [G#1.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.4)
- 1.4.1: "Medical apps that could provide inaccurate data or information, or that could be used for diagnosing or treating patients may be reviewed with greater scrutiny." "Apps must clearly disclose data and methodology to support accuracy claims relating to health measurements, and if the level of accuracy or methodology cannot be validated, we will reject your app. For example, apps that claim to take x-rays, measure blood pressure, body temperature, blood glucose levels, or blood oxygen levels using only the sensors on the device are not permitted." "Apps should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions." "If your medical app has received regulatory clearance, please submit a link to that documentation with your app." Apple rule. [G#1.4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.4.1) (verified 2026-11-10)
- Documentation Apple "typically requests" for 1.4.1 (Diagnosing or Treating Patients): "Regulatory clearance (FDA, CE, or CFDA documentation); a supporting test report or peer-reviewed study." Applies to "Medical apps that make diagnostic or treatment claims". Apple guidance. [CR](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)
- 1.4.5: "Apps should not urge customers to participate in activities (like bets, challenges, etc.) or use their devices in a way that risks physical harm to themselves or others." Apple rule. [G#1.4.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.4.5)
- 1.5 Developer Information: "Make sure your app and its Support URL include an easy way to contact you". Apple rule. [G#1.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.5)
- 1.6 Data Security: "Apps should implement appropriate security measures to ensure proper handling of user information ... and prevent its unauthorized use, disclosure, or access by third parties." Apple rule. [G#1.6](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.6)
- 1.2 User-Generated Content covers "Apps that enable users to create, generate, upload, share, or otherwise contribute content ... This includes social media, user-generated content platforms, and AI content creation apps", and requires filtering, reporting, blocking and "Published contact information so users can easily reach you". Apple rule. [G#1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.2)
- Apple's age rating definitions treat User-Generated Content as "the broad distribution of content created by users as a component of the app's intended user experience". Apple guidance. [Age ratings values and definitions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions)
- 1.1.6: "False information and features, including inaccurate device data or trick/joke functionality" are not allowed. Apple rule. [G#1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.1)
- 1.3 Kids Category and 2.3.8: "Use of terms like 'For Kids' and 'For Children' in app metadata is reserved in the App Store for the Kids Category." Apple rule. [G#1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.3), [G#2.3.8](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.8)
- Repo: a search of apps/web/src, apps/api/src and packages for "medical advice", "check with a doctor", "talk to a doctor", "see a doctor" returned no matches.
- Repo: the Health usage string says Logbook reads "sleep, heart rate, HRV, workouts and activity from Apple Health to show your recovery, strain and sleep against your own normal" (apps/web/ios/App/App/Info.plist). So the app presents derived scores (recovery, strain).

### Inferences
- Logbook is a fitness and wellness app, not a medical app, but recovery and strain scores are "health measurements" in the ordinary sense. The safe reading of 1.4.1 is: say where each number comes from (Apple Health, which device recorded it), say how each derived score is calculated, and never present a score as a diagnosis.
- The AI coach is the most likely place for a medical-sounding answer (injury, illness, heart rate anomalies). A standing reminder near the assistant and in the Health screens, plus a system prompt rule to refer medical questions to a doctor, covers the "should remind users" sentence. Suggested copy in the house style: "Logbook is not medical advice. Check with a doctor before you change training for a health reason."
- 1.2 does not bite today because journal entries, goals and AI answers are visible only to the account owner. It would apply the moment any sharing, feed, or public profile is added.

### Gaps
- Apple does not publish what wording or placement of the doctor reminder satisfies 1.4.1. No primary source specifies it.
- I found no Apple statement on whether proprietary "recovery" or "strain" scores trigger the "disclose data and methodology" sentence. Treat as probably applicable.

## 3. Performance (Section 2): completeness, metadata, software requirements

### Takeaway
2.1 is where most rejections happen (Apple: over 40% of unresolved issues). For Logbook that means: a working, pre-filled demo account, a live Fly.io backend, no placeholder or dev screens, complete review notes that list Anthropic and Strava as external services, and a tested iPad experience because the Xcode target is universal. 2.5.2 is satisfied today because the web build is bundled and no live-update plugin is installed. 2.5.19 requires that nothing dormant ships undisclosed.

### Cited Findings
- 2.1(a): "Submissions to App Review ... should be final versions with all necessary metadata and fully functional URLs included; placeholder text, empty websites, and other temporary content should be scrubbed before submission. Make sure your app has been tested on-device for bugs and stability before you submit it, and include demo account info (and turn on your back-end service!) if your app includes a login. If you are unable to provide a demo account due to legal or security obligations, you may include a built-in demo mode in lieu of a demo account with prior approval by Apple... We will reject incomplete app bundles and binaries that crash or exhibit obvious technical problems." Apple rule. [G#2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.1) (verified 2026-11-10)
- Before You Submit: "Provide App Review with full access to your app. If your app includes account-based features, provide either an active demo account or fully-featured demo mode"; "Enable backend services so that they're live and accessible during review."; "Include detailed explanations of non-obvious features ... in the App Review notes". Apple rule. [G](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- "On average, over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1: App Completeness, which covers crashes, placeholder content, incomplete information, and more." Apple guidance. [AR](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/) (verified 2026-11-10)

### Inferences
- The reviewer's device will have little or no Apple Health data and no Strava account. Health and Strava screens must look finished when empty, and the demo account should carry seeded training, habit, goal and journal data on the server so that Today, Plan and Progress show the app "in use". A short screen recording of the Health connect flow on a real device is cheap insurance, per Apple's "be prepared to provide a demo video".
- Because the target is universal, Logbook will be listed for iPad, needs iPad screenshots, and can be opened on an iPad by the reviewer. Either make the iPad layout presentable or change the target to iPhone only. Even iPhone only, Apple says the app can still be downloaded to iPad and run in compatibility mode, so it must not break there.
- 2.5.2 is satisfied by the current build. It would be put at risk by adding an over-the-air web bundle updater or pointing the WebView at a remote URL. If one is ever added, updates must not introduce or change features.
- 2.5.19 plus the Oct 2026 enforcement note means: remove dev routes from production builds, and for every server-side feature flag either ship the feature on for review or leave the code out. List the AI features, Apple Health sync and Strava in the notes so nothing reads as hidden.
- The API host must be reachable over IPv6-only networks (NAT64). Fly.io public hosts normally have IPv6 addresses, but this should be tested rather than assumed.
- The missing entitlements file needs checking: HealthKit access requires the HealthKit capability on the app target. It may be generated or untracked, but if it is absent the Health feature cannot work in a store build.
- Age rating: "exercise recommendations" puts Logbook at Health or Wellness Topics (9+ band). An AI coach that can discuss injuries or illness may count as "Infrequent medical or treatment information" (13+ band). Choosing the higher answer is the honest and safer one.

### Gaps
- Apple does not publish how reviewers test HealthKit features without data. No primary source found.
- Whether the absence of an entitlements file and privacy manifest in the repo is real or an artifact of untracked files could not be confirmed in this pass.
- Apple does not define "hard to replicate"; whether a demo video is required for Health features is a judgment call.

## 4. Business (Section 3): what applies with no purchases today, and what changes with a subscription?

### Takeaway
With no purchases, Section 3 mostly asks Logbook to say so plainly in the review notes and not to unlock anything by codes or outside payments. If a subscription is added, 3.1.1 forces Apple In-App Purchase for any digital feature unlock, 3.1.2 sets the subscription rules, the paywall must carry specific disclosures plus Terms of Use and Privacy Policy links, and the account deletion flow must handle billing.

### Cited Findings
- Section 3 intro: "If your business model isn't obvious, make sure to explain in its metadata and App Review notes." Also: manipulating reviews or rankings can lead to expulsion. Apple rule. [G#3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#3)
- 3.1.1: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, game levels, access to premium content, or unlocking a full version), you must use in-app purchase. Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys, augmented reality markers, QR codes, cryptocurrencies and cryptocurrency wallets, etc." "you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases." Apple rule. [G#3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#3.1.1) (verified 2026-11-10)
- 3.1.1(a): entitlements for external purchase links "are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps"; in other storefronts "apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase" unless an entitlement applies. Apple rule. [G#3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#3.1.1) (verified 2026-11-10)
- 3.1.2(a): "you must provide ongoing value to the customer, and the subscription period must last at least seven days and be available across all of the user's devices." Appropriate examples include "software as a service ('SAAS'); and cloud support". "those offering subscriptions should allow a user to get what they've paid for without performing additional tasks". "If you are changing your existing app to a subscription-based business model, you should not take away the primary functionality existing users have already paid for." Scam or bait-and-switch subscription flows lead to removal. Apple rule. [G#3.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#3.1.2) (verified 2026-11-10)
- 3.1.2(b): no way to "inadvertently subscribe to multiple variations of the same thing". 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price... Ensure you clearly communicate the requirements described in Schedule 2 of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement." Apple rule. [G#3.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#3.1.2)

### Inferences
- An AI coach with real per-user API cost fits "software as a service" under 3.1.2(a), so a subscription is a permitted model later. It would have to be sold through StoreKit; a web checkout could be linked only on the United States storefront without an entitlement.
- A future paywall must not gate reminders themselves (4.10) and must not make a paid feature conditional on granting Apple Health access or AI data consent (5.1.1(ii)).
- Adding a subscription also adds a Terms of Use (EULA) requirement that does not strictly exist today. Writing the terms now avoids a second legal pass later.

## 5. Design (Section 4): copycats, minimum functionality for a web-wrapped app, notifications, login

### Takeaway
4.2 is the structural risk for a Capacitor app: the test is whether the app is "sufficiently different from a mobile browsing experience". Logbook has real mitigations (bundled build, native HealthKit plugin, local notifications, haptics, camera) and should name them in the review notes. 4.8 is satisfied by email and password only. 4.5.4 and 5.1.2(i) together require that reminders are optional and carry no sensitive data. 4.1 means the name, icon and look must be Logbook's own.

### Inferences
- Logbook is not a "repackaged website": there is no public website version that the app merely frames, the bundle is local, and Apple Health sync is a native capability a browser cannot have. - What still gets Capacitor apps flagged is web feel: text selection callouts, rubber-band scrolling of the whole page, tap delay, a blank or browser-style error on no network, layout that ignores safe areas or Dynamic Type. Each of these is worth a line in the page-by-page audit.

The same conduct rules apply by contract, not only by prudence: optional, no sensitive content on the lock screen (no health numbers, no journal text), no marketing, no "come back" or new feature nudges without an explicit opt-in, and not too many. (corrected: DPLA Attachment 1, sections 2.1 to 2.4 and 4, names Local Notifications; the earlier text treated this as an analogy only)

## 6. Privacy (5.1): data collection, third-party AI, Apple Health

### Takeaway
Three things carry most of the weight. (1) 5.1.2(i): before any personal data goes to Anthropic, the app must say what is sent, name who receives it, and get explicit permission; reviewers must be able to see that prompt. (2) HealthKit data may reach a third party only with express consent and only so that the third party can provide a health or fitness service (DPLA 3.3.3(H)); it may never be used for ads or data mining. (3) 5.1.1(ix): apps "that require sensitive user information" should come from a legal entity, which is a real question for an individual developer handling health data.

### Inferences
- Logbook already does the hard part of 5.1.2(i): it names Claude and Anthropic and separates sent from never sent. Three points deserve tightening against the rule's words "explicit permission before doing so":
  1. A consent switch that is on by default is weaker than an affirmative choice. An unchecked switch, or two equal buttons ("Allow" and "Not now"), is the conservative reading of "explicit".
  2. The list shows flows that are not behind the training data switch: a daily journal entry sent for "Remember when", and files and photos handed to the assistant. Journal text is personal data. Every flow to Anthropic needs to sit behind a permission the user gave, and with permission off nothing personal should be sent.
  3. Reviewers must meet the permission step on the normal path with the demo account. Because the demo account is pre-made, the reviewer skips signup, so the consent must also appear on first use of the assistant for an account that has not answered, and the review notes should say where it is.
- Health data to Anthropic is the sharpest edge. DPLA 3.3.3(H) allows disclosure to a third party "only for purposes of enabling the third party to provide health, motion, fitness ... services". The defensible position: Anthropic acts as Logbook's processor to generate the user's own fitness coaching answer, the user gave express consent on a screen that names Anthropic and the data, Anthropic may not train on it, and nothing is used for ads or mining. Sending the minimum (derived summary, not raw samples) strengthens this. This is an interpretation, not something Apple has confirmed.
- 5.1.1(ix) is a real exposure for an individual account. Logbook is not "healthcare" and Apple Health is optional, so the app does not "require" sensitive information. Keeping Health optional, avoiding medical claims, and choosing a fitness framing all reduce the risk. Enrolling as an organisation (for example an LLC) would remove it and would also keep a personal name off the store listing.
- 5.1.1(v) "let people use it without a login" could be raised because onboarding leads to a mandatory account. The justification is that plans, journal and AI live on the server and sync requires an account; say so in the review notes.
- Strava may be read as a "social network" under 5.1.1(v). At minimum Logbook needs an in-app Disconnect that revokes the token and removes Strava data. The sentence about not storing social network tokens off the device conflicts with any server-side sync design; see Gaps.
- "may not store personal health information in iCloud" plus the Before You Submit link to iCloud Backup guidance suggests that any health data cached on the device (WebView storage, files) should be excluded from iCloud Backup or not cached at all.
- The HIG lines about not building screens that "replicate the standard permission screen" or "affect the flow of health data" mean Logbook's own Health consent sheet should explain and then hand off to the system sheet, and per-type sharing changes should point to Settings, while the Anthropic permission (a different thing) stays in-app.
- With no tracking and no ad SDKs, the privacy label should declare no tracking, and the app should not show an App Tracking Transparency prompt.
- Account deletion must also meet Strava's rule to delete Strava data on revocation and should cover server-side Apple Health data, AI conversation history and uploaded files.

### Gaps
- Apple has not published a definition of "third-party AI" or said whether a server-side processor relationship (developer's server calls the model API) differs from in-app SDK sharing. The rejection text quoted by developers suggests it does not differ.
- Apple has not said whether a general purpose LLM provider counts as a party that "provide[s] a health or fitness service to the user" for HealthKit data. No primary source resolves this.
- Whether Apple treats Strava as a "social network" for the token storage sentence in 5.1.1(v) is unknown. I found no Apple statement or rejection report either way.
- Whether a default-on consent switch satisfies "explicit permission" has no primary source answer.
- Apple does not define "sensitive user information" in 5.1.1(ix), and I found no data on how often individual developers of HealthKit fitness apps are rejected under it.
- Anthropic's data retention period for API inputs was not verified in this pass; the privacy policy should cite Anthropic's current published terms.
- GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA, Washington My Health My Data Act and FTC Health Breach Notification Rule obligations were not researched here; they bind through the Section 5 intro ("comply with all legal requirements").

## 7. Intellectual property (5.2) and the Developer Code of Conduct (5.6)

### Takeaway
5.2.2 makes Strava's own terms part of App Review: Logbook must be "specifically permitted" by Strava's API Agreement and Policy, which since June 1, 2026 ban any use of Strava data with an AI application, including "ingestion into a context window". Apple Health must be named and drawn the way Apple says, and ring charts must not resemble Activity rings. Quotes and other third-party content need rights. 5.6 asks for honesty with users and with App Review.

### Inferences
- Logbook's rule that Strava data never reaches the AI matches Strava Policy 5.3 exactly, and should be enforced in code at the point where assistant context is assembled, including derived values (weekly totals computed from Strava activities count as derived data). Policy 5.4's ban on combining Strava data with other customer data and the seven day cache limit reach further than the AI rule and affect how Progress charts and server storage may use Strava activities. This needs a dedicated check; it is outside App Review text but enters through 5.2.2.
- If a reviewer asks for authorization under 5.2.2, the evidence is the registered Strava API application and compliance with the agreement. Screens that look like Strava (orange accents, Strava-like activity cards) would breach Strava Policy 5.2 and weaken that position.
- Any ring visual that shows Apple Health activity (move, exercise, stand, steps) should be visibly different from Apple's three nested rings, or use Apple's HKActivityRingView for those three metrics only.
- UI and metadata should say "Apple Health" (never "HealthKit"), avoid the phrase "Apple Health data" in marketing copy (HIG allows "uses data from the Apple Health app"), and use Apple's own Health icon unmodified and not as a tappable button.
- Naming "Claude" and "Anthropic" in consent copy is nominative and is in fact required by 5.1.2(i) practice. They should not appear in the app name, subtitle, icon or keywords (4.1(c), 2.3.7).
- Quotes: short attributed quotations from named people carry some copyright and publicity risk and App Review can ask for rights. Public domain sources, the user's own words, or original lines are the low risk options.

### Gaps
- Anthropic's brand and trademark usage rules were not fetched in this pass.
- The source and licence of the quote set shown in Journal were not audited.
- Whether Logbook's Strava API application is approved for more than the default athlete capacity, and whether the current server design meets the seven day cache and no-combination rules, was not checked here. The fact-check found the capacity rule itself: Policy 3.3 caps the lowest Standard Tier level at 10 registered Strava users, so a public launch with Strava connect needs the higher level or Strava's approval first, and the published subscription requirement must be read on the Strava developer site (not fetched).
- Licences for fonts, icon sets and any imagery in the bundle were not audited.

## 8. What gets apps rejected: Apple's published reasons and 2025 to 2026 trends

### Takeaway
Apple's own list is led by 2.1 completeness (over 40% of unresolved issues), then broken links, placeholder content, incomplete review information, privacy policy gaps, vague permission strings, inaccurate screenshots, substandard UI, web-clipping apps and wrong submitting entity. In 2025 Apple rejected 2,093,244 of 9,100,620 submissions. The clearest new trend relevant to Logbook is rejection under 5.1.1(i) and 5.1.2(i) for third-party AI data sharing without a visible, specific permission step; 4.2 remains the standing risk for web-wrapped apps.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 totals: 9,100,620 app submissions reviewed; 2,093,244 rejected; rejections by section: Safety 151,159; Performance 1,354,418; Business 283,820; Design 415,532; Legal 495,673; Other 4,145; 387,087 submissions approved after rejection; 166,899 apps removed from the store. Apple guidance. [2025 App Store Transparency Report](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025/) (verified 2026-11-10, every figure)
- Top removal grounds in 2025: DPLA 3.2(f) Fraud 90,608; Guideline 4.0 Design 72,271; DPLA 6.3 Intellectual Property Infringement 1,177; Guideline 4.3.0 Spam 418; Guideline 4.1.0 Copycats 279. Apple guidance. [2025 App Store Transparency Report](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025/) (verified 2026-11-10)
- "On average, over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1: App Completeness". Apple's named common issues: crashes and bugs; broken links; placeholder content; incomplete information; privacy policy issues; unclear data access requests; inaccurate screenshots; substandard user interface; web clippings, content aggregators, or a collection of links; repeated submission of similar apps; copycats; misleading users; not enough lasting value; submitted by incorrect entity. Apple guidance. [AR](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- Review speed: "typically reviewing at least 50% of submissions in less than 24 hours and 90% in less than 48 hours". Apple guidance. [AR](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/) (verified 2026-11-10)
- Apple offers 30 minute one-on-one App Review appointments to discuss guidelines before submitting. Apple guidance. [AR](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- Apple says that in the prior year it "prevented over 150K apps with safety issues from reaching customers and terminated over 193K developer accounts for engaging in fraud and abuse", in the context of bait-and-switch enforcement. Apple guidance. [Apple news Oct 16, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=vvzx6cul)
- AI data sharing rejections in 2026: see the two forum threads in section 6 (Feb and Mar 2026). The template text asks the app to "clearly explain what data is sent, identify who the data is sent to, and ask the user's permission before sharing the data". Practitioner. [Forums 816140](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/816140), [Forums 820209](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/820209)
- Blog guidance (unverified against Apple): a privacy policy line alone does not satisfy 5.1.2(i); the consent step should name the provider; a call to the AI provider before consent leads to rejection. Practitioner. [Stora](https://stora.sh/blog/2026-05-06-apple-ai-consent-rule-5-1-2-i-implementation-guide), [QAwerk](https://qawerk.com/blog/apple-app-store-ai-data-sharing-guidelines/)
- Press coverage of the Nov 13, 2025 change described it as Apple clamping down on apps sharing personal data with third-party AI. Practitioner (press). [TechCrunch, Nov 13, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai)
- Web-wrapped apps: see the Jan 2026 Capacitor rejection under 4.2 in section 5. Practitioner. [Forums 812889](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812889)

### Inferences
- Performance (which contains 2.1 and 2.3) accounts for roughly two thirds of 2025 rejections by section count (1,354,418 of 2,093,244 rejected submissions cited at least one Performance guideline; sections overlap). The cheapest wins for Logbook are therefore mechanical: demo account, live backend, working links, final copy, accurate screenshots, complete notes.
- For a health plus AI plus web-shell app, the three topical risks stack: 5.1.2(i) AI permission, HealthKit disclosure rules, and 4.2. Each is best answered in advance in the review notes with the exact screen where the reviewer can see the evidence.
- A one-on-one App Review appointment before first submission is a low cost way to raise the two questions Apple has not answered in writing (Health summary to an LLM provider, individual developer with health data).

### Gaps
- Apple does not publish rejection counts per individual guideline (only per section), so there is no primary figure for how often 4.2, 5.1.2 or 1.4.1 are cited.
- No primary or well-sourced data was found on rejection rates specific to health and fitness apps or to Capacitor apps. Blog percentages on this were not used.
- No well-sourced 2026 report was found of a rejection specifically for sending HealthKit data to an LLM provider.
- The "Other 4,145" figure is read from a page where footnote markers sit next to numbers; the value is as displayed, but the supplemental data file was not opened to confirm. (Fact-check 2026-11-10: the page HTML shows "4145" and "1177" as plain cell values with no footnote marker attached, so 4,145 and 1,177 are the figures; only "72,271" carries a footnote marker, number 7.)

## Overall gaps (for the report writer)

- No Apple source says whether sending a HealthKit-derived summary to a general purpose LLM provider, with express consent, satisfies DPLA 3.3.3(H)'s "only for purposes of enabling the third party to provide health, motion, fitness ... services". This is the largest open interpretive question.
- No Apple source says whether a default-on switch counts as "explicit permission" under 5.1.2(i).
- No Apple source defines "sensitive user information" for 5.1.1(ix) or says how it is applied to individual developers of fitness apps that read Apple Health.
- No Apple source says whether Strava is a "social network" for the 5.1.1(v) sentence that tokens may not be stored off the device.
- Apple publishes rejection counts by section only; there are no official per-guideline or per-category rejection rates.
- Apple Developer Forums pages could not be fetched directly; forum evidence here is second hand through a summarising fetch. Still true on 2026-11-10.
- Not researched in this note (belongs to other workstreams): privacy law (GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA, Washington My Health My Data Act, FTC Health Breach Notification Rule), state age assurance laws (Texas SB 2420 and others), export compliance, privacy manifest and required-reason API rules, accessibility labels, App Store Connect asset specifications, Schedule 2 of the DPLA.
- Repo observations in this note are a spot check. In particular the absence of an entitlements file, a privacy manifest, a hosted privacy policy URL, a support URL and a doctor reminder should each be confirmed by the page-by-page audit.
