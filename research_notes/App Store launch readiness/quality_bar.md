# Quality, accessibility and UX bar for a featured-quality launch (Logbook, as of 2026-11-10)

Researched 2026-11-10. Scope: what "professional and ready" means on iPhone for Logbook (Capacitor 8, WKWebView, light palette, six tabs, email and password accounts, Apple Health, Strava, AI coach). Binary, signing, privacy label, age rating and policy topics belong to the sibling notes (`ios_technical.md`, `submission_requirements.md`, `review_guidelines.md`); they are referenced here only where the quality bar touches them.

How to read source types. Every item carries one label:

- **Apple rule**: App Review Guidelines, App Store Connect upload or metadata requirements. Breaking it can block review or get the app removed.
- **Apple guidance**: Human Interface Guidelines (HIG), App Store Connect Help, developer documentation, App Store marketing pages. Not enforced line by line. App Review does list "Substandard user interface" as a common issue and tells developers to follow "our design guides", so the HIG is the yardstick when a reviewer or an editor judges polish.
- **W3C standard**: WCAG 2.2 (W3C Recommendation, 5 October 2023, republished 12 December 2024). Not an Apple requirement. Apple cites its contrast numbers. It is not in the list of source types the brief gave, so it is labelled separately.
- **Official vendor docs**: Capacitor, Capawesome, WebKit.
- **Practitioner report**: vendor blogs and benchmark reports. Directional only.
- **[Repo]**: something read in this repository on 2026-11-10. Not a full audit, only enough to judge whether a requirement applies.

Apple pages were read as primary text (the HIG and documentation JSON behind each page, and the Help pages). Quotes are verbatim, except that omissions are marked with an ellipsis and a dash inside a quote is written as a comma or semicolon.

Fact check, 2026-11-10: the most consequential claims below were re-opened at their primary sources. Items that held are marked "(verified 2026-11-10)". Items that changed are marked "(corrected: ...)". Four things changed: the scope of the iPhone Duo screenshot rule (wider than first written), which iPhone screenshot size is required (Apple's page contradicts itself, so upload a large and a medium set), the keyword limit (100 bytes, not 100 characters, in App Store Connect), and the source of one ratings quote. No checked claim was unreachable.

- 2025-12-16: HIG Typography added emphasized weights to the Dynamic Type tables. 2025-03-07: Dynamic Type guidance moved from Accessibility to Typography; VoiceOver got its own page.
- 2026-03-24: HIG Sheets "Updated guidance for button placement" (Cancel leading, Done trailing in a single-view sheet). (verified 2026-11-10)
- 2026-09-09: HIG Layout "Updated guidance to reflect current best practices" (device size tables removed; Device Hub and iPhone Mirroring resize testing added).
- 2026 (iOS 27): the App Store shows "creative assets" (product page header, search result asset) on iOS 27 and later; guideline 2.3.14 covers them. (verified 2026-11-10)
- 2026: iPhone Duo (folding iPhone) is on the iOS 27 device list (verified 2026-11-10). Apps built with the iOS 27 SDK resize when it folds. "Starting in April 2027, screenshots will be required for any app using the iOS 27.1 SDK or later." (corrected: that sentence is on the screenshot specifications page, but Apple's news post of October 5, 2026, https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=kkphp5qo , has no SDK condition: "Starting April 2027, any apps or games submitted will need to include screenshots for iPhone Duo." Plan for the wider reading: every submission from April 2027 needs iPhone Duo screenshots, whatever SDK it was built with.)
- iOS usage as measured by Apple on 2026-06-07: iOS 26 on 79 percent of all iPhones, iOS 18 on 14 percent, earlier 7 percent. (verified 2026-11-10)

## 1. What does the HIG ask for at launch, item by item?

### Takeaway
The HIG is guidance, not a rule set, but it is the yardstick App Review points to for "Substandard user interface" and the one editors use for "UI design". For a web-view app the weight falls on things UIKit gives native apps for free: safe areas, 44 pt targets, text scaling, press states, sheet behaviour, keyboards and system-matching appearance.

### Cited Findings
Layout and safe areas
- "Respecting the safe area is essential to make sure system UI and hardware features like the Dynamic Island don't obstruct content and controls." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- "Design a layout that adapts gracefully and consistently ... respecting system-defined safe areas, margins, and guides". "Even if your app is locked to a certain orientation ... it's still important to ensure your interface resizes well". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- "Be prepared for text-size changes ... Apps that don't respond to this setting can be difficult or impossible to use for people who rely on this feature." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- "Preview your app on multiple devices, using different size classes, localizations, and text sizes. You can streamline the testing process by first testing versions of your experience that use the largest and the smallest layouts." It names Device Hub and iPhone Mirroring resize. Page change log: "September 9, 2026: Updated guidance to reflect current best practices." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- "Apps built with the iOS 27 SDK and later resize dynamically on iPhone Duo ... Hardcoded screen sizes, fixed orientations, and device assumptions can break layouts, leading to stretched, clipped, or misplaced content." Apps built with the iOS 26 SDK "appear in the center of the display, with empty space surrounding the window". [Apple guidance] — [Three steps for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/iphone-duo/prepare/)

Touch targets
- "As a general rule, a button needs a hit region of at least 44x44 pt". "Always include a press state for a custom button. Without a press state, a button can feel unresponsive". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)
- Control size table for iOS: default 44x44 pt, minimum 28x28 pt. "it works well to add about 12 points of padding around elements that include a bezel. For elements without a bezel, about 24 points of padding works well". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

Typography and Dynamic Type
- iOS default text size 17 pt, minimum 11 pt. "In general, avoid light font weights." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- Dynamic Type table: body is 14 pt at xSmall, 17 pt at Large (default), 23 pt at xxxLarge, 53 pt at AX5. Caption 2 is 11 pt at default. [Apple guidance] — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- "Make sure your app's layout adapts to all font sizes." "Keep text truncation to a minimum as font size increases." "Consider adjusting your layout at large font sizes ... consider using a stacked layout". "Increase the size of meaningful interface icons as font size increases." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- "Prioritize important content when responding to text-size changes ... when people increase text size to read the content in a tabbed window, they don't expect the tab titles to increase in size." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- "Ideally, give people the option to enlarge text by at least 200 percent". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

Colour and contrast
- Contrast table used by Accessibility Inspector: up to 17 pt 4.5:1; 18 pt 3:1; bold 3:1. "If your app doesn't provide this minimum contrast by default, ensure it at least provides a higher contrast color scheme when the system setting Increase Contrast is turned on." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- "Avoid relying solely on color to differentiate between objects, indicate interactivity, or communicate essential information." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)
- The Dark Mode page asks for more than the minimum in custom palettes: "make sure the contrast ratio between colors is no lower than 4.5:1. For custom foreground and background colors, strive for a contrast ratio of 7:1, especially in small text." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)

Motion and Reduce Motion
- "Add motion purposefully ... Gratuitous or excessive animation can distract people and may make them feel disconnected or physically uncomfortable." "Make motion optional." "In apps, generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently." "Let people cancel motion ... don't make people wait for an animation to complete before they can do anything". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- With Reduce Motion on, "ensure your app or game responds by reducing automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion." Listed practices: tighten springs, track gestures directly, avoid z-axis depth changes, replace x, y and z transitions with fades, avoid animating into and out of blurs. [Apple guidance] — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

Haptics
- "Use system-provided haptic patterns according to their documented meanings." "Use haptics consistently". "Avoid overusing haptics." "Make haptics optional. Let people turn off or mute haptics". Notification haptics are for "the outcome of a task or action"; impact haptics "complement a visual experience", for example "a view snaps into place"; selection haptics are for "while the values of a UI element are changing". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)
- Capacitor's Haptics plugin exposes impact, notification and selection calls; "On devices that don't have Taptic Engine or Vibrator, the API calls will resolve without performing any action." [Official vendor docs] — [Capacitor Haptics](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/haptics)

Loading, empty states, errors
- "Show something as soon as possible. If you make people wait for loading to complete before displaying anything, they can interpret the lack of content as a problem". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Loading](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading)
- "When possible, use a determinate progress indicator." "Keep progress indicators moving". "Avoid vague terms like loading or authenticating because they seldom add value." "When it's feasible, let people halt processing." "Perform automatic content updates ... Don't make people responsible for initiating every update." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Progress indicators](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/progress-indicators)
- "Provide clear next steps on any blank screens ... guide people on actions they can take, and give them a button or link to do so if possible." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing)
- "Write clear error messages ... display it as close to the problem as possible, avoid blame, and be clear about what someone can do to fix it ... Interjections like 'oops!' or 'uh-oh' are typically unnecessary and can sound insincere." Also: "Avoid using we altogether ... 'Unable to load content' is much clearer." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing)
- "Avoid showing an alert when your app starts ... If your app detects a problem at startup, like no network connection ... show cached or placeholder data and a nonintrusive label that describes the problem." "Avoid using an alert merely to provide information." "Avoid displaying alerts for common, undoable actions, even when they're destructive." "If there's a destructive action, include a Cancel button". "Avoid using OK as the default button title unless the alert is purely informational." Titles should not be "Error" or an error number. [Apple guidance] — [HIG Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts)
- "Show people when a command can't be carried out and help them understand why." "Warn people when they initiate a task that can cause data loss that's unexpected and irreversible." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback)

Modality and sheets
- "Always give people an obvious way to dismiss a modal view ... in iOS ... people typically expect to find a button in the top toolbar or swipe down". "help people avoid data loss by getting confirmation before closing a modal view". "Make it easy to identify a modal view's task." "Let people dismiss a modal view before presenting another one." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Modality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality)
- "for sheets with a single view, the Cancel button belongs on the leading edge of the top toolbar. When present, the Done button belongs on the trailing edge." "Display only one sheet at a time". "If you provide a Done button, always pair it with a Cancel button ... or a Back button". "Include a grabber in a resizable sheet." "Support swiping to dismiss a sheet ... If people have unsaved changes ... use an action sheet to let them confirm". Change log: "March 24, 2026: Updated guidance for button placement." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)

Navigation, tab bar, back
- "Use a tab bar to support navigation, not to provide actions." "Make sure the tab bar is visible when people navigate to different sections". "Don't disable or hide tab bar buttons, even when their content is unavailable ... If a section is empty, explain why". "Include tab labels". "it's generally easier to navigate among fewer tabs". "Avoid overflow tabs." On iOS, "A tab bar floats above content at the bottom of the screen." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)
- "Use the standard Back and Close buttons ... Prefer the standard symbols for each, and don't use a text label that says Back or Close. If you create a custom version of either, make sure it still looks the same, behaves as people expect". "Write a concise title ... keep the title under 15 characters long". "Don't title windows with your app name." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)

Text fields and keyboards
- "Show a hint in a text field ... Because placeholder text disappears when people start typing, it can also be useful to include a separate label". "Always use a secure text field when your app asks for sensitive data". "Validate fields when it makes sense ... when entering an email address, it's best to validate when people switch to another field; when creating a user name or password, validation needs to happen before people switch to another field." "show the appropriate keyboard type". On iOS, "Display a Clear button in the trailing end of a text field". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Text fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-fields)
- "Choose a keyboard that matches the type of content people are editing." "Consider customizing the Return key type". "Use the keyboard layout guide to make the keyboard feel like an integrated part of your interface ... keep important parts of your interface visible while the virtual keyboard is onscreen." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Virtual keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards)
- "Never prepopulate a password field." "When possible, offer choices instead of requiring text entry." "Dynamically validate field values." "if you include a Next or Continue button after a set of text fields, make the button available only after people enter the data you require." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Entering data](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/entering-data)

Launch and onboarding
- "Launch instantly." "Design a launch screen that's nearly identical to the first screen ... make sure that your launch screen matches the device's current orientation and appearance mode." "Avoid including text on your launch screen". "Don't advertise. The launch screen isn't a branding opportunity." "Restore the previous state when your app restarts". "If your interface only runs in one orientation, launch in that orientation". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching)
- "design a flow that's fast, fun, and optional." "Teach through interactivity." "If you let people skip the tutorial ... make sure it's easy for people to find if they want to view it later." "Postpone nonessential setup flows". "Avoid displaying licensing details within your onboarding flow." "Prefer letting people experience your app or game before prompting them for ratings or purchases." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- "Request permission only when your app clearly needs access ... Ideally, wait to request permission until people actually use an app feature that requires access." For a custom screen before a system alert: "Include only one button and make it clear that it opens the system alert ... Use a term like 'Continue' or 'Next'"; "don't provide a way for people to leave the screen or window without viewing the system alert". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- "Delay sign-in for as long as possible. People often abandon apps when they're forced to sign in before they can do anything useful." "If you don't use Sign in with Apple ... prefer using a passkey." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- App Review lists "Substandard user interface" among common issues: "Apple places a high value on clean, refined, and user-friendly interfaces. Make sure your UI meets these requirements by planning your design carefully and following our design guides." [Apple guidance] — [App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- The App Review Guidelines text contains no mention of Dark Mode or Dynamic Type (searched 2026-11-10). [Apple rule, by absence] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- [Repo] The floating tab capsule matches the iOS 26 and 27 pattern ("A tab bar floats above content"). Six tabs is more than most iPhone apps carry. The current HIG gives no number for iPhone, only "fewer tabs" and a "five or fewer" default for customisable iPad tab bars. On the narrowest current phones six tabs still fit 44 pt targets but leave little room for labels, less still at larger text sizes. This is the one structural navigation question worth deciding before launch (for example fold Learned into Journal, or Progress into Plan), because changing tabs after launch is costly.
- The web view gets none of the native back behaviour for free. People expect an edge swipe to go back on pushed screens; the HIG says custom back controls must behave "as people expect". I found no HIG sentence that names the swipe itself (see Gaps).
- The pre-permission guidance lists camera, microphone, location, contacts, calendar and tracking. Notifications are not in that list, so a "Not now" on the nudge step is defensible, but the one-button pattern is the safer reading.
- "Delay sign-in" conflicts with Logbook's flow (value pager, privacy page, then sign up before any use). It is guidance, and an account is needed to sync, so it is acceptable; the value pager and a short path to first value are the mitigation. It affects the featuring factor "User experience" more than review.
- Apple's own text for error and empty-state copy matches the project's style rule (plain, short). Em dashes are not mentioned by Apple; that is a house rule.

### Gaps
- The HIG no longer publishes iPhone point dimensions (removed in the 2026-09-09 Layout update). Point widths for specific phones are not cited here; use Device Hub.
- No HIG sentence found that requires an edge-swipe back gesture. Treat it as user expectation, unsourced.
- No Apple statement found on the right number of tabs on iPhone in the current HIG.
- Whether WKWebView reflects the Bold Text, Button Shapes and Increase Contrast settings into CSS (`prefers-contrast`) was not verified against a WebKit source.

## 2. Is a light-only app acceptable, and how is it declared?

### Takeaway
Yes for review, no for best practice. No guideline requires Dark Mode. Apple's documentation calls the opt-out temporary and "strongly" encourages support, the HIG says people expect apps to follow the system, and a light-only app cannot claim the Dark Interface label. Declare it with `UIUserInterfaceStyle = Light`. Logbook currently declares `Dark`, which is wrong for its light UI.

### Cited Findings
- "The system automatically opts in any app linked against the iOS 13.0 or later SDK to both light and dark appearances. If you need extra time to work on your app's Dark Mode support, you can temporarily opt out by including the UIUserInterfaceStyle key (with a value of Light) in your app's Info.plist file. Setting this key to Light causes the system to ignore the user's preference and always apply a light appearance to your app." "Important: Supporting Dark Mode is strongly encouraged. Use the UIUserInterfaceStyle key to opt out only temporarily". [Apple guidance] — [Choosing a specific interface style](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/choosing-a-specific-interface-style-for-your-ios-app)
- "people often choose Dark Mode as their default interface style, and they generally expect all apps and games to respect their preference." "Avoid offering an app-specific appearance setting." "In rare cases, consider using only a dark appearance". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)
- Dark Interface label: "You may indicate support if your app is dark by default for all common tasks ... If your app isn't dark by default ... you may still indicate support if it allows the user to enable a mode or setting that keeps the interface dark. This can include supporting the systemwide Dark Mode appearance or using a custom dark color scheme, or as a last resort, supporting the systemwide Smart Invert accessibility setting." "Even temporary flashes of bright content may cause discomfort". [Apple guidance] — [Dark Interface criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/dark-interface-evaluation-criteria)
- Product page advice is conditional: "If your app supports Dark Mode, consider including at least one screenshot that showcases what the experience looks like". [Apple guidance] — [Product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- The App Review Guidelines do not mention Dark Mode. [Apple rule, by absence] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- [Repo] `apps/web/ios/App/App/Info.plist` has `UIUserInterfaceStyle` = `Dark`. `src/styles/tokens.css` sets `color-scheme: light`. `capacitor.config.ts` uses background `#f3f8fb` and `StatusBar.style: 'LIGHT'`. `src/features/todos/todos.css` sets `color-scheme: dark` on `.tsheet-date`.

### Inferences
- With `Dark` in Info.plist, every system surface the app raises (keyboard, alerts, share sheet, the notification and Health permission sheets, date pickers) is dark over a pale blue app. That reads as unfinished and is the cheapest high-visibility fix in this file: change one value to `Light`, then recheck the status bar text colour and the date field.
- A dark theme is the biggest single gap against "featured quality": it touches the featuring factors UI design and accessibility, and the Dark Interface label. It is not needed to pass review.
- Smart Invert is named by Apple as a last resort. It would need images and charts excluded from inversion, which a web view cannot mark per element in a way I could source. Do not rely on it.

### Gaps
- No Apple statement found on whether editors exclude light-only apps from featuring.
- How `StatusBar.style: 'LIGHT'` in Capacitor 8 maps to text colour (light text or light background) was not rechecked here; verify on device after the plist change.

## 3. Accessibility Nutrition Labels: what must work to claim each one?

### Takeaway
A label may be declared only if people can complete all common tasks with that feature: the app's primary functions plus first launch, login, purchase (if any) and settings. Declaring is voluntary today, will become required at an unstated date, and must be accurate under guideline 2.3. For Logbook the realistic first set, after fixes and device testing, is Sufficient Contrast, Differentiate Without Color Alone, Reduced Motion and Larger Text; VoiceOver and Voice Control need the most work; Dark Interface is out; Captions and Audio Descriptions do not apply in the usual sense.

### Cited Findings
General rules
- "providing these labels will be voluntary to start ... over time, you'll be required to share accessibility support details to submit new apps and app updates". Labels "appear on Apple devices running iOS 26 ... or later". "If you don't provide this information for a device, the section will still appear on your product page and show that you haven't indicated support yet." [Apple guidance] — [Overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)
- "To indicate support for an accessibility feature ... users must be able to complete all of the common tasks of your app using that feature." "Common tasks consist of the primary functionality that you expect users to perform in your app, plus functionality that's fundamental to using an app in general: first launch experience, login, purchase, and settings." [Apple guidance] — [Overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)
- Test for "common": "ask yourself whether you'd ship an urgent fix if users were blocked from completing this task." Include "default and loading screens, such as the screen to view the task list when the user hasn't added tasks yet" and "actions a user can take outside your app ... such as marking a task complete in a notification". [Apple guidance] — [Overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)
- "make sure to follow App Review Guideline 2.3 for accurate metadata. If an app potentially has intentionally misleading or harmful accessibility labels, App Review has the ability to contact the developer". "If bugs are significant enough to change your answer ... then don't indicate support". [Apple rule for accuracy] — [Overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)
- Search effect: "in searches like 'VoiceOver note taking apps' or 'running apps with Larger Text,' apps that have indicated support for those features in their labels will be considered more relevant". [Apple guidance] — [Overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)
- Implementation tips: "Don't attempt to make a custom implementation of the functionality provided by core assistive technologies like VoiceOver or Voice Control." Other features may be custom, "such as a custom dark color scheme". "In apps that use a web view to load third-party websites, WebKit provides default support for most accessibility labels for web content by using web standards like HTML, CSS, and ARIA." [Apple guidance] — [Overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)
- Where to declare: App Store Connect, the app, "App Accessibility" in the sidebar; answers are saved as a draft, then published; "You can only publish support for devices that have a live version on the App Store"; an optional accessibility URL can be added and "will automatically go live with the first release". [Apple guidance] — [Manage labels](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/manage-accessibility-nutrition-labels)

VoiceOver
- "You may indicate your app supports VoiceOver if users are able to navigate and interact with visual elements ... using only VoiceOver ... without sighted assistance." Criteria: concise accurate labels on all controls; labels make sense out of context; no control type or state inside the label text; images described or hidden if decorative; "Charts and other data visualizations should include accessibility information ... or at minimum, include a reasonably complete text alternative"; element type and state spoken; all visible text spoken; "Temporary status banners and important in-app alerts should be conveyed to VoiceOver users"; logical, complete order with no loops; reading position not reset when content refreshes; "VoiceOver users shouldn't be able to move into invisible, offscreen, or background content"; modals dismissable "using the Escape key or an accessibilityPerformEscape action"; context menus, long presses, drag and drop offered "as a custom action"; "Custom elements ... should provide equivalent accessibility of native elements". [Apple guidance] — [VoiceOver criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/voiceover-evaluation-criteria)

Voice Control
- "You may say your app supports Voice Control if users are able to navigate and interact with your app using only voice commands ... without touching the display." Checks: "Show numbers" and "Show names" mark all interactive elements; "Match Voice Control labels to the visible text"; swipes, long presses and hidden UI have a speech-only route; scrolling works; "users should be able to dictate and edit text in any text field". [Apple guidance] — [Voice Control criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/voice-control-evaluation-criteria)

Larger Text
- "You can indicate that your app supports Larger Text if users can enlarge text to at least 200% or the maximum font size for the system ... Body text in the primary iPhone ... views should increase in size without affecting readability through overlapping layouts or severe truncation." "Dynamic Type on iOS ... reaches sizes larger than 200% at the AX3 size, and allows body text sizes over 300% at the AX5 size." "Don't rely on system-provided assistive technology like Zoom or Hover Text to claim support". An in-app size control is allowed, but "we recommend detecting when a user has Apple system settings enabled". "If your app uses a web view as part of its common tasks, provide a font size controller or let Safari handle this task for your users." "prioritize scaling the main content areas before repeated, predictable navigational elements"; "some features, like Tab Bars, don't grow with dynamic text ... Consider other ways to make those elements accessible, such as the Large Content Viewer." [Apple guidance] — [Larger Text criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/larger-text-evaluation-criteria)
- WKWebView does not scale ordinary web text with the system setting; a WebKit engineer's answer on the bug: "you can either adjust the web content to use the legacy vendor-prefixed CSS keywords (like -apple-system-body) or implement your own font size controller similar to what iOS Safari has done". [Official vendor docs] — [WebKit bug 187013](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=187013)
- Capacitor also ships a plugin for this: "The Text Zoom API provides the ability to change Web View text size for visual accessibility", with `getPreferred()` returning the preferred zoom level. [Official vendor docs] — [Capacitor Text Zoom](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/text-zoom)
- "Don't use the large content viewer as a replacement for proper Dynamic Type support ... Rely on the large content viewer only in situations where items must remain small due to unavoidable design constraints. For example, buttons in a tab bar". [Apple guidance] — [UILargeContentViewerInteraction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uilargecontentviewerinteraction)

Dark Interface
- See section 2. Not claimable for a light-only app. [Apple guidance] — [Dark Interface criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/dark-interface-evaluation-criteria)

Differentiate Without Color Alone
- "You may indicate your app supports Differentiate Without Color Alone if the common tasks of your app don't rely on color as the only way to convey information ... If your app does use color as the only differentiator (e.g. color-coded charts), make sure that users can customize the design". Test: "try testing using the Grayscale color filter ... If you can't use your own app in grayscale, rethink your app's design". For charts, "consider arranging the chart colors in the same order as the legend keys" or label elements directly. [Apple guidance] — [Differentiate criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/differentiate-without-color-alone-evaluation-criteria)

Sufficient Contrast
- "You may indicate your app supports Sufficient Contrast if the user interface for your common tasks, including text, buttons, and other controls, meets general contrast guidelines by default, usually 4.5 to 1 for most text elements." If not by default, an app on custom frameworks should "provide users with a choice of multiple color schemes that meet contrast minimums". "Meeting a 3:1 minimum contrast ratio is commonly recommended for non-text contrast." Test with Bold Text, Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency on: "Enable all three settings before testing". [Apple guidance] — [Sufficient Contrast criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/sufficient-contrast-evaluation-criteria)

Reduced Motion
- "If your app doesn't contain any problematic motion triggers, you may indicate that it supports Reduced Motion." Triggers: "depth simulation (including parallax effects, animated blur, and depth-of-field effects)", "multi-axis motion, multi-speed motion, spinning, or vortex effects", "auto-advancing carousel screens or any other ongoing motion". "don't remove or modify all animations prematurely"; where motion carries meaning, provide "a dissolve, highlight fade, or color shift". A custom setting is allowed, but "we recommend detecting when a user has the Apple system setting enabled". The page lists the WebKit article "Responsive Design for Motion" under Related. [Apple guidance] — [Reduced Motion criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/reduced-motion-evaluation-criteria)

Captions and Audio Descriptions
- "You may say that your app supports Captions if it provides captions for video played through your app ... You may also indicate support for Captions if your app provides text transcripts of audio-only content". "If your app includes the functionality to display captions, but very little or no captioned content is available on your app, don't indicate support for Captions." [Apple guidance] — [Captions criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/captions-evaluation-criteria)
- "An accessibility feature doesn't apply to your app. Review the evaluation criteria for the accessibility feature to determine if it's applicable and whether to indicate support." [Apple guidance] — [Overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)

Apple's own testing method
- "compile a list of the main tasks that a person can perform on each screen", then build a matrix of devices, settings and assistive technologies; "turn on each of the following accessibility features ... one at a time". Visual list: Bold Text, Larger Accessibility Sizes, Button Shapes, On/Off Labels, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast, Differentiate Without Color, Color Filters, Reduce Motion, Dim Flashing Lights. Assistive technologies: VoiceOver, Voice Control, Switch Control. [Apple guidance] — [Performing accessibility testing](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/performing-accessibility-testing-for-your-app)
- Capacitor's Screen Reader plugin only reports state and speaks text: `isEnabled()`, `speak()`. It does not make web content accessible. [Official vendor docs] — [Capacitor Screen Reader](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/screen-reader)

### Inferences
- Logbook's common tasks (proposed rows for the matrix): (1) first launch: value pager, privacy page; (2) sign up, log in, password reset; (3) onboarding steps including the notification nudge and plan build; (4) Today: read the training card, check a habit, add, complete and undo a to-do, move an item on the timeline; (5) Plan: read the week, open a session, mark it done, move a session; (6) Goals: create, edit, complete, set a reminder; (7) Learned: add and read; (8) Progress: read each chart; (9) Journal: write, edit, read, pick a date; (10) Assistant: open from the Ask bar, ask, read the streamed answer, stop, attach a photo; (11) Settings: profile, AI consent switch, connect and disconnect Apple Health and Strava, haptics, sign out, delete account; (12) outside the app: act on a goal reminder notification.
- Because Logbook's own UI is all web content, "let Safari handle this task" does not apply; the app must scale text itself. [Repo] `dynamicType.ts` already follows the WebKit advice by reading `-apple-system-body`. The open work is layout at 200 to 310 percent and the fixed-px rules.
- The Larger Text criteria let chrome stay small if content scales. A ceiling on the tab capsule and bar buttons (while body text keeps scaling) is consistent with Apple's text, but there is no web equivalent of the Large Content Viewer; a long-press label bubble would be a custom stand-in and is my suggestion, not Apple's.
- Captions: Logbook plays no video or audio, so there is nothing to caption. The safe reading is to leave Captions and Audio Descriptions undeclared.
- VoiceOver in a web view depends on real semantics. The likely failures in this codebase are custom pressables without a button role, sheets whose background stays reachable, hold menus with no alternative, the draggable timeline, charts drawn as SVG without text, and streamed AI text that is either not announced or announced word by word.

### Gaps
- No date for when labels become mandatory. Apple says only "over time".
- No Apple statement on whether an app with no media may declare Captions. Left undeclared by inference.
- I did not find Apple guidance specific to hybrid apps for VoiceOver focus management in a WKWebView (for example how the two-finger scrub maps to web dialogs). Needs device testing.
- Not verified from a WebKit primary source in this pass: that `prefers-reduced-motion` in WKWebView follows the iOS Reduce Motion switch live. Apple's criteria page links the WebKit article, which suggests it does. Verify on device.

## 4. Which WCAG 2.2 AA items do web-view apps fail most?

### Takeaway
WCAG is not an Apple requirement, but Apple's contrast numbers come from it and W3C has draft guidance for applying it to hybrid apps. There is no dataset of failures in web-view apps specifically. The best proxy (WebAIM's yearly scan of a million web home pages) shows the same six failures every year, led by low contrast. For Logbook the WCAG items with real exposure are contrast, names and roles on custom controls, dragging alternatives, text resize, status messages and the orientation lock.

### Cited Findings
- WCAG 2.2 is a W3C Recommendation (5 October 2023; current edition dated 12 December 2024). "Content that conforms to WCAG 2.2 also conforms to WCAG 2.0 and WCAG 2.1." [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- W3C's "Guidance on Applying WCAG 2.2 to Mobile Applications (WCAG2Mobile)" is a Group Draft Note dated 6 May 2025. It covers "native mobile apps, mobile web apps and hybrid apps using web components inside native mobile apps" and "provides informative guidance (guidance that is not normative and does not set requirements)". [W3C standard, draft] — [WCAG2Mobile](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2mobile-22/)
- 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), AA: "contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1", large text 3:1. [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum)
- 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, AA: user interface components and graphical objects "have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against adjacent color(s)". [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast)
- 1.4.1 Use of Color, A: "Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response, or distinguishing a visual element." [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#use-of-color)
- 1.4.4 Resize Text, AA: "text can be resized without assistive technology up to 200 percent without loss of content or functionality." [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#resize-text)
- 1.4.10 Reflow, AA: no two-dimensional scrolling for "Vertical scrolling content at a width equivalent to 320 CSS pixels". [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#reflow)
- 1.3.4 Orientation, AA: "Content does not restrict its view and operation to a single display orientation, such as portrait or landscape, unless a specific display orientation is essential." [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#orientation)
- 1.3.5 Identify Input Purpose, AA: the purpose of fields collecting information about the user "can be programmatically determined". [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#identify-input-purpose)
- 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), AA, new in 2.2: "The size of the target for pointer inputs is at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels", with spacing and other exceptions. [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum)
- 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, AA, new in 2.2: "All functionality that uses a dragging movement for operation can be achieved by a single pointer without dragging, unless dragging is essential". [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dragging-movements)
- 2.5.1 Pointer Gestures, A: path-based or multipoint gestures need a single-pointer alternative. 2.5.3 Label in Name, A: "the name contains the text that is presented visually." [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#pointer-gestures)
- 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), AA, new in 2.2: a focused component "is not entirely hidden due to author-created content." [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum)
- 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable, A: time limits set by the content can be turned off, adjusted or extended. [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#timing-adjustable)
- 3.3.1 Error Identification, A: "the item that is in error is identified and the error is described to the user in text." [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#error-identification)
- 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum), AA, new in 2.2: no cognitive function test such as remembering a password unless there is an alternative or "A mechanism is available to assist the user" (password managers and paste are the usual mechanism). [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#accessible-authentication-minimum)
- 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value, A: for all components "the name and role can be programmatically determined; states, properties, and values ... can be programmatically set". "This success criterion is primarily for web authors who develop or script their own user interface components." [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#name-role-value)
- 4.1.3 Status Messages, AA: status messages "can be programmatically determined through role or properties such that they can be presented to the user by assistive technologies without receiving focus." [W3C standard] — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#status-messages)
- WebAIM Million, February 2026 (top one million home pages, automated detection): 95.9 percent of pages had detected failures. Most common: low contrast text 83.9 percent, missing image alternative text 53.1 percent, missing form input labels 51 percent, empty links 46.3 percent, empty buttons 30.6 percent, missing document language 13.5 percent. "96% of all errors detected fall into these six categories. These most common errors have been the same for the last 7 years." [Practitioner report] — [WebAIM Million 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)
- Apple's HIG uses the same thresholds: "Accessibility Inspector uses the following values from WCAG Level AA as guidance". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Apple on time-boxed UI: "Minimize use of time-boxed interface elements. Views and controls that auto-dismiss on a timer can be problematic". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

### Inferences
- Logbook exposure, in rough order of risk: contrast on a pale palette (1.4.3, 1.4.11); custom controls (4.1.2); drag-only actions on the timeline, plan and to-dos (2.5.7); long-press menus and the swipe pager (2.5.1); text resize and reflow at 200 percent on a narrow phone (1.4.4, 1.4.10); toasts and streamed text (4.1.3, 2.2.1); floating bars covering focus (2.4.11); portrait lock (1.3.4).
- Already in decent shape from what the repo shows: document language, autocomplete tokens on auth fields, a 44 px hit token (well above 24 CSS px), live regions in use.
- Apple's 44 pt target is stricter than WCAG's 24 CSS px. Meeting Apple's number satisfies 2.5.8.
- WebAIM measures public home pages, not app UIs. It is a proxy for "what web developers usually get wrong", and its top five map directly to things a React codebase can lint for.

### Gaps
- No published study of WCAG failures in hybrid or web-view iOS apps was found. The ranking above is inference from a web proxy plus Logbook's code.
- Legal accessibility duties (for example the European Accessibility Act, or US law for a private individual developer of a free app) were not researched here. No claim is made about them.
- Whether pinch zoom is available inside the Capacitor web view (relevant to 1.4.4 as a fallback) was not verified.

## 5. What makes a strong product page for a new Health & Fitness app?

### Takeaway
Apple's rules fix the limits (30-character name and subtitle, 100-character keywords (100 bytes in App Store Connect), up to 10 screenshots with most showing the app in use, previews of 15 to 30 seconds of real capture). Apple's guidance says the first one to three images do the selling because they are what search shows. Practitioner data agrees that few people look past the first frames but is old and vendor-sourced. For a new app with no ratings, the levers are a clear subtitle, specific keywords, three strong first screenshots and a declared accessibility section.

### Cited Findings
Screenshots
- "You can feature up to 10 screenshots ... Depending on the orientation of your screenshots, the first one to three images will appear in search results when no app preview is available, so make sure these highlight the essence of your app. Focus each subsequent screenshot on a main benefit or feature". [Apple guidance] — [Product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- "Screenshots should show the app in use, and not merely the title art, login page, or splash screen." [Apple rule] — [Guideline 2.3.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.3)
- "App Review ... will approve only those submissions where more than half of screenshots show the app in use." "Update your screenshots with each new version so they match the current user interface." "You can include messaging and other visuals, as long as the primary app functionality in the screenshot isn't obscured." "one screenshot for a device is sufficient as long as it shows your app in use." [Apple rule, as applied by App Review] — [2.3.3 help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/2-3-3-screenshots)
- "Lead with your best features ... sequence them so they tell a cohesive story". "avoid using the entire asset to highlight general accolades or call-to-action statements." For text on images: "use a short phrase that enhances your visual rather than describes it". Do not include "specific pricing, discounts, website URLs, copyright symbols", unverifiable claims, other platforms' logos, or Apple recognitions. "Assets displayed on the App Store must meet a 4+ age rating, even if your app's rating is higher". [Apple guidance] — [Asset best practices](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/asset-best-practices/)
- Required size: "iPhone: At least one screenshot for iPhone with Dynamic Island (medium display)" (1179 x 2556 or 1206 x 2622 portrait); larger sets scale down. (corrected: the quote is accurate, but the table on the same page still marks iPhone with Face ID (large display), 1284 x 2778 or 1242 x 2688, as "Required if app runs on iPhone and screenshots for iPhone with Dynamic Island (large display) aren't provided", and the page says to provide "the highest required resolution" and let App Store Connect scale down. The medium row falls back to the large rows, not the other way round. The safe reading is to upload a large Dynamic Island set, 1320 x 2868 or 1290 x 2796 or 1260 x 2736, and a medium set.) iPhone Duo: "Starting in April 2027, screenshots will be required for any app using the iOS 27.1 SDK or later"; submitting Duo screenshots adds a "made for iPhone Duo" badge. (corrected: Apple's news post of October 5, 2026, https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=kkphp5qo , says "Starting April 2027, any apps or games submitted will need to include screenshots for iPhone Duo", with no SDK condition. Treat Duo screenshots as required for every submission from April 2027. Sizes: 1398 x 2034 outer display, 2007 x 2853 inner display. Verified 2026-11-10.) [Apple rule] — [Screenshot specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/screenshot-specifications)
- Practitioner data, 2017 (AppAgent via Phiture; five apps, over 25,000 store visitors, three A/B tools): average 7 seconds on a listing; "only 9% of people scroll portrait screenshots of apps"; under 4 percent enlarge a screenshot; under 1 percent expand the description. [Practitioner report, dated] — [Phiture / AppAgent](https://phiture.com/asostack/why-7-seconds-could-make-or-break-your-mobile-app-f41000fb2a17/)

App previews
- "Previews can be up to 30 seconds long and use footage captured on the device". "up to three app previews". "App previews autoplay with muted audio when users view your product page, so make sure the first few seconds of your video are visually compelling. App preview poster frames appear whenever videos do not autoplay." [Apple guidance] — [Product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- "previews may only use video screen captures of the app itself." [Apple rule] — [Guideline 2.3.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.4)
- "Stay within the app ... Don't film people interacting with a device". "App previews must be appropriate for all ages". "By default, app previews play with the sound muted, so consider using copy to add additional context". "Avoid any seasonal or timely references". "Ensure that any transitions between scenes don't imply functionality that your app doesn't include." [Apple guidance] — [App previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/)
- Specifications: optional; up to three per device size and language; minimum 15 seconds, maximum 30 seconds; maximum 500 MB; `.mov`, `.m4v`, `.mp4` (H.264) or ProRes 422 HQ; at most 30 frames per second; default poster frame at 5 seconds; accepted iPhone resolution 886 x 1920 portrait (or 1920 x 886 landscape). ## 6. What does App Store editorial look for, and how is a nomination submitted?

### Takeaway

Innovation. New technologies that solve a unique problem ... Uniqueness. A fresh approach to a familiar category ... Accessibility. A great experience for a broad range of users with well integrated features. Localization. High-quality support for multiple languages ... App Store product page. ### Inferences
- The two Apple pages disagree on minimum lead time (two weeks and three weeks). Use three.

## 7. Ratings and review prompts: what are the rules?

### Takeaway
One rule and a set of guidance. The rule: use Apple's API; custom review prompts are disallowed and manipulating or filtering feedback can end the developer account. The guidance: ask after real engagement, at a pause, never at launch, in onboarding or from a tap, and no more often than every week or two. The system shows the prompt at most three times in 365 days. Logbook has no prompt code yet.

### Cited Findings
- "Use the provided API to prompt users to review your app; this functionality allows customers to provide an App Store rating and review without the inconvenience of leaving your app, and we will disallow custom review prompts." [Apple rule] — [Guideline 5.6.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.6.1)
- "If we find that you have attempted to manipulate reviews, inflate your chart rankings with paid, incentivized, filtered, or fake feedback ... we will take steps ... which may include expelling you from the Apple Developer Program." [Apple rule] — [App Review Guidelines, introduction to section 3 (Business)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#business)
- StoreKit behaviour: "If the person hasn't rated or reviewed your app on this device, StoreKit displays the ratings and review request a maximum of three times within a 365-day period. If the person has rated or reviewed your app on this device, StoreKit displays the ratings and review request if the app version is new, and if more than 365 days have passed since the person's previous review." "Because this API may not present an alert, don't call it in response to a button tap or other user action." In development builds the prompt always shows; "this method has no effect in apps that you distribute for beta testing using TestFlight." [Apple guidance] — [RequestReviewAction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/requestreviewaction)
- Timing: "Make the request at a time that doesn't interrupt what someone is trying to achieve ... for example, at the end of a sequence of events that they successfully complete. Avoid showing a request for a review immediately when a user launches your app, even if it isn't the first time it launches. Avoid requesting a review as the result of a user action." Apple's sample waits until a process was completed at least four times, the current version has not prompted before, and the person has paused "for a few seconds". "people can disable requests for reviews from ever appearing on their device." [Apple guidance] — [Requesting App Store reviews](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/requesting-app-store-reviews)
- "Ask for a rating only after people have demonstrated engagement ... Avoid asking for a rating on first launch or during onboarding". "Avoid interrupting people while they're performing a task". "Avoid pestering people ... Consider allowing at least a week or two between requests". "Prefer the system-provided prompt." [Apple guidance] — [HIG Ratings and reviews](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ratings-and-reviews)
- Manual route: "you may include a persistent link to your App Store product page in your app's settings ... Append the query parameter action=write-review to your product page URL". [Apple guidance] — [RequestReviewAction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/requestreviewaction)
- "Ratings and reviews appear on your product page and in search results, and can influence how your app ranks in App Store search." [Apple guidance] — [App Store search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/) (corrected: this sentence is on the search page, not the ratings page, and Apple's wording is "can influence")
- "Make the request when users are most likely to feel satisfaction with your app, such as when they've completed an action, level, or task." "You can prompt for ratings up to three times in a 365-day period." [Apple guidance] — [Ratings, reviews, and responses](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/) (verified 2026-11-10)
- Resetting: "we recommend using this feature sparingly ... having few ratings may discourage potential users". Marketing use: "You may use your app's rating in marketing materials as long as it accurately reflects your app's current rating. Customer reviews may be used in marketing materials only if permission from the reviewer is granted." [Apple guidance] — [Ratings, reviews, and responses](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/)
- Review summaries: product pages show an LLM-generated summary of reviews on iOS 18.4 or later. [Apple guidance] — [Ratings and reviews overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/monitor-ratings-and-reviews/ratings-and-reviews-overview)
- Capacitor options: `@capawesome/capacitor-app-review` (version 8.x for Capacitor 8) offers `requestReview()` and `openAppStore()`. "On iOS, review requests are limited to 3 requests per year, so the dialog may not appear every time you call requestReview()." "Use requestReview() for gentle prompts and openAppStore(...) for an explicit 'Rate this app' button." A community plugin, `@capacitor-community/in-app-review`, also exists. [Official vendor docs] — [Capawesome App Review](https://capawesome.io/docs/plugins/app-review/)

### Inferences
- A "Do you enjoy Logbook?" screen that sends happy people to the prompt and unhappy people to a feedback form is a filter. It is common in the wild, but the guideline text names "filtered" feedback and "custom review prompts". Do not build it. Offering "Help and feedback" in Settings at all times is fine.
- Good trigger candidates in Logbook: finishing all planned sessions in a week, completing a goal, a seven-day habit streak, each only after at least a week of use and several sessions, and only when the screen is at rest. Never after an AI answer (it may have been unhelpful) and never during onboarding or plan build.
- Since TestFlight builds never show the prompt, the trigger logic must be tested in a development build and by logging the call.

### Gaps
- Apple does not publish how its system decides to show or suppress a prompt beyond the limits quoted.
- No primary source on the best trigger moment for fitness apps. The candidates above are judgement.

## 8. What support and feedback channels are expected?

### Takeaway
A working way to reach the developer is a rule, both inside the app and at the Support URL. Apple also ties it to ratings: a visible support route catches problems before they become one-star reviews. Logbook's Settings has no contact row today.

### Cited Findings
- "People need to know how to reach you with questions and support issues. Make sure your app and its Support URL include an easy way to contact you ... Failure to include accurate and up-to-date contact information not only frustrates customers, but may violate the law in some countries or regions." [Apple rule] — [Guideline 1.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.5)
- "All links in your app must be functional. A link to user support with up-to-date contact information and a link to your privacy policy is required for all apps." [Apple rule, as stated by App Review] — [App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)
- "Ensure that your support contact information is easy to find in your app and on your App Store product page. This gives users a direct way to reach you if they encounter difficulties, and gives you an opportunity to resolve negative experiences that may otherwise lead to poor reviews." [Apple guidance] — [Ratings, reviews, and responses](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/)
- An optional accessibility URL "should correspond uniquely to your app and can include information such as other accessibility features ... or areas of your app that don't support an accessibility feature." [Apple guidance] — [Overview of labels](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)
- If the tutorial is skippable, "make sure it's easy for people to find if they want to view it later ... in a help, account, or settings area". [Apple guidance] — [HIG Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- [Repo] A search of `apps/web/src/features/settings` for contact, support, feedback and help found only the data source help sheets and a privacy page; no contact route.

### Inferences
- Minimum set: a "Help and feedback" row in Settings that opens an email draft or a form (include app version and iOS version automatically, never health data); a public support page with a monitored address and short answers to the likely questions (Apple Health not showing data, Strava connection, deleting the account, what the AI can read); the privacy policy link; optionally an accessibility page.
- For an individual developer the support address is public. Use a dedicated address, not a personal one.
- A signed-out route to support matters too: people who cannot log in or reset a password cannot reach Settings. Put a contact link on the log in screen or the support page.

### Gaps
- No Apple text sets a response time for support. None is claimed.
- Legal contact-detail duties by country (the "may violate the law" clause) were not researched here.

## 9. What should the pre-launch QA matrix contain?

### Takeaway
Apple's own "Testing a release build" document is close to a ready-made matrix: release build, real devices, no debugger, fresh install and update, several OS versions and devices, adverse network, Low Power Mode, low memory, bad input, regions. Add the accessibility settings pass and the permission-denied pass. The largest scoping decision is the deployment target, because 15.0 pulls in 4-inch phones and four generations of WebKit.

### Cited Findings
- "test your release build in a variety of conditions before submitting ... Xcode allows development and release build settings to diverge, and disables watchdog timeouts." "always test release builds on an actual device." "The easiest way to check for watchdog terminations is to disconnect the device from the development machine and launch the app from the home screen." [Apple guidance] — [Testing a release build](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/testing-a-release-build)
- Fresh install and update: "remove the app during development to ensure that Xcode installs a fresh build"; "The system preserves an app's keychain data on the device even after you delete the app"; "test the app update to prevent any regressions". [Apple guidance] — [Testing a release build](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/testing-a-release-build)
- Devices and versions: "test on a wide array of devices and OS versions your app supports." [Apple guidance] — [Testing a release build](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/testing-a-release-build)
- Network: "be sure to also test an IPv6 connection to catch any potential issues related to DNS64 or NAT64"; "To simulate slow or unreliable internet connections on iOS, you can use the Network Link Conditioner ... [Apple guidance] — [Testing a release build](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/testing-a-release-build)
- Power and memory: "enable Low Power Mode in Settings > Battery"; "One way to limit available memory is to open other apps"; memory terminations produce "a jetsam event report". [Apple guidance] — [Testing a release build](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/testing-a-release-build)

- Regions: "Test with a 12-hour region, a 24-hour region, a 12-hour region that's overridden to use 24-hour, and a 24-hour region that's overridden to use 12 hours. [Apple guidance] — [Testing a release build](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/testing-a-release-build)

[Apple guidance] — [App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)

### Inferences
Proposed matrix for Logbook 1.0 (rows are conditions, each run against the common-task list in section 3):

- Devices and OS. (a) A home-button phone on the newest iOS it runs (iPhone SE 2nd or 3rd generation on iOS 27): smallest current screen, no notch. (b) A Dynamic Island phone, medium size, iOS 27: the review device class. (c) A Pro Max or Air: largest layout. (d) One phone on iOS 26 and one on iOS 18 (93 percent of iPhones between them in June 2026). (e) The lowest OS the deployment target allows, on its smallest phone, or raise the target. (f) The resizable simulator for iPhone Duo behaviour if building with Xcode 27 (Apple: the iPhone Duo simulator itself is in Xcode 27.1 and "is the best way to test the full native experience"; verified 2026-11-10).
- Install state. Fresh install; update over the previous TestFlight build with data; delete and reinstall; signed-out launch; expired session.
- Network. Wi-Fi; cellular; Network Link Conditioner "Very Bad Network" or similar; airplane mode at launch and mid-action; IPv6-only; API down; request in flight when the app is backgrounded.
- Interrupted flows. Background and return, and force-quit and relaunch, during: the value pager, sign up, each onboarding step, the notification prompt, plan build, an AI answer in mid-stream, Apple Health first sync, the Strava OAuth round trip (browser and back), writing a journal entry, an open sheet with typed text. Also an incoming call and a notification tap while in each.
- Permissions. Notifications: denied at the prompt, allowed then turned off in Settings. Apple Health: all off, some on, limited history window (iOS 27, see `ios_technical.md`), revoked later. Camera and photos: denied, limited selection.
- Data edge cases. Empty account; a very long name, goal title and journal entry; emoji and right-to-left text in user content; hundreds of entries; a week with no sessions; midnight and time zone change; daylight saving change; year boundary.
- Device state. Low Power Mode; many apps open, then return (web view not blank); very large photo and PDF attachments; rotation attempts (portrait lock holds without glitches); hardware keyboard attached; a third-party keyboard.
- Accessibility. Each setting one at a time (Bold Text, text sizes xSmall, xxxLarge, AX3, AX5, Button Shapes, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast, Differentiate Without Color, Grayscale, Reduce Motion), then VoiceOver, Voice Control, Switch Control.
- Regions. 12-hour and 24-hour, each overridden; week starting Monday and Sunday; miles and kilometres; a non-Gregorian calendar; non-Latin digits.
- Review readiness. The exact build submitted, with the demo account, on the newest iOS: every link opens, no placeholder text, support and privacy pages live, server up.

Notes
- "Interrupted flows" as a list is my construction. Apple's sources for it are the HIG rule to restore state and the release-testing advice to launch from the Home Screen so suspension actually happens.
- A deployment target of 15.0 means iOS 15 WebKit, which lacks some CSS and JavaScript features that newer code may assume. Raising the target is the cheapest way to shrink the matrix; Apple's own tip says so. What to raise it to is a product decision; Apple's June 2026 numbers put everything older than iOS 18 at 7 percent.

### Gaps
- Low storage: no Apple source found that describes a low-storage test for apps, and no number is claimed. It is a sensible condition to try (fill the device, then attach a photo and sync), listed here without a source.
- No Apple usage figure for iOS 27 yet, so the share of people on the OS that App Review tests on is unknown.
- Whether Capacitor 8.5 has known problems on iOS 15 or 16 web views was not researched.
- Which device App Review uses is not published. "Latest software" is the only statement.

## 10. What should be monitored after launch, and what counts as good?

### Takeaway
Apple gives the tools and comparative goals but no pass mark for crash rate. Use Xcode Organizer for crashes, hangs, launch time and regressions, and App Store Connect Analytics for conversion, retention and crash rate against Health & Fitness peers. Vendor benchmarks put the median app at about 99.95 percent crash-free sessions. Because Logbook's logic runs in a web view, most of its failures will be JavaScript errors and blank screens that Apple's crash data never sees.

### Cited Findings
- "Use the Xcode Organizer to view anonymized performance data from your app's users, including launch times, memory usage, UI responsiveness, and impact on the battery." - Goals: "Xcode Organizer compares your app's metrics against two types of goals: similar-app goals, which are based on metrics from apps with functional and technical similarities to yours, and historical performance goals". [Apple guidance] — [Analyzing the performance of your shipping app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/analyzing-the-performance-of-your-shipping-app)

### Inferences
- A working target for Logbook: native crash-free sessions at or above 99.9 percent, and no version worse than the Health & Fitness peer benchmark Apple shows. The 99.9 figure is a practitioner convention, not an Apple number; with a few hundred users one crash moves the percentage a lot, so read counts, not only rates, in the first weeks.
- Apple's crash, hang and launch metrics describe the native shell. A JavaScript exception, a failed chunk load, a white screen after the web content process is discarded, or a failed API call will not appear. Logbook needs its own signal for those: at least server-side logging of client-reported errors (route, app version, iOS version, no health data), and the existing error boundary reporting once per failure. Any third-party crash SDK changes the privacy label (see `ios_technical.md`, IOS-72).
- First month routine: daily for week one (reviews, crashes, server errors, support mail), then weekly (Organizer, Analytics benchmarks, reviews). Reply to every review while volume is low. Keep a 1.0.1 slot ready for the first real-world bug.
- Product page optimization tests (icon and first screenshots) only make sense once there is enough traffic to reach a result.

### Gaps
- Apple publishes no target crash rate, hang rate or launch time for review or for featuring. The 400 ms first-frame goal cited in `ios_technical.md` is separate guidance.
- The Luciq figures come from that vendor's customers, which skew large. No independent benchmark for small indie apps was found.
- Which retention days App Store Connect reports, and the minimum volume before peer benchmarks appear, were not confirmed in this pass.
- No source found for typical conversion rate in Health & Fitness. Use the peer benchmark in Analytics once live.
