# App Store Connect submission requirements and checklist (Logbook, as of 2026-11-10)

Scope: what it takes to submit and release Logbook (iPhone, Capacitor 8, HealthKit read, Strava, server-side Claude assistant, free, individual US developer) on App Store Connect. Adjacent topics (full guideline-by-guideline review, privacy law, Strava and Anthropic terms) are covered by other researchers and are only touched here where they change a submission field.

Source type labels used throughout: **[Apple rule]** = binding text (App Review Guidelines, App Store Connect Help "must/required", upcoming requirements, upload validation), **[Apple guidance]** = Apple documentation, HIG, news posts that advise, **[Law/regulator]**, **[Vendor docs]** (Capacitor, Anthropic, Strava), **[Practitioner]** = forum or blog reports, **[Repo]** = read directly from /home/user/LogBook on 2026-11-10.

Important context: Apple's pages changed a lot in 2026. Everything below was read live on 2026-11-10. The Guidelines page shows "Last Updated: October 16, 2026" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

## 1. Developer Program enrollment, agreements, tax and banking

### Takeaway
An individual can ship a free app with only the 99 USD membership and the Program License Agreement: no D-U-N-S, no tax forms, no bank account. The price of staying individual is that the developer's legal name is the public seller name, and that Guideline 5.1.1(ix) gives App Review a lever to ask for a legal entity if the app is read as a health service.

### Cited Findings
- Individual enrollment needs an Apple Account with two-factor authentication, legal name (no alias or company name), and legal age of majority; Apple verifies name, email, phone and address and "P.O. boxes are not accepted". [Apple rule] — [Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- "Your name will be displayed as the seller name of your apps on the App Store" (individual); "Your organization's name will be displayed as the seller name" (organization). [Apple rule] — [Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- Organizations need legal entity status ("We do not accept DBAs, fictitious business names, trade names, or branches"), a D-U-N-S Number, a work email on the organization's domain and a public, functional website. [Apple rule] — [Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- Fee: "99 USD per membership year." [Apple rule] — [Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- "By joining the Apple Developer Program, you can distribute free apps on the App Store under the Apple Developer Program License Agreement"; "To sell your apps on the App Store or offer In-App Purchases, the Account Holder must sign the Paid Apps Agreement." [Apple rule] — [Sign and update agreements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-agreements/sign-and-update-agreements)
- Workflow step: "If you want to sell your apps or offer In-App Purchases on the App Store, visit the Business section to sign the Paid Apps Agreement. Then, enter banking and tax information." [Apple rule] — [App Store Connect workflow](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/get-started/app-store-connect-workflow)
- "If the most recent version of this agreement hasn't been accepted, you can only offer your app for free." [Apple rule] — [Set a price](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-pricing/set-a-price)
- The Program License Agreement was revised on October 13, 2026 (and March 30, June 8, June 18, August 18, 2026); developers must "sign in to your account to accept the updated terms". The June 8, 2026 revision added Section 7.9 on "providing information regarding apps in App Store Connect, and protection of end users who are minors". [Apple rule] — [Oct 13, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=17mwd36z); [June 8, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=a233fmpw)
- DSA: every account declares trader or non-trader status; traders (individuals) supply an address or P.O. Box, phone and email, verified by two-factor codes and a document, plus "Payment account details, if not already entered in App Store Connect"; the information is published on EU product pages. Factors: revenue from the app, commercial practices, VAT registration, developing "in connection with your trade, business, craft, or profession"; a hobbyist who never intended to commercialize "may not be considered a trader". [Apple rule relaying law] — [DSA trader requirements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements/)
- "Apps without trader status will be removed from the App Store in the European Union (EU) until trader status is provided and verified" (since February 17, 2025). [Apple rule] — [Upcoming requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/)
- Guideline 5.1.1(ix): apps in "highly regulated fields (such as banking and financial services, healthcare, gambling, legal cannabis use, air travel and crypto exchanges) or that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer." [Apple rule] — [Guidelines 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.1)

### Inferences
- Tax and banking are not needed for Logbook v1.0 (free, no IAP). They become mandatory the day a subscription is added, and the Paid Apps Agreement must then be re-accepted after each membership renewal that lapses.
- If the developer intends Logbook as a future business, "trader" is the defensible DSA answer, which puts a home address and phone on EU product pages unless a P.O. Box or business address is used. This is a second reason to consider an LLC before EU launch.
- 5.1.1(ix) says "should", and fitness trackers by individuals are common on the store, so the risk is moderate, not high. It rises with any medical-sounding copy. (corrected 2026-11-10: do not lean on "should". Apple's App Review page, under "Submitted by incorrect entity", says these apps "must be submitted by the legal entity that provides the services rather than an individual developer" and names apps "that require sensitive user information". Health data on a server fits that phrase, so keep the organization route ready.)

### Gaps
- How long individual enrollment verification and DSA trader verification take: no Apple figure found.
- Whether an individual account can later be converted to an organization without changing the bundle ID or losing ratings: Apple's membership help was not read in this pass.
- Whether a US individual who declares "not a trader" for a free app is ever challenged by Apple: no reliable source.

## 3. App Store Connect app record: name, text fields, category, content rights

### Takeaway
The record needs a unique name (30 max), subtitle (30), privacy policy URL (mandatory), category, age rating, content rights, pricing and availability at the app level, and description (4000), keywords (100 bytes), support URL, copyright and screenshots at the version level. Choosing Health & Fitness is the accurate category and triggers one extra declaration (regulated medical device: No).

### Cited Findings
- Name: "at least two characters and no more than 30 characters". Subtitle: "can't be longer than 30 characters". Privacy Policy URL: "Required for iOS and macOS apps." SKU: not visible to customers, cannot be changed. Primary language can be changed. Age rating: "This property is required." License Agreement: "Apple provides a standard EULA ... You may provide a custom license agreement." [Apple rule] — [App information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/)
- Promotional text: "can't be longer than 170 characters" (editable without a new version). Description: required, "Limited to 4000 characters", plain text. Keywords: required, "up to 100 bytes", each longer than two characters, "Names of other apps or companies aren't allowed." Support URL: required, "must lead to actual contact information". Marketing URL: optional. Copyright: required, year plus owner, "The copyright symbol is added automatically." What's New: not available for the first version. [Apple rule] — [Platform version information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/platform-version-information/)
- "Choose a unique app name ... don't try to pack any of your metadata with trademarked terms, popular app names, pricing information ... App names must be limited to 30 characters." [Apple rule] — [Guideline 2.3.7](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.7)
- Categories: "The primary category you select is important for your app's discoverability"; it is where the app appears when people browse or filter search. Health & Fitness: "Apps related to healthy living, including stress management, fitness, and recreational activities" (workout tracking, running, cycling). Productivity: "Apps that make a specific process or task more organized or efficient" (task management, note taking). Lifestyle: "general-interest subject matter or service" (real estate, crafts, hobbies, parenting). Medical: "medical education, information management, or health reference". [Apple rule] — [App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)

[Apple rule] — [App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)

## 9. Regional rules that touch submission (EU, age-assurance laws)

### Takeaway
Two things outside classic App Review now ride on the submission: DSA trader status for the EU, and age-assurance laws (Texas in force since June 4, 2026; Utah since May 6, 2026 and Louisiana since July 1, 2026 for new Apple Accounts; Brazil, Australia, Singapore covered by Apple's tools) (corrected 2026-11-10: Utah and Louisiana were listed as only announced) that put duties on developers through the Declared Age Range API. A US-first launch narrows but does not remove the second.

### Cited Findings
- Texas SB 2420: after a December 2025 injunction was lifted, "new Apple Accounts in Texas are now subject to the law" from June 4, 2026: age assurance and parental consent for downloads, purchases and "significant changes"; developers "can request age category data ... through the Declared Age Range API", use the Significant Change API under PermissionKit, and can receive a server notification when consent is revoked; "it's the developer's responsibility to determine when there's a significant change to their app". [Law relayed by Apple guidance] — [June 3, 2026 news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=sg176nne); background: [Dec 23, 2025](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=8jzbigf4), [Oct 8, 2025](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=btkirlj8)
- February 24, 2026: Apple updated tools "for developers to meet their age assurance obligations under upcoming U.S. and regional laws, including in Brazil, Australia, Singapore, Utah, and Louisiana". The same post gives the dates: "For users with new Apple Accounts in Utah as of May 6, 2026, and in Louisiana as of July 1, 2026, age categories will be shared with the developer's app when requested through the Declared Age Range API." It also says Apple blocks 18+ downloads in Australia, Brazil and Singapore for unconfirmed adults from February 24, 2026 (not relevant to a 9+ or 13+ app). (verified 2026-11-10) [Apple guidance] — [Feb 24, 2026 news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=f5zj08ey)
- "In those regions, you must check the age of the people using your app"; "developers are responsible for their own age restrictions"; "For questions about your compliance obligations, consult your legal counsel"; consent revocation: "Apple will prevent the app from launching" and sends `RESCIND_CONSENT`. [Apple guidance] — [Age assurance Q&A](https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance)
- EU App Tracking Transparency prompt changes from iOS 27.2 (September 16, 2026) affect only apps that track. [Apple guidance] — [Sept 16, 2026 news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=idsft9ai)
- Guideline 2.3.6: "you are responsible for complying with local requirements in each territory where your app is available." [Apple rule] — [Guideline 2.3.6](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.3.6)

### Inferences
- The Declared Age Range API is native (iOS 26+), so a Capacitor app needs a small Swift plugin to call it. Whether Logbook must is a legal question (the Texas statute's developer duties were not read here); the minimum prudent step is a stated minimum age plus an age rating that matches.
- Restricting v1.0 to the US storefront avoids the DSA address publication and GDPR work for now. It does not avoid US state laws.

### Gaps
- The statutory text of Texas SB 2420, Utah and Louisiana laws (what exactly a developer of a free, non-social app must do, and penalties) was not read; only Apple's summaries were. Needs the legal track.
- Effective dates for Utah, Louisiana, Brazil, Australia and Singapore obligations were not captured. (corrected 2026-11-10: Apple's dates are Utah May 6, 2026 and Louisiana July 1, 2026, both for new Apple Accounts, and February 24, 2026 for the 18+ download block in Australia, Brazil and Singapore. The statutes' own effective dates and developer duties are still unverified.)
