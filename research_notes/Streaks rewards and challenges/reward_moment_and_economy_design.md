## 1. Apple's guidance on haptics, motion, sound and accessibility, and what a Capacitor web view can really do

### Takeaway
Apple's rules reduce to five things: use system haptics for their documented meaning, keep feedback brief and tied to a clear cause, match the haptic to the animation and sound, make both motion and haptics optional, and never make people wait on an animation they will see more than once. Inside Capacitor the Haptics plugin gives only Light, Medium and Heavy impacts, the three notification types, selection ticks and one flat full-strength buzz of a set length; anything richer (Rigid, Soft, intensity, a designed Core Haptics pattern with synced audio) needs a small custom native plugin.

### Cited Findings

**Haptics (Human Interface Guidelines, "Playing haptics")**
- **[Platform rule]** "Use system-provided haptic patterns according to their documented meanings... If the documented use case for a pattern doesn't make sense in your app or game, avoid using the pattern to mean something else. Instead, use a generic pattern or create your own, where supported." ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** Documented meanings. Notification: "feedback about the outcome of a task or action, such as depositing a check or unlocking a vehicle"; Success "Indicates that a task or action has completed." Impact: "a physical metaphor you can use to complement a visual experience. For example, people might feel a tap when a view snaps into place or a thud when two heavy objects collide"; Light, Medium, Heavy are collisions between small, medium and large UI objects; Rigid is "hard or inflexible", Soft is "soft or flexible". Selection: "feedback while the values of a UI element are changing." ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** Consistency: "build a clear, causal relationship between each haptic and the action that causes it... If a haptic doesn't reinforce a cause-and-effect relationship, it can be confusing and seem gratuitous." Apple's own example is that a pattern used for a failed mission must not also be used for "a positive outcome like a level completion". ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** Matching: "you generally want to match the intensity and sharpness of a haptic with the intensity and sharpness of the animation it accompanies. You can also synchronize sound with haptics." ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** Overuse: "Sometimes a haptic can feel just right when it happens occasionally, but become tiresome when it plays frequently... Often, the best haptic experience is one that people may not be conscious of, but miss when it's turned off." ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** Length: "In most apps, prefer playing short haptics that complement discrete events... long-running haptics in an app can dilute the meaning of the feedback and distract people from their task." ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** Optional: "Let people turn off or mute haptics, and make sure people can still enjoy your app or game without them." ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** Custom patterns: "Games often use custom haptics to enhance gameplay. Although it's less common, nongame apps might also use custom haptics to provide a richer, more delightful experience." Building blocks are transient events ("brief and compact, often feeling like taps or impulses"), continuous events ("sustained vibrations"), sharpness ("soft, rounded, or organic" versus "crisp, precise, or mechanical") and intensity, "including optional audio content". ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** UIKit has four concrete feedback generators: `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator`, `UISelectionFeedbackGenerator`, `UINotificationFeedbackGenerator` ("to indicate successes, failures, and warnings") and `UICanvasFeedbackGenerator`. The plain `init()` is marked deprecated in favor of `init(view:)`. ([Apple: UIFeedbackGenerator](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uifeedbackgenerator))
- **[Platform rule]** Apple's three audio and haptic design principles (WWDC 2021, session 10278). Causality: "For feedback to be useful, it must be obvious what caused it." Harmony: "It should feel the way it looks, and the way it sounds. A small ball should feel small and sound small." Utility: "Don't add feedback just because you can. It can quickly become overwhelming and unpleasant. Reserve haptics and audio for significant moments in your app." The session's sample pairs one haptic and audio asset with "an animation that is 500 milliseconds in duration". ([Apple WWDC21: Practice audio haptic design](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10278))
- **[Platform rule]** "The simulator doesn't work for haptics. You need a physical iPhone 8 or newer to feel the haptics." ([Apple WWDC21: Practice audio haptic design](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10278))

**Motion (HIG, "Motion")**
- **[Platform rule]** "Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion. Gratuitous or excessive animation can distract people and may make them feel disconnected or physically uncomfortable." ([Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion))
- **[Platform rule]** "Make motion optional... it's essential to avoid using it as the only way to communicate important information... supplement visual feedback by also using alternatives like haptics and audio." ([Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion))
- **[Platform rule]** "Aim for brevity and precision in feedback animations... when a game displays a succinct animation that's precisely tied to a successful action, players can instantly get the message without being distracted from their gameplay." ([Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion))
- **[Platform rule]** "In apps, generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently... you generally want to avoid making people spend extra time paying attention to unnecessary motion every time they interact with it." ([Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion))
- **[Platform rule]** "Let people cancel motion. As much as possible, don't make people wait for an animation to complete before they can do anything, especially if they have to experience the animation more than once." ([Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion))

**Reduce Motion and accessibility of animated content (HIG, "Accessibility")**
- **[Platform rule]** With Reduce Motion on, "ensure your app or game responds by reducing automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion." Apple's listed practices: "Tightening animation springs to reduce bounce effects", "Tracking animations directly with people's gestures", "Avoiding animating depth changes in z-axis layers", "Replacing transitions in x-, y-, and z-axes with fades to avoid motion", "Avoiding animating into and out of blurs". ([Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility))
- **[Platform rule]** "Be cautious with fast-moving and blinking animations... it can be distracting, cause dizziness, and in some cases even result in epileptic episodes." ([Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility))
- **[Platform rule]** "Minimize use of time-boxed interface elements. Views and controls that auto-dismiss on a timer can be problematic for people who need longer to process information, and for people who use assistive technologies... Prefer dismissing views with an explicit action." ([Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility))
- **[Platform rule]** "Use haptics in addition to audio cues. If your interface conveys information through audio cues, such as a success chime, error sound, or game feedback, consider pairing that sound with matching haptics for people who can't perceive the audio or have their audio turned off." Also "Convey information with more than color alone." ([Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility))
- **[Platform rule]** "Avoid autoplaying audio and video content without also providing controls to start and stop it." ([Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility))
- **[Platform rule]** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3 (Level AAA): "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled, unless the animation is essential". Sufficient techniques include the CSS `prefers-reduced-motion` query in CSS (C39) or in JavaScript (SCR40). ([W3C: Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html))
- **[Platform rule]** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 (Level A): moving or blinking content that "(1) starts automatically, (2) lasts more than five seconds" and "(3) is presented in parallel with other content" needs "a mechanism for the user to pause, stop, or hide it". ([W3C: Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html))

**Sound and the silent switch (HIG, "Playing audio")**
- **[Platform rule]** "People switch a device to silent when they want to avoid being interrupted by unexpected sounds... they also want to silence nonessential sounds, such as keyboard clicks, sound effects, game soundtracks, and other audible feedback. When a device is in silent mode, it plays only the audio that people explicitly initiate, like media playback, alarms, and audio/video messaging." ([Apple HIG: Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio))
- **[Platform rule]** Audio session categories: Ambient ("Sound isn't essential, and it doesn't silence other audio") "Responds to the silence switch. Mixes with other sounds." Solo ambient responds to the switch but "Doesn't mix with other sounds". Playback "Doesn't respond to the silence switch". Apple adds: "don't make people stop listening to music from another app if you don't need to." ([Apple HIG: Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio))
- **[Platform rule]** "People expect their volume settings to affect all sound in the system, including music and in-app sound effects." ([Apple HIG: Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio))
- **[Practitioner]** (search summary) In WebKit on iOS, Web Audio is treated as "ambient" sound, so it mixes with other apps and obeys the mute switch, while HTML media elements ignore the switch. WebKit engineers point to `navigator.audioSession.type` as the way to change the category from web content. ([WebKit bug 237322](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322))

**What Capacitor's Haptics plugin exposes on iOS**
- **[Platform rule]** The plugin API is six calls: `impact({style})`, `notification({type})`, `vibrate({duration})`, `selectionStart()`, `selectionChanged()`, `selectionEnd()`. `ImpactStyle` has only `Heavy`, `Medium`, `Light` (default Heavy). `NotificationType` has `Success`, `Warning`, `Error` (default Success). `vibrate` takes a `duration` in ms (default 300). "On devices that don't have Taptic Engine or Vibrator, the API calls will resolve without performing any action." ([Capacitor docs: Haptics](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** Installed source (version 8.0.2 in this repo). `impact` creates a fresh `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator(style:)` and calls `impactOccurred()` with no `prepare()` and no intensity argument; the bridge maps only the strings MEDIUM and LIGHT and falls back to heavy, so Rigid and Soft are not reachable. `notification` creates a fresh `UINotificationFeedbackGenerator` each call. `selectionStart` creates and prepares a `UISelectionFeedbackGenerator`; `selectionChanged` fires and re-prepares it. Every call is dispatched with `DispatchQueue.main.async` and the JS promise resolves at once, before the haptic plays. (Local file: `node_modules/.pnpm/@capacitor+haptics@8.0.2_@capacitor+core@8.5.3/node_modules/@capacitor/haptics/ios/Sources/HapticsPlugin/Haptics.swift` and `HapticsPlugin.swift`; API reference at [Capacitor docs: Haptics](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/haptics))
- **[Platform rule]** Installed source: `vibrate(duration)` does use Core Haptics, but only as one `hapticContinuous` event at intensity 1.0 and sharpness 1.0 for the given duration, with a new `CHHapticEngine` created per call; where Core Haptics is unsupported it falls back to `AudioServicesPlayAlertSound(kSystemSoundID_Vibrate)`. There is no API for patterns, transients, intensity curves or AHAP files. (Same local files; API reference at [Capacitor docs: Haptics](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/haptics))
- **[Practitioner]** (search summary) iOS Safari has no `navigator.vibrate`. A known workaround clicks a hidden `<input type="checkbox" switch>` label so Safari plays its switch haptic; sources disagree on whether that arrived in Safari 17.4 or 18.0, a WebKit bug asks that it require user activation, and none of the sources tested it inside WKWebView. ([WebKit bug 285120](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=285120); [ios-haptics README](https://unpkg.com/ios-haptics@0.0.8/README.md))
- **[Platform rule]** What this app already has: a named-event wrapper with a user preference (`logbook.haptics`), `orb` (Light impact, fired on the pop's peak frame through `hapticAtPeak`), `success` (Success notification, "Exactly two a day"), `celebrate` ("Heavy, then Success 120ms later. At most once a week"), and `playHaptics`, which schedules a timed phrase against the animation's own clock and drops any step more than 120 ms late and anything while the page is hidden. (Local file: `/home/user/LogBook/apps/web/src/lib/haptics.ts`)

**Keeping a celebration smooth in WKWebView**
- **[Practitioner]** "Where possible, restrict animations to opacity and transform to keep animations on the compositing stage of the rendering path." Animating layout or paint properties is "particularly expensive". CSS animations and native Web Animations typically run on the compositor thread, so they continue while the main thread is busy. ([web.dev: How to create high-performance CSS animations](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide); [web.dev: Animations and performance](https://web.dev/articles/animations-and-performance))
- **[Practitioner]** "Use the will-change property sparingly, and only if you encounter a performance issue"; overuse "can cause the browser to waste resources". Apply it from JavaScript when a change is about to happen and remove it afterward. ([web.dev: How to create high-performance CSS animations](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide))
- **[Practitioner]** Lottie versus Rive (search summary, mostly vendor claims): Rive says to test on the devices you ship to and cites Duolingo reporting a 15x file size reduction after adopting Rive; one React article puts the Rive WASM module at 78 KB; a developer article says Lottie parses large JSON at runtime and its cost depends on animation complexity. None of the results gave independent canvas versus WebGL frame-rate measurements. ([Rive blog](https://rive.app/blog/rive-as-a-lottie-alternative); [Callstack: Lottie vs Rive](https://callstack.com/blog/lottie-vs-rive-optimizing-mobile-app-animation); [DEV: optimizing Rive in React](https://dev.to/alex_barashkov/optimization-techniques-for-rive-animations-in-react-apps-1a8p))
- **[Platform rule]** Apple's only frame-rate statement in the Motion page is for games: "maintaining a consistent frame rate of 30 to 60 fps typically results in a smooth, visually appealing experience." ([Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion))

### Inferences
- The Success notification is the documented haptic for "a task or action has completed", so it is the right single haptic for "you earned an Orb". Impact is for things that visibly collide or land (an Orb dropping into its counter). Selection is for values changing under a finger and is the wrong choice for a counter that rolls up by itself.
- Because the plugin fires fresh, unprepared generators through an async hop and resolves before the haptic plays, exact frame sync is not guaranteed. The app's existing approach (fire on the visual peak, drop late steps) is the right mitigation. A multi-tap "phrase" built from JS timers will have some jitter; keep phrases to two or three well-spaced events (100 ms or more apart) so jitter is not felt as wrongness.
- A true "glowing orb" haptic (a soft swell that resolves in a thud, with the sound in the same asset) is only possible with Core Haptics and an AHAP file, which means a custom plugin. The repo already ships one custom native plugin (`packages/capacitor-health`), so the pattern exists. Until then, the best available composition is Light or Medium impact at the landing frame, then Success, which is what `celebrate` already does with Heavy.
- `Haptics.vibrate({duration})` is a flat full-strength buzz. By Apple's "prefer short haptics that complement discrete events" rule it should not be used for a celebration.
- Sound for a reward moment is a "nonessential sound effect" in Apple's terms, so it must go silent with the switch and must not interrupt the person's music: the Ambient category. In a web view, Web Audio already behaves that way; do not switch the session to playback, and do not use an HTML audio element for the chime (search-level evidence says media elements ignore the switch). Because sound will often be off, the haptic and the visual must each carry the whole message alone.
- Reduce Motion version of the reward: no scale pop, no flight path, no particles, no blur glow animating in or out. Cross-fade the Orb into its lit state and change the number, keep the haptic. This follows Apple's "replace transitions with fades" and "avoid animating into and out of blurs" lines directly.
- A full-screen milestone moment should end on an explicit tap (Apple: "Prefer dismissing views with an explicit action"), and any tap should cut the animation short (Apple: "don't make people wait for an animation to complete"). Keeping the automatic part under five seconds also stays clear of WCAG 2.2.2.
- For the Orb itself: animate `transform` and `opacity` only. A glow drawn as a pre-rendered gradient layer whose opacity and scale animate is compositor-friendly; animating `box-shadow`, `filter: blur()` radius or gradient stops is paint work on every frame. Particles belong on one canvas, with a small cap on count, not as dozens of DOM nodes.

My unverified recollection is that Apple describes the prepared state as lasting on the order of seconds and says not to call `prepare()` and fire immediately.

## 2. Game "juice" and feedback design: principles, durations, easing and sequencing

### Takeaway
Juice is redundant, layered feedback on one action (motion, particles, sound, touch), and the best-evidenced rule is "moderate, not maximum": a study of 3,018 players found medium and high juice beat both none and extreme. For interface use, practitioners converge on 100 to 500 ms for most motion, ease-out by default, a windup before a big payoff, and shorter, subtler motion the more often it plays; specific numbers for a collect-and-fly or counter roll-up are thinly sourced and should be tuned by testing.

### Cited Findings

**The canonical talks and writing**
- **[Practitioner]** "Juice It or Lose It" was given by Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho at GDC Europe 2012: "Martin and Petri will demonstrate the neat little tricks you can apply to any game to make it more satisfying to play. To do this they will be cranking a boring old game up to eleven, live on stage. There will be particles, children cheering, and you get the source code too!" ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1016487/Juice-It-or-Lose))
- **[Practitioner]** (search summary) Jonasson's definition of juice, as quoted secondhand: "things that wobble, squirt, bounce around, and make little cute noises." A 2012 Game Developer piece ran under the headline "Indies, resist the urge to 'juice it or lose it'", a sign that the over-polish critique is as old as the talk. ([ModDB: Make it juicy](https://www.moddb.com/games/zeroes/news/make-it-juicy); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-indies-resist-the-urge-to-juice-it-or-lose-it-))
- **[Practitioner]** (search summary) Jan Willem Nijman's "The Art of Screenshake" (2013, INDIGO Classes) starts from a dull side-scrolling shooter and applies about 30 small tweaks one at a time. A secondhand university slide summary lists basic animation and sound, bigger explosions, lingering smoke for permanence, and slow motion among them. One write-up notes the screenshake "degenerates quickly", and a commentator cautions that the talk shows the importance of physicality and is not a general guide to game feel. ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games); [Infovore: Making Game "Feel"](https://infovore.org/tag/feel/); [Univ. Bayreuth slides](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/GxD-10-Game-Feel.pdf))
- **[Practitioner]** The most directly relevant teardown is Andy Allen's account of the checkbox in (Not Boring) Habits, a habit app that won a 2022 Apple Design Award (award per search summary). Design choices: a long press instead of a tap so the action is deliberate, with the build animation starting instantly on press; particles "feed" a growing sphere while a solid sphere fills the empty checkbox; a big action, "in order to feel big", "needs a big wind up"; "if you release the hold early, the animation plays in reverse to reset"; on release the sphere and checkmark pop in and a starburst fills the screen; rays take the color of the user's chosen skin; a rotate-and-scale transition replaces a fade. ([Andy Allen: The Art of Game Feel (a.k.a Juice) in Product Design](https://notbor.ing/words/the-most-satisfying-checkbox))
- **[Practitioner]** Same source on sound and haptics: the main sound "swells slowly and then releases in a satisfying 'ka-thunk!'"; sound can be "thwarted by the mute switch", while haptics work anywhere and are "sound for touch"; the haptic pattern was generated from the sound file, cleaned up and shipped as an AHAP file. The team chose not to use screen shake. ([Andy Allen](https://notbor.ing/words/the-most-satisfying-checkbox))
- **[Practitioner]** Disney's twelve principles (Johnston and Thomas) are widely mapped onto interface motion; easing (slow in and slow out) gets the most agreement, anticipation is applied to presses and drag starts, and squash and stretch maps to scale transforms. A counter-view argues most of the twelve solve a different problem and that exaggeration, solid drawing and appeal are largely irrelevant to interface behavior. (search summary) ([IxDF](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/ui-animation-how-to-apply-disney-s-12-principles-of-animation-to-ui-design); [UX in Motion: "Disney is Dead"](https://medium.com/ux-in-motion/ui-animation-principles-disney-is-dead-8bf6c66207f9))
- **[Practitioner]** (search summary) Write-ups of Apple's WWDC 2018 "Designing Fluid Interfaces" give three properties for motion that feels good under a finger: responsive, interruptible and redirectable. Springs start fast and spend most of their time settling; a damping of 1 settles with no bounce. ([Nathan Gitter: Building Fluid Interfaces](https://medium.com/@nathangitter/building-fluid-interfaces-ios-swift-9732bb934bf5))

**Durations and easing**
- **[Practitioner]** "the duration of most animations should be in the range of 100–500 ms"; simple feedback such as a checkbox "should be roughly 100 ms"; substantial screen changes "200–300 ms"; "400ms being a very slow animation, to be used only for big movements across large screens"; "At 500ms, animations start to feel like a real drag for users." ([NN/g: Animation duration](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/))
- **[Practitioner]** "the more frequent the animation, the more subtle and shorter you'll want it to be." A long checkmark animation is "very likely to be annoying with repetition." "It is far more common for animations to be too long than too short." ([NN/g: Animation duration](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/))
- **[Practitioner]** "Completely linear motion looks weird and unnatural"; "Most frequently, you'll want to use an ease-out animation, that starts quickly but slows down"; ease-in can "feel unresponsive if the initial motion takes a little while to get going"; entering objects need "a subtly longer duration" than exiting ones (example: 300 ms in, 200 to 250 ms out). ([NN/g: Animation duration](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/))
- **[Platform rule]** Apple's sample "significant moment" (a shield forming on a ball) uses a 500 ms animation with one synced haptic and audio asset. ([Apple WWDC21 10278](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10278))

**Collect-and-fly-to-counter, counter roll-up, stagger** (all search summaries of developer write-ups; low confidence)
- **[Practitioner]** A straight-line flight reads as cheap; add a height offset that peaks mid-flight or a Bezier path. Accelerate on launch and ease out into the counter slot. ([MarbleMaze: Bezier curve currency animations](https://sstyles93.github.io/blogs/mmgs_bezier); [Bugnet: coin pickup tween arc](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-coin-pickup-tween-arc-looks-flat-no-height))
- **[Practitioner]** One game example updates the HUD counter as soon as the first icon lands, and fires the completion event only once every icon has finished; another caps the spawned icons at 10 however large the reward. ([MarbleMaze](https://sstyles93.github.io/blogs/mmgs_bezier); [CoinZoom](https://github.com/leonard4/CoinZoom))
- **[Practitioner]** For product UI staggering, one guide suggests roughly 30 to 80 ms between items with the total kept under about 300 ms, and staggering only the first few items of a long list. ([Emil Kowalski skill notes: staggered animations](https://instagit.com/emilkowalski/skills/staggered-animations-delay-intervals/))

**Research on juice**
- **[Research]** Hicks et al., CHI PLAY 2019, "Juicy Game Design: Understanding the Impact of Visual Embellishments on Player Experience": two studies (40 and 32 participants). Embellishments "contribute to the visual appeal of all games, but only affects aspects such as competence under specific circumstances." (search summary) ([DOI 10.1145/3311350.3347171](https://doi.org/10.1145/3311350.3347171))
- **[Research]** Kao, 2020, "The effects of juiciness in an action RPG" (Entertainment Computing), N = 3,018, four otherwise identical versions (None, Medium, High, Extreme). Secondary summaries report that Medium and High did better than None and Extreme across measures, and that "none and extreme amounts of juiciness significantly decrease player experience." (search summary; full text not read) ([DOI 10.1016/j.entcom.2020.100359](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcom.2020.100359); [Improbable Research summary](https://improbable.com/tag/rpg/))
- **[Research]** A University of Waterloo study on "juicy haptics" found that vibrotactile feedback, in either a "low juicy" or "high juicy" version, improved player experience measures over none. (search summary) ([Waterloo Haptic Experience Lab](https://uwaterloo.ca/haptic-experience-lab/projects/juicy-haptic-design-vibrotactile-embellishments-can-improve))

### Inferences
- A workable three-beat grammar for the Orb, assembled from the sources above: (1) anticipation, about 100 to 150 ms (the control dips or the Orb gathers light; starts on the touch frame); (2) payoff, about 200 to 350 ms (pop with ease-out or a lightly damped spring, haptic on the peak frame, sound thud on the same frame); (3) settle and bookkeeping, about 300 to 600 ms (the Orb or a token arcs to the counter with an ease-in start and ease-out landing, counter ticks as it lands). Total about 0.6 to 1.1 s for the everyday earn. These numbers are my synthesis from NN/g's ranges, Apple's 500 ms example and Allen's windup rule, not a published standard.
- The counter should change when the first token lands and be final when the last lands. For a roll-up, ease-out over a fixed short duration (about 400 to 800 ms) regardless of the amount, so large awards do not take longer; with small integer amounts (1 to 5) a roll-up is unnecessary and a single flip or tick per Orb reads better.
- A multiplier reveal is its own beat and should come after the base award has landed: show the base number, pause about 150 to 250 ms (the "moment of no motion" practitioners recommend), then stamp the multiplier and let the total update once. Putting both in one motion hides the cause, which breaks Apple's causality principle.
- Scale the celebration with rarity, not with the number: the daily earn stays in the 1 s class; a milestone gets the windup, full-screen burst and the richer haptic, 2 to 4 s, skippable. Allen's "a big action needs a big wind up" plus Kao's "extreme is worse" both point at reserving the big version.
- Screen shake is a combat idiom. Allen's team left it out of a habit app, and Reduce Motion rules argue against it. Skip it.
- Follow-through for an Orb: a small overshoot and settle on landing (spring with slight bounce), and a glow that lingers and decays over about 400 to 600 ms after the motion stops. Under Reduce Motion, tighten the spring to no bounce, as Apple says.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a primary list of the techniques in "Juice It or Lose It" (the video is on GDC Vault, no transcript fetched). From background knowledge, unverified in this session, the talk layers: tweening with easing on everything, squash and stretch, staggered delays, sound on every event with pitch rising on consecutive hits, particles, screen shake, color flashes, and "personality" (eyes on the paddle).
- I did not source Steve Swink's "Game Feel" directly. From background knowledge, unverified here: Swink frames game feel as real-time control, simulated space and polish, and cites response-time thresholds of roughly 100 ms for an action to feel instantaneous.
- No authoritative published timing for coin fly-to-counter, counter roll-up or multiplier reveal was found. The ranges above are synthesis and should be tuned on device with the owner.

## 3. Teardowns of reward moments in consumer apps: length, frequency, opt-outs, reception, and habituation

### Takeaway
The pattern across well-run apps is a two-tier system: a tiny, sub-second acknowledgment on every completion, and a large, character-driven celebration only at spaced milestones or at random low frequency, usually with a setting to turn it off. The cautionary cases are Robinhood (celebration attached to a risky, repeatable action, removed in 2021 and restricted by a 2024 consent order) and Asana (delight some users found childish and could not reliably turn off). Evidence that repeated gamified feedback loses force is real but comes mostly from classroom studies: effects dropped after about four weeks.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- How the leaders keep big moments rare: (a) fixed, widening milestones (Duolingo: 7, 30, 100, 365); (b) random low frequency with a rarer variant for harder work (Asana's yeti under the oldest tasks); (c) a dated, once-only award (Apple's 10-year award). All three give the person something to anticipate that everyday completion does not deliver.
- The opt-out is standard equipment: Todoist (goal celebrations, Karma), Headspace (run streak), Asana (two switches). Asana's failure mode is instructive: a switch that does not reliably work is worse than none. For Orby: one setting for "celebrations" that reduces every reward moment to the quiet version, separate from the existing haptics switch.
- Robinhood's lesson generalizes beyond finance: do not attach celebration to an action that can be repeated at will and that is not itself the goal. Confetti for "firsts" drew criticism once it read as rewarding the act of trading. For a habit app the equivalent is celebrating taps (check, uncheck, check) and not verified outcomes (the day closed, the run synced from Apple Health).
- The Headspace anecdote (fast-forwarding to keep a streak) is the self-report version of the same failure: when the reward is easier to reach than the behavior, people take the shortcut. A single-user personal app has no opponent to cheat, so the design target is not policing but keeping the honest path the easiest path.
- Given the four-week novelty dip in the research, plan for the Orb moment to stop being interesting by the second month, and put the lasting value in the collection and its thresholds (section 5), not in the animation.
- Finch shows a soft currency working without real money when it buys visible, personal, cosmetic change; it also shows the cost when upkeep becomes "another task".

## 4. Soft currency and multiplier design: rules that keep a small points economy meaningful and hard to game

### Takeaway
Economy designers treat a currency as a flow between faucets (ways to earn) and sinks (ways to spend); with no sink it becomes a score, which can still work if, like Todoist Karma, it has levels, rising difficulty and decay. The exploits on record (Duolingo XP farming, Zwift coasting, Headspace fast-forwarding) all come from rewarding an action that is cheaper than the behavior it stands for, and the fixes are consistent: tie rewards to verified effort, cap or cool down the high-value sources, make multipliers bounded, time-limited and resettable, and keep the rule simple enough to state in a line.

### Cited Findings

**Economy basics**
- **[Practitioner]** "Every game economy is built from four basic parts: currencies, faucets, sinks, and converters." "Soft currency is usually earned through play and comes in the form of coins, gold, or energy." "Faucets are how players can earn currency and control the pace of progression." On sinks: "Without enough of them, players stockpile rewards until nothing feels worth earning or buying." ([Unity: Game economy design guide](https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide))
- **[Practitioner]** Health measures: inflation rate (faucets minus sinks over a period), sink-to-faucet ratio, and "days-of-currency-on-hand" (average unspent balance expressed as days of typical earnings). ([Unity](https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide))
- **[Practitioner]** Pacing: "Reward tiers should unlock quickly at first to build early emotional investment"; an example cadence is about one meaningful reward a day for an engaged player, stretching to every two or three days for a casual one; "Plotted on a graph, the thresholds should form an S-curve rather than a straight line." ([Unity](https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide))
- **[Practitioner]** Common mistakes and fixes: inflation spirals (audit every faucet against a matching sink); broken exchange rates ("re-anchor the rate to a fixed unit of time or effort"); too-generous early rewards (save generous rewards for milestones); uncapped sources ("Cap ad-based currency like any other faucet"); insufficient sinks (add rotating or limited-time sinks). (search summary for one point: limited-time events that inject currency without sinks create an inflation spike that outlasts the event.) ([Unity](https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide))

**Todoist Karma: a score with levels, scaling difficulty and decay**
- **[Platform rule for that product]** Points are earned for adding tasks, completing tasks on time, using advanced features (labels, recurring dates, reminders), reaching daily or weekly goals, and keeping streaks. "The higher the Karma level you have, the more challenging it becomes to earn points." Points are lost "when you have tasks that are five or more days overdue" (another help page says four; search summary). ([Todoist Help: Introduction to Karma](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/introduction-to-karma-OgWkWy))
- **[Platform rule for that product]** Eight levels with widening thresholds: Beginner 0 to 499, Novice 500 to 2,499, Intermediate 2,500 to 4,999, Professional 5,000 to 7,499, Expert 7,500 to 9,999, Master 10,000 to 19,999, Grand Master 20,000 to 49,999, Enlightened 50,000+. Reaching Enlightened unlocks a mystery theme. The default score is 50. ([Todoist Help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/introduction-to-karma-OgWkWy))
- **[Platform rule for that product]** Default goals are 5 tasks a day and 25 a week (search summary). Goals are checked each time a task is completed, so lowering a goal mid-day without completing another task can still break a streak. Days off can be chosen per weekday; vacation mode freezes Karma and streaks. Support can restore the most recently broken streak (search summary: up to three times per account) but cannot set custom streaks. ([Todoist Help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/introduction-to-karma-OgWkWy))

**Zwift Drops: effort-priced, with bounded and resettable multipliers**
- **[Practitioner]** The formula is not public ("Zwift isn't sharing the formula"). The main driver is calories from power: "Zwift is constantly adjusting your Drop rate based on your watts." A 300 W minute earned about 470 Drops on the flat and about 770 on a 9% climb (63% more). The article puts one Drop at roughly one US cent. Leveling up pays a fixed 50,000 Drops. The sink is the Drop Shop (launched February 2019) for frames and wheels. ([Zwift Insider: How Drops work](https://zwiftinsider.com/drops/))
- **[Practitioner]** Multipliers are bounded, time-based and lose their progress on exit: riding near a RoboPacer raises the multiplier from 1.2x up to 2.5x the longer you stay, and teleporting to another RoboPacer resets it; a Ride On doubles the rate for 15 seconds. ([Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/drops/); [Zwift Forums: RoboPacer multiplier](https://forums.zwift.com/t/how-robopacer-drops-multiplier-game-works/628822))
- **[Practitioner]** Exploit and fix: after Bike Upgrades launched in March 2025 (progress measured in distance or time), riders teleported onto long descents and coasted to accumulate progress with no effort. Update 1.95 (August 2025) said only: "We made some improvements to ensure that bike upgrades are unlocked via genuine efforts." A test found only about 3 km of a 12 km coasted descent counted for one bike and 0 to 2 minutes of a 9-minute coast for another. Zwift said it would not retroactively revoke rewards earned through the exploit. ([Zwift Insider: Testing the anti-cheat measures](https://zwiftinsider.com/upgrades-exploit-test/))
- **[Practitioner]** Inflation indicator: the author earned about 39,000 Drops an hour, projecting to over 14 million a year, and cites over 60,000 an hour with boosts. ([Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/drops/))

**Duolingo XP: farming under competitive pressure**
- **[Press]** Kotaku (June 5, 2023): a normal 5 to 10 minute lesson grants 10 to 20 XP and a casual player might earn about 50 XP a day, yet farmers earned over a thousand XP in hours by repeating easy exercises, switching to an easier language, stacking limited-time boosts, using legendary challenges, timing a last-minute lesson to win a league, or running scripts and auto-clickers. One user "had earned 25% of his total XP in 6 years of using the app in just this week alone." Duolingo: "We do take steps to reduce XP exploitation on Duolingo." ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/duolingo-app-cheats-hacks-leagues-xp-why-duohacker-1850506482))
- **[Practitioner]** (search summaries, pages not read) One newsletter says Duolingo raised path-lesson XP from roughly 10 to 25, read as rewarding the core activity over farmable side activities; another piece reports new mandatory waits between consecutive rounds of high-value activities. ([DuoPlanet](https://newsletter.duoplanet.com/posts/has-duolingo-finally-fixed-xp); [Substack: When gamification goes too far](https://linksiwouldgchatyou.substack.com/p/when-gamification-goes-too-far))
- **[Practitioner]** Legibility: Duolingo found a streak tied to XP "confused users"; "one lesson a day" worked better. ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks))

**Habitica: a daily cap on random drops, and undo behavior**
- **[Platform rule for that product]** (search summaries) Habitica limits random item drops with a daily drop cap; subscribers get "a doubled drop cap"; a release added a notification "when the user reaches the daily limit for item drops". A completed Daily gives XP, gold, mana and "a chance of a drop", and "if a drop is received, it is also not removed when the Daily is unchecked; this is intended behavior." Checklist items pay a fixed amount each, with a bonus on completing the parent task. ([Habitica Wiki: What's New 2022](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Whats_New_2022); [Habitica release v4.166.1](https://newreleases.io/project/github/HabitRPG/habitica/release/v4.166.1); [Habitica Wiki: Establishing Your Goals](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Establishing_Your_Goals))

**Rewards that are cheaper than the behavior**
- **[Press]** (search summary) A Headspace user "admitted to fast-forwarding through his meditations simply to keep his streak going." ([Mindful.org](https://www.mindful.org/what-does-headspace-want/))

### Inferences
- **With no sink, the Orb count is a score. That is fine if it is designed as one.** Todoist Karma shows the minimum kit for a score that stays meaningful: named levels with widening thresholds, earning that gets harder with level, and something that can be lost. For a collectible Orb the gentler equivalent is: a visible collection, thresholds that unlock cosmetic change (section 5), and no spending at all. If Orbs are ever spendable (streak repair, a cosmetic), that is the sink and it needs a price that takes days, not minutes, of honest effort ("re-anchor the rate to a fixed unit of time or effort").
- **Fixed, small, integer awards.** Anchor one Orb to one unit of real effort (a closed day, a completed planned workout). Small integers stay legible, need no rounding, and keep each Orb feeling like an object. Zwift's millions of Drops a year are the counter-example: numbers that large stop meaning anything per unit. If multipliers produce fractions, round down at the moment of award and show the arithmetic ("2 Orbs x 1.5 = 3"), never a hidden fractional balance.
- **Multipliers: bounded, visible, time-limited, earned by continuity.** Zwift's design is the template: a floor of 1x, a published ceiling (2.5x), growth with continuous time, reset on leaving. For a streak multiplier: steps at published streak lengths, a hard cap (for example 2x or 3x), and a reset or step-down rule stated in the same sentence. A cap matters because an uncapped streak multiplier makes late Orbs worth many times early ones, which is inflation, and makes a broken streak catastrophic, which is the guilt problem Todoist softens with days off, vacation mode and streak restore.
- **Diminishing returns and caps against farming.** Options seen in the wild: a daily cap on the lucrative source (Habitica's drop cap), cooldowns between high-value actions (Duolingo), price by verified effort not by count (Zwift watts), and bonus for the whole not the parts (Habitica's checklist bonus on the parent task; Todoist's goal bonus). For Orby: cap Orbs per day; pay for "the day's plan done" more than for each item; give habits created today no Orb value until tomorrow.
- **Self-report anti-gaming, for one honest user.** The risks are accidental, not adversarial: check, uncheck, re-check; bulk-completing a backlog; splitting one task into five. Rules that follow from the cases above: (1) award at a settle point (closing the day, or after a short grace period), not on the tap, so undo simply prevents the award; (2) if an award was already granted and the item is unchecked, reverse it (Habitica's choice not to reverse drops is acceptable there only because a daily cap bounds the damage); (3) only items that existed before today, or that are part of the plan, earn; (4) completions backdated more than a day do not earn or earn without multiplier; (5) prefer verified sources where they exist (workouts from Apple Health) for the larger awards, mirroring Zwift's "genuine efforts" fix.
- **Do not retroactively take Orbs away when a rule changes.** Zwift explicitly declined to revoke exploit gains; clawbacks damage trust more than inflation does. Version the rules going forward.
- **Do not reward bookkeeping.** Todoist pays for adding tasks and using features, which trains use of the tool and is trivially farmable. In a personal training app, pay for the behavior only.
- **One-screen rules.** Duolingo's lesson (XP-tied streaks confused people) and Zwift's secret formula (the community reverse-engineers it and finds the exploit anyway) both argue for a rule that fits in three lines: what earns an Orb, what the multiplier is right now and why, and what the daily cap is. Show the current multiplier and when it changes next to the counter.

### Gaps
- No source was found for Todoist Karma being exploited or rebalanced, or for the per-action Karma values (Todoist does not publish them).
- Habitica's exact drop cap numbers, drop chance formula, and any history of anti-farming changes were not retrieved (the wiki blocked fetching). From background knowledge, unverified: the base cap has been 5 item drops a day and 10 for subscribers.
- Fitbit's badges or any Fitbit points economy were not researched. Duolingo's gem economy (earn rates, sinks, any rebalance) was not found in credible sources.
- No GDC talk or established economy designer's text was read directly; the economy basics rest on Unity's guide and search summaries. Classic references (for example Machinations, Lost Garden, Deconstructor of Fun on currency) did not surface.
- No source was found on rounding and number legibility for currencies; the small-integer recommendation is inference.
- No research evidence was found on the best cap value for a streak multiplier; any number is a design choice to test.

## 5. Collectibles and long-term meaning, and App Store Review rules for random rewards and virtual currency

### Takeaway
A collected item keeps its value without money when it is scarce by effort, visibly accumulates, marks dated personal history, and unlocks cosmetic or status change at widening thresholds. On the App Store, an earned-only currency with no purchase path does not trigger the in-app purchase or loot box rules; those bite only when currency, items or randomized rewards are sold, and section 5.3 concerns sweepstakes, contests and real-money gaming, none of which a private Orb collection resembles as long as nothing of real-world value can be won.

### Cited Findings

**App Store Review Guidelines (page states "Last Updated: October 16, 2026")**
- **[Platform rule]** 3.1.1: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, game levels, access to premium content, or unlocking a full version), you must use in-app purchase. Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys, augmented reality markers, QR codes, cryptocurrencies and cryptocurrency wallets, etc." ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
- **[Platform rule]** 3.1.1: "Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire, and you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases." ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
- **[Platform rule]** 3.1.1: "Apps offering 'loot boxes' or other mechanisms that provide randomized virtual items for purchase must disclose the odds of receiving each type of item to customers prior to purchase." ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
- **[Platform rule]** 5.3 preamble: "Gaming, gambling, and lotteries can be tricky to manage and tend to be one of the most regulated offerings on the App Store. Only include this functionality if you've fully vetted your legal obligations everywhere you make your app available and are prepared for extra time during the review process." ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
- **[Platform rule]** 5.3.1: "Sweepstakes and contests must be sponsored by the developer of the app." 5.3.2: "Official rules for sweepstakes, contests, and raffles must be presented in the app and make clear that Apple is not a sponsor or involved in the activity in any manner." ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
- **[Platform rule]** 5.3.3: "Apps may not use in-app purchase to purchase credit or currency for use in conjunction with real money gaming of any kind." 5.3.4: real money gaming and lottery apps "must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where the app is used, must be geo-restricted to those locations, and must be free on the App Store... Lottery apps must have consideration, chance, and a prize." ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))
- **[Press]** Outside Apple's rules, regulators have treated chance-styled reward features as a problem in a financial context: the 2024 Massachusetts consent order with Robinhood cites "digital scratch tickets" and bars push notifications for features that "mimic games of chance". (search summary) ([WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/regulation-compliance/robinhood-pays-7-5m-to-settle-massachusetts-gamification-charge); [ThinkAdvisor](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/18/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-over-gamification-practices/))

**What gives a collected item lasting value without money (examples)**
- **[Platform rule for that product]** Levels with widening thresholds and one cosmetic prize at the top: Todoist's eight Karma levels end at Enlightened (50,000+), which "unlocks a mystery theme". ([Todoist Help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/introduction-to-karma-OgWkWy))
- **[Practitioner]** Spaced milestones with a transformed mascot and a shareable card: Duolingo's phoenix at one week, one month, 100 days, one year "and beyond"; learners can save or post the card without leaving the app. ([Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/streak-milestone-design-animation))
- **[Practitioner]** (search summary) Cosmetic unlocks tied to a creature that visibly changes: Finch's currency buys outfits and room decor, and progress evolves the pet and unlocks areas. ([ScreensDesign: Finch](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/finch-self-care-pet))
- **[Practitioner]** A rare variant tied to harder work: Asana's yeti appears under "the deepest, oldest, hardest-to-complete tasks", and Asana ran a contest for the first person to screenshot a celebration. ([Asana](https://asana.com/inside-asana/new-celebrations-meet-the-yeti))
- **[Press]** (search summary) Dated, limited awards: Apple's limited-edition award for 10 years of Activity rings (April 2025). ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/04/14/apple-celebrates-10-years-of-activity-rings))
- **[Practitioner]** A fixed lump-sum for a level: Zwift pays 50,000 Drops on each level-up, and the Drop Shop gives the currency a durable, visible use (equipment in the garage). ([Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/drops/))
- **[Practitioner]** Skin-matched reward visuals: in (Not Boring) Habits the burst takes the color of the user's selected skin, tying the reward to a personal cosmetic choice. ([Andy Allen](https://notbor.ing/words/the-most-satisfying-checkbox))
- **[Practitioner]** Thresholds should come quickly at first and then widen ("S-curve"); "save generous rewards for milestones". ([Unity](https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide))

### Inferences
- **Compliance position for an earned-only Orb.** Guideline 3.1.1's currency, expiry and loot box sentences all speak of things "purchased" or offered "for purchase". Orbs that can only be earned by activity, cannot be bought, and unlock nothing that is otherwise sold, sit outside those sentences. The moment Orbs or anything they unlock can be bought, in-app purchase is mandatory, purchased Orbs may not expire, and any randomized purchase must show odds before purchase.
- **Random rewards.** A surprise variant Orb (a rare color or pattern) awarded by chance on an earned event is a randomized virtual item that is not "for purchase", so the odds-disclosure sentence does not literally apply. Publishing the odds anyway on the rules screen costs nothing, protects against a later change of business model, and fits the "explain the rules in one screen" goal. Avoid slot machine, scratch card, wheel or raffle imagery and language; the Massachusetts order shows regulators single out features that "mimic games of chance", and App Review gives 5.3 "extra time".
- **Stay out of 5.3 entirely.** No prizes of real-world value, no entry mechanics, no drawings among users: then there is no sweepstakes, contest or lottery ("consideration, chance, and a prize") to regulate. Personal challenges in the app should pay only in Orbs or cosmetics.
- **Never let earned Orbs expire or decay silently.** The no-expiry rule covers purchased currency only, but it reflects the expectation Apple sets; a collection is also only meaningful if it is permanent.
- **What should give an Orb lasting value in Orby**, drawn from the examples: (1) each Orb is dated and carries what earned it, so the collection doubles as a record wall; (2) thresholds at widening counts change something visible (the collection's vessel, the Orb's finish, an app theme), early ones close together; (3) a few rare Orbs exist for rare things (a personal record, a 100-day streak, a year), earned not rolled; (4) a periodic look back (month, year) built from the same collection; (5) the reward moment's color follows the person's own choices. Items (1) and (4) rest on general pattern knowledge more than on sources found here.
- A single currency is enough. Unity notes that dual currencies exist to separate paid from free players; a personal app with no purchases has no reason for a second one.

### Gaps
- The first fetch of the guidelines did not surface text of 3.1.1 beyond what is quoted, and I did not check 2026 changes to 3.1.1 line by line against earlier versions. The quoted sentences are from the live page as fetched in November 2026.
- I did not research guideline 4.x items that could touch a reward system (for example incentivized ratings or notifications rules), nor privacy or health data rules; those belong to the launch-readiness research.
- No source was found, and none was sought in depth, for year-in-review features (for example Strava's or Spotify's) or personal record walls; their inclusion above is inference.
- No research evidence was found on which collectible mechanics sustain engagement beyond the novelty period in a single-user app without social comparison.
- Country-specific loot box law (for example Belgium, the Netherlands, or US state law) was out of scope and not checked; it matters only if random items are ever sold.
