# Habit formation, self-regulation and executive function: what the evidence says for streaks, rest days, comebacks and sprints

Verification key used on every finding below. **[V]** = checked against a search result or the fetched paper in this session (November 2026). **[R]** = cited from the published paper as known to the researcher, with its DOI, but not re-opened in this session; treat exact figures as needing a spot check before they appear in a public report. Evidence labels: **META** (meta-analysis), **FIELD** (large field experiment or megastudy), **RCT**, **LAB** (small lab study), **OBS** (observational or correlational), **CONTESTED** (failed or disputed replication).

## 1. Habit formation: goal-directed to habitual control, the time course, rewards, and missed days

### Takeaway
Habit strength grows with repetition in a stable context along a curve that rises fast then flattens, the time to plateau is counted in months for exercise and varies about tenfold between people, and a single missed day does not measurably set the process back. The animal neuroscience (dorsomedial to dorsolateral striatum) is solid but the standard human lab demonstration of "overtraining makes behavior habitual" has repeatedly failed to replicate, so an app should design from the field evidence (repetition, stable cues, low friction, a behavior that feels good at the time) and not from the brain story.

### Cited Findings

**Neuroscience and its replication problem**
- [R] Rodent and human reviews describe two controllers of instrumental action: a goal-directed (action-outcome) system depending on prefrontal cortex and dorsomedial striatum (caudate in humans) and a habit (stimulus-response) system depending on dorsolateral striatum (posterior putamen in humans); with overtraining, control shifts toward the second. Evidence is strong in rodents (lesion and inactivation studies), suggestive in humans. — [Balleine & O'Doherty 2010, Neuropsychopharmacology](https://doi.org/10.1038/npp.2009.131); [Yin & Knowlton 2006, Nat Rev Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1919); [Graybiel 2008, Annu Rev Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.neuro.29.051605.112851)
- [R] LAB. The one widely cited human demonstration that overtraining produces devaluation-insensitive responding with rising posterior putamen activity used a small fMRI sample. — [Tricomi, Balleine & O'Doherty 2009, Eur J Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-9568.2009.06796.x)
- [V] CONTESTED. de Wit et al. (2018, J Exp Psychol Gen 147(7):1043-1065) reported five failed attempts to show that overtraining makes human instrumental behavior habitual, across two avoidance tasks, an appetitive slips-of-action task and two independent replications of the Tricomi design. Extensive training did not increase habits in any of them, and the authors question whether outcome-devaluation tasks are sensitive to training duration in humans at all. — [de Wit et al. 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6033090)
- [V] CONTESTED. A later preregistered multi-laboratory study (Pool, Gera et al. 2022, Learning & Memory) found no statistical evidence for a main effect of training amount on habit expression; exploratory analysis suggested people high in affective stress or anxiety were already habitual after moderate training while low-stress people became habitual with more training. — [Pool et al. 2022 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8686594)

**Context-dependent repetition and automaticity (real-world habits)**
- [R] Wood and Neal define habits as context-response associations learned through repetition; once formed, the context cues the response directly and goals matter less. Diary studies put roughly 43 to 45% of daily actions as performed in the same place almost daily, usually while thinking about something else (OBS, student samples). — [Wood & Neal 2007, Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.114.4.843); [Wood, Quinn & Kashy 2002, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.6.1281); [Wood & Rünger 2016, Annu Rev Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417)
- [R] META. Self-reported habit strength correlates with behavior at about r = .45 for nutrition and physical activity (cross-sectional; inflated by shared method). — [Gardner, de Bruijn & Lally 2011, Ann Behav Med](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12160-011-9282-0)
- [R] OBS. For exercise, the habit that predicts frequency is the habit of *instigating* (automatically deciding to start), not the habit of *executing* the routine. Instigation habit strength predicted exercise frequency over a month; execution habit did not. — [Phillips & Gardner 2016, Health Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1037/hea0000249)

**The time course: what "66 days" really shows**
- [V] Lally et al. (2010, Eur J Soc Psychol 40:998-1009). 96 volunteers chose one eating, drinking or activity behavior to do daily after a chosen cue, and rated automaticity each day for 12 weeks (84 days). 82 finished; the asymptotic curve fitted well for only 39. For those 39, the median time to reach 95% of each person's own plateau was 66 days, range 18 to a model-extrapolated 254 days (beyond the 84 days observed). The figure is a median for a self-selected subset doing mostly simple behaviors, not a rule. — [Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674); [BPS Research Digest summary](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit); [close reading of the sample and curve fitting](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit)
- [V] Same paper, abstract: "missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process." Early repetitions produced the largest gains in automaticity; gains shrank as the plateau approached. — [Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)
- [V] RCT. Keller et al. (2021, Br J Health Psychol 26:807-824). 192 adults aged 18 to 77 picked a nutrition behavior and linked it to either a daily routine cue or a clock-time cue, with daily reports for 84 days. Automaticity rose in both arms with no between-arm difference; repeated enactment of the plan was the key predictor; among people who did form the habit, median time to peak automaticity was 59 days. — [Keller et al. 2021 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33405284/)
- [V] FIELD/OBS with machine learning. Buyalskaya et al. (2023, PNAS). 30,110 gym members (over 12 million check-ins, median 1,525 observed days per person) and 3,124 hospital workers (over 40 million handwashing opportunities). Habit was defined as context-predictability of behavior (LASSO out-of-sample AUC), with formation time = when the fitted curve reached 95% of its asymptote; automaticity was not measured directly. Gym habits took a median of 122 to 226 days (about 4 to 7 months); handwashing took about 9 to 10 shifts (roughly 220 to 250 opportunities, a couple of weeks). The authors conclude there is no magic number. — [Buyalskaya et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500/)
- [V] Same study, predictors. For gym visits the strongest predictor was time since last visit (a negative predictor for 76% of members: the longer the gap, the less likely the next visit), then a same-day-of-week streak (positive for 69%), and Monday or Tuesday attendance (positive for 57%). For handwashing, compliance on the previous shift was positive for 100% of workers. — [Buyalskaya et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500/)
- [V] Same study. More predictable (more habitual) gym-goers responded less to the StepUp incentive program (P < .001), and across the megastudy more habitual attendees responded less to interventions (r = -.48). — [Buyalskaya et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500/)
- [V] META. Singh et al. (2024, Healthcare 12(23):2488) reviewed 20 studies of health habit formation. Only four reported time to formation: medians 59 to 66 days, means 106 to 154 days, individual range 4 to 335 days. Pooled pre-post change in habit score was SMD 0.69. Secondary coverage reports that 11 of the 20 studies were at high risk of bias and that timing data were too heterogeneous to pool. The abstract names frequency, timing, type of habit, individual choice, affective judgments, behavioral regulation and preparatory habits as determinants, with morning practices and self-selected habits tending to be stronger. — [Singh et al. 2024, Healthcare](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/12/23/2488); [University of Adelaide record](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/fe6ba93e-1d6b-42b8-9ee7-a231a64ff006); [secondary coverage with bias ratings](https://siliconcanals.com/t-habit-systematic-review-59-66-335-days/)
- [R] OBS. New gym members who exercised at least four times a week for about six weeks were the ones who established an exercise habit; consistency, low behavioral complexity, a supportive environment and positive affect predicted habit (N = 111, 12 weeks). — [Kaushal & Rhodes 2015, J Behav Med](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10865-015-9640-7)
- [R] Methods guidance from the UCL group: track habit formation with repeated automaticity measures, expect an asymptotic curve, and treat "days to habit" as person- and behavior-specific. — [Gardner, Rebar & Lally 2022, Cogent Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1080/23311908.2022.2041277)

**Are rewards needed?**
- [V] OBS (exploratory). Judah, Gardner, Kenward et al. (2018, BMC Psychology), 118 adults, vitamin C tablets and flossing. Pleasure and intrinsic motivation *moderated* the repetition-to-habit link: each repetition added more habit strength when the behavior felt pleasant or intrinsically motivated. Reward did not act simply by increasing repetition. — [Judah et al. 2018 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6302524)
- [R] OBS. Same pattern for physical activity: intrinsic motivation strengthened the relation between repetition and habit (N = 192), and intrinsic rewards predicted exercise through intentions for beginners but through habit strength for maintainers. — [Gardner & Lally 2013, J Behav Med](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10865-012-9442-0); [Phillips et al. 2016, Sport Exerc Perform Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1037/spy0000071)
- [R] Review position (Wood & Neal): rewards build habit associations best when they are experienced during or immediately after the action and are part of the activity itself; delayed external incentives mainly buy repetitions while they last. — [Wood & Neal 2016, Behavioral Science & Policy](https://doi.org/10.1353/bsp.2016.0008)
- [V] FIELD. Paying for exercise produces behavior while the payment lasts and little after: in the 24 Hour Fitness megastudy only 8% of 53 four-week programs produced a detectable increase in the four weeks after they ended (2.5% expected by chance). — [Milkman et al. 2021, Nature (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/)
- [V] FIELD, counter to the simple habit story. Beshears, Lee, Milkman, Mislavsky & Wisdom (2021, Management Science 67:4139-4171), 2,508 Google employees. Paying people only when they exercised in a fixed, self-planned daily two-hour window ("routine incentives") produced *fewer* gym visits than paying for exercise at any time, both during the program and after it ended, and the routine group fell off more afterwards even when matched on in-program frequency. — [Beshears et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3706); [HBS Working Knowledge summary](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/unexpected-exercise-advice-for-the-super-busy-ditch-the-rigid-routine)

### Inferences
- "Days in a row" is a poor proxy for habit strength. The evidence-based quantities are *number of repetitions in a consistent context* and *short gaps between repetitions*. A streak that tolerates a single miss is more faithful to Lally's data than one that resets to zero, and Buyalskaya's "time since last visit" result says the thing to protect is the gap not growing, not the unbroken chain.
- For running and strength, expect months, not weeks, before turning up feels automatic. A 21- or 30-day challenge can start a habit but should not be presented as forming one. Sprints are a way to stack repetitions early, when each repetition adds the most automaticity.
- A training plan with scheduled rest already breaks the "every day" pattern. The matching unit is "did what the plan said today" (a rest day counts) or a weekly count, with same-weekday consistency (Buyalskaya's day-of-week streak) as the natural rhythm.
- The reward that helps habit formation is the immediate felt one (the moment of checking off, a pleasant session), so the app's reward moment should sit at the instant of completion and be attached to the act. Points redeemed later are an incentive, not a habit builder.
- As behavior becomes habitual, prompts and incentives matter less (Buyalskaya). Rewards and challenges can taper for established behaviors and concentrate on new or wobbling ones.
- Beshears warns against strict time-window streak rules for busy adults: flexible "any time today" credit outperformed "only at your planned time".

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures automaticity under different streak rules (strict reset vs forgiving), so the "one miss is harmless" claim rests on Lally's small sample plus indirect field data.
- The Lally 2010 study has not been directly replicated at scale; a registered replication was in preparation according to a Peer Community In Registered Reports preprint, outcome not found.
- The MDPI page for Singh et al. returned a 403 in this session; participant total and the determinant list are from the abstract as indexed and secondary coverage.
- Human neuroimaging evidence for a dorsomedial-to-dorsolateral shift during real-world habit formation (months of daily behavior) does not exist; the lab tasks last hours to days.

## 2. Cues and context: implementation intentions, habit stacking, event versus time cues, and when a prompt should appear

### Takeaway
If-then plans that tie an action to a specific cue are one of the most replicated tools in behavior science, but the honest effect size is small to moderate (bias-corrected estimates are well below the famous d = 0.65), plans stop helping when people make many at once, and large field trials of bare planning prompts for exercise have come up empty. Routine-based and clock-time cues build automaticity equally well when the plan is actually enacted, so the app should let the person pick the anchor and make enactment easy, and deliver prompts at the anchor moment.

### Cited Findings
- [R] META. Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006): 94 independent tests, d = 0.65 for goal attainment from forming implementation intentions. Mostly small lab and student studies, short follow-ups. — [Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, Adv Exp Soc Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1)
- [V] META. Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (2024, Eur Rev Soc Psychol): 642 independent tests; effects on cognitive, affective and behavioral outcomes ranged .27 ≤ d ≤ .66. Per the abstract, effects were larger when plans used a contingent if-then format, when people were highly motivated toward the goal, and when plans were rehearsed. The paper adds a taxonomy of cues (time-and-place, task juncture) and responses. — [Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563)
- [V] CONTESTED (secondary report, verify before quoting). A summary of the same paper reports bias-corrected estimates of d = 0.35 (trim-and-fill) and d = 0.15, 95% CI 0.08 to 0.22 (robust Bayesian meta-analysis). — [Goals and Progress summary](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-research/)
- [R] FIELD. Prompting employees to write the date and time they would get a flu shot raised vaccination from 33.1% to 37.1% (N = 3,272; +4.2 points); prompting only the date had a smaller, non-significant effect. — [Milkman, Beshears, Choi, Laibson & Madrian 2011, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1103170108)
- [R] FIELD, null. A planning prompt asking 877 gym members to schedule their workouts had no effect on gym attendance, despite people making plans and intending to follow them. — [Carrera, Royer, Stehr, Sydnor & Taubinsky 2018, J Health Econ](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhealeco.2018.09.002)
- [V] FIELD. In the 24 Hour Fitness megastudy the baseline package of planning, text reminders before scheduled workouts and micro-incentives (about US$0.22 per visit) raised weekly visits by about 0.14 (9%) over a placebo control during four weeks; N = 61,293 across 54 conditions. Planning was not tested alone. — [Milkman et al. 2021, Nature (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/)
- [V] RCT. Routine-based ("after breakfast") and time-based ("at 8:00") cue plans produced the same gains in automaticity and enactment over 84 days (N = 192). What mattered was how often the plan was carried out in response to its cue. — [Keller et al. 2021 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33405284/)
- [V] In Lally's design every participant anchored the behavior to a self-chosen daily event ("after breakfast"), which is the empirical root of "habit stacking". The popular technique itself has no dedicated trials that were found. — [Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)
- [V] LAB plus FIELD. "Reminders through association": tying an intention to a distinctive cue that will be present at the moment of action raised follow-through (74% vs 42% in one study; six experiments in all). Cues worked better when distinctive relative to the surroundings, and people undervalued them. — [Rogers & Milkman 2016, Psychol Sci (APS summary)](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/hacking-memory-to-follow-through-with-intentions.html)
- [R] LAB/FIELD. Plans help with one goal and stop helping with many: implementation intentions improved follow-through for a single goal but not when people formed them for six goals, because many plans highlight how hard it all is and dilute commitment. Forming multiple plans against snacking was likewise less effective than forming one. — [Dalton & Spiller 2012, J Consum Res](https://doi.org/10.1086/664500); [Verhoeven, Adriaanse, de Ridder, de Vet & Fennis 2013, Eur J Soc Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.1963)
- [V] Context stability. In the Singh review and in Kaushal & Rhodes, consistency of context and timing predicted habit strength; morning practice tended to form stronger habits. — [Singh et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/12/23/2488); [Kaushal & Rhodes 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10865-015-9640-7)
- [V] But rigid timing has a cost: see Beshears et al. 2021 above, where rewarding only a fixed time window reduced total exercise relative to flexible credit. — [Beshears et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3706)

### Inferences
- A daily challenge should carry one if-then anchor chosen by the person ("After my morning coffee, I do X"), shown at setup and rehearsed once. The plan format, motivation and rehearsal are the three moderators in the 2024 meta-analysis.
- Cap concurrent challenges. One at a time is the evidence-based default (Dalton & Spiller); a weekly challenge plus one daily sprint is already near the limit.
- Reminders should fire at the anchor moment the person named (event or time), since enactment in response to the cue is what builds automaticity. A reminder at an arbitrary time is a nag, not a cue.
- Credit should be flexible ("done today") even when the cue is fixed. Fixed cue to start, flexible window to count.
- Expect planning prompts alone to do little for workouts (Carrera 2018). Their value is as a carrier for reminders and for the comeback mechanics in section 3.

### Gaps
- No trial of "habit stacking" as a named technique was found; support is by analogy with routine-based cue planning.
- Event-based versus time-based *notifications* in apps (as opposed to self-set plans) were not compared in any study found here.
- The bias-corrected estimates for Sheeran et al. 2024 come from a secondary summary; the primary paper was not opened.

## 3. Self-regulation after a lapse: what-the-hell effects, self-compassion, goal adjustment, and restarting

### Takeaway
A lapse is most dangerous when the goal is framed as an unbroken record and the person blames themselves; under those conditions people stop for longer than the lapse itself warrants. The best-supported countermeasures are structural, not motivational: build priced slack into the goal, make the record repairable, make the first return after a miss the rewarded act, and frame the miss as outside the self or as part of the plan.

### Cited Findings

**The lapse-to-collapse pattern**
- [R] Marlatt and Gordon's relapse model: after a lapse from an absolute rule, an internal, stable, global attribution ("I have no willpower") plus guilt raises the chance of full relapse (the abstinence violation effect). Developed in addiction; the attribution pattern was validated in smoking cessation samples. Clinical populations, so transfer to everyday habits is by analogy. — [Curry, Marlatt & Gordon 1987, J Consult Clin Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-006X.55.2.145)
- [R] LAB. The dieting counterpart: restrained eaters who believe they have broken their diet eat more afterwards (counterregulation, later nicknamed the "what the hell" effect). Example: dieters led to believe their pizza slice was larger than others' ate more cookies afterwards. Small lab samples, mostly female students. — [Polivy & Herman 1985, Am Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.40.2.193); [Polivy, Herman & Deo 2010, Appetite](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appet.2010.07.015)
- [R] CONTESTED. In daily life the effect is not reliably seen: an electronic diary study found restrained eaters did not eat more after diet violations outside the lab. — [Tomiyama, Moskovich, Haltom, Ju & Mann 2009, Psychol Sci](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02436.x)
- [V] LAB (seven studies), directly about streaks. Silverman & Barasch (2023, J Consum Res 49:1095-1117): people shown an intact streak in a behavior log were more likely to continue the behavior than people shown a broken one, holding actual past behavior constant, because the logged streak becomes a goal of its own. The drop after a break was larger when people blamed themselves and smaller when the break was attributed to something external or when the streak could be repaired. — [Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029); [University of Colorado summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)
- [V] FIELD. Time since the last gym visit was the strongest negative predictor of the next one for 76% of 30,110 members: gaps feed on themselves. — [Buyalskaya et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500/)
- [R] OBS. Dichotomous (all-or-nothing) thinking was the strongest psychological predictor of weight regain after weight loss in a prospective study of people with obesity. Clinical sample. — [Byrne, Cooper & Fairburn 2004, Behav Res Ther](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2003.10.004)

**Self-compassion and self-forgiveness**
- [V] LAB (four experiments, student samples). Breines & Chen (2012, PSPB): inducing self-compassion after a failure or weakness increased motivation to improve, including more time spent studying for a retest after an initial failure (Scientific American reports 33% more study time than a self-esteem condition and 51% more than a neutral control; secondary figure). — [Breines & Chen 2012 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22645164/); [Scientific American summary](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-self-compassion-solution/)
- [V] LAB (female undergraduates). Adams & Leary (2007, J Soc Clin Psychol 26:1120-1144): after an unhealthy food preload, a brief self-compassion induction reduced distress and attenuated subsequent eating among highly restrictive eaters, i.e. it blunted the what-the-hell response. Single small study; no direct replication was found. — [Adams & Leary 2007 (Duke record)](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/782823)
- [R] OBS. Students who forgave themselves for procrastinating before a first exam procrastinated less before the second (N = 119), mediated by reduced negative affect. — [Wohl, Pychyl & Bennett 2010, Pers Individ Dif](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2010.01.029)
- [R] META (correlational). Trait self-compassion is associated with more health-promoting behavior: r = .25 across 15 samples (N = 3,252). A later, larger meta-analysis reports similar small-to-moderate associations with health behavior. Cross-sectional; says nothing about a one-line message in an app. — [Sirois, Kitner & Hirsch 2015, Health Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1037/hea0000158); [Phillips & Hine 2021, Health Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2019.1705872)

**Goal disengagement and re-engagement**
- [R] Wrosch's model: when a goal has become unattainable, two separate capacities matter, letting go (withdrawing effort and commitment) and re-engaging (finding and committing to a new goal). — [Wrosch, Scheier, Miller, Schulz & Carver 2003, PSPB](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167203256921)
- [V] META. Barlow, Wrosch & McGrath (2020, J Pers 88): 31 samples. Goal disengagement related to fewer negative well-being indicators (r = -.12) but not to positive ones (r = .02); goal re-engagement related to both (positive r = .24, negative r = -.17). Disengagement's link to lower depressive symptoms (r = -.11) reversed in samples at risk for depression (r = .08). — [Barlow, Wrosch & McGrath 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/jopy.12492)
- [V] META. A 2025 Nature Human Behaviour meta-analysis of goal adjustment (Riddell et al.) pooled 1,421 effect sizes from 235 studies; exact pooled values not retrieved. — [Riddell et al. 2025 (Southampton repository)](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/506833/)

**Structural ways to help people restart**
- [V] FIELD. The single best of 53 programs in the 24 Hour Fitness megastudy (N = 61,293) paid a tiny bonus (125 points, about US$0.09) for coming back to the next planned workout after a missed one. It added about 0.40 visits a week over placebo, a 27% increase, versus 9% for planning, reminders and micro-incentives alone. Milkman's reading: the money is trivial; the message "do not miss twice" is the active ingredient. — [Milkman et al. 2021, Nature (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/); [Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/wharton-study-best-ways-boost-workout-habits)
- [V] FIELD plus LAB. "Emergency reserves" (Sharif & Shu 2017, J Mark Res 54:495-509): a hard goal with a small number of explicitly labeled skip passes that feel costly to use (for example "7 days a week, with 2 emergency skips") beat both the same hard goal with no slack and an easier goal with the slack built in ("5 days a week"). In a step-count field experiment people with reserves took up to 20% more steps and hit their daily goal on up to 40% more days. People resist using the reserve, so they try harder, and when they do use it they do not read it as failure. — [Sharif & Shu 2017](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.15.0231); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/)
- [V] LAB/FIELD. Follow-up (Sharif & Shu 2021, OBHDP 163:17-29): goals with emergency reserves kept people going after a subgoal failure and improved long-term goal performance. — [Sharif & Shu 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jobhdp:v:163:y:2021:i:c:p:17-29)
- [V] LAB (two experiments plus a survey). Planned deviations: a goal plan that includes scheduled days off (simulated and real dieting; 59 students in the first study per the BPS digest) preserved self-regulatory capacity and motivation as well as or better than a rigid plan, with equal weight loss. — [Coelho do Vale, Pieters & Zeelenberg 2016, J Consum Psychol](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/the-benefits-of-behaving-badly-on-occasion-successful-regulation-/); [BPS Research Digest](https://digest.bps.org.uk/2016/04/18/good-news-planning-naughty-lapses-can-help-you-achieve-your-goals)
- [V] LAB plus archival. Resets cut both ways (Dai 2018, OBHDP 148:12-29): wiping the performance record motivated people whose recent record was weak and demotivated those whose record was strong. In one experiment fewer than 40% of strong performers chose to continue after a reset. — [Dai 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v148y2018icp12-29.html); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://www.anderson-review.ucla.edu/reset/)
- [R] Review. Theories of maintenance converge on five themes: maintenance motives (enjoyment, identity), self-regulation (coping plans for lapses), habit, resources, and a supportive environment. Planning in advance for obstacles is the lapse-specific technique. — [Kwasnicka, Dombrowski, White & Sniehotta 2016, Health Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2016.1151372)

### Inferences
- Design the miss before the person has one. A streak that silently drops to zero creates the exact conditions (absolute rule, visible break, self-blame) that the lab studies show reduce continuation.
- A small number of *priced* passes beats both a strict streak and a lenient one. Sharif & Shu's point is that the slack must be explicit, limited and feel like a cost. That maps onto a few "rest passes" per sprint that the person spends, not onto an unlimited freeze. An easier rule ("5 of 7 days") is the weaker option.
- The comeback should be the most rewarded moment in the system. The megastudy's top result was a bonus for the first return after a miss. A "comeback" reward on the first check-in after a gap, sized above an ordinary day, is directly supported. Keep it honest: the bonus is for returning, not for missing.
- Offer repair, soon and bounded. Silverman & Barasch found that the chance to repair a broken streak removes most of the damage. Do tomorrow's session or a make-up action within a day or two and the streak stands.
- Never reset a strong record. Dai's result says keep lifetime totals, best streak and total days visible after a break, and offer the "fresh start" framing only to the current run.
- Word the miss as external or planned ("Life got in the way" or "Rest is in the plan"), never as a verdict on the person. Attribution is the moderator in both the relapse literature and the streak studies.
- Keep the gap short. Because time since last session predicts the next, the comeback prompt belongs on day one or two after a miss, with a smaller-than-usual ask (see section 4).
- For a goal that has stopped fitting (injury, a new job), make swapping or pausing it a first-class, blameless action. Re-engagement with a new goal carries the well-being benefit, more than disengagement alone.

### Gaps
- No experiment was found that tests self-compassionate wording inside an app after a missed day; the self-compassion evidence is small lab inductions plus trait correlations.
- The what-the-hell effect is contested outside the lab, and no study quantifies it for exercise or to-do behavior specifically.
- The search for a systematic review of the abstinence violation effect returned only low-quality pages; the AVE findings above are from the primary papers as recalled.
- Emergency-reserve effects ("up to 20%", "up to 40%") are best-case contrasts; average effects and the long-run durability were not retrieved.

## 4. Executive function and time: forgetting, time perception, landmarks, getting started, and overload

### Takeaway
Many missed intentions are failures of remembering, timing or starting, not of motivation: people forget at the moment of action, underestimate how long things take, and stall on aversive or vague tasks. The reliable fixes are external (a cue present at the right moment, a concrete next action small enough to begin, fewer simultaneous goals), and temporal landmarks give a real but modest and double-edged boost to starting.

### Cited Findings

**Prospective memory**
- [R] Remembering to act later depends on either spontaneous retrieval triggered by a cue or effortful monitoring. Time-based intentions ("at 6 pm") require self-initiated monitoring and are more fragile under load than event-based intentions tied to a salient cue. — [McDaniel & Einstein 2000, Appl Cogn Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.775)
- [V] LAB plus FIELD. Follow-through fails through forgetting, not only weak self-control; a distinctive cue at the point of action raised it from 42% to 74% in one study, and people undervalue such cues. — [Rogers & Milkman 2016 (APS summary)](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/hacking-memory-to-follow-through-with-intentions.html)
- [R] Review. People readily offload intentions to external reminders, more so when they doubt their memory, and offloading improves fulfilment of intentions, with the cost that the offloaded item is remembered less well without the aid. — [Gilbert, Boldt, Sachdeva, Scarampi & Tsai 2023, Psychon Bull Rev](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-022-02139-4)
- [R] FIELD. Simple reminder messages have real but small effects at scale: in a 47,306-patient megastudy of 19 text nudges before a doctor visit, reminders raised flu vaccination by an average of 2.1 percentage points (about 5%), and the best ("a vaccine is reserved for you") by 4.6 points (11%). — [Milkman et al. 2021, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2101165118)

**Time perception and the planning fallacy**
- [R] LAB. People underestimate their own completion times even when they know past tasks ran late: students predicted 33.9 days to finish a thesis and took 55.5, and fewer than a third finished by their own estimate. — [Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.67.3.366)
- [R] LAB. Breaking a task into its components before estimating ("unpacking") reduces the underestimate, more so for complex tasks. — [Kruger & Evans 2004, J Exp Soc Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2003.11.001)
- [V] META. Timing differs between people: a 2022 meta-analysis of 55 studies found a broad range of perceptual timing deficits in people with ADHD, with small-to-medium effects for time estimation. A 2023 review of adult studies concludes time perception is impaired in adult ADHD but that the adult literature is small and methods inconsistent. — [Marx et al. 2022, J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry (Southampton repository)](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/456003); [Mette 2023, Int J Environ Res Public Health (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9962130/)

**Temporal landmarks**
- [R] ARCHIVAL/FIELD. Dai, Milkman & Riis (2014, Management Science 60:2563-2582): gym visits by university students rose at the start of the week (33.4%), month (14.4%), year (11.6%) and semester (47.1%) and after birthdays (7.5%); "diet" searches and commitment contracts showed the same pattern. Archival, not randomized. — [Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)
- [V] FIELD. Fresh-start framing replicated in a randomized mailing to 6,082 university employees: inviting people to raise retirement saving at a fresh-start date (a birthday, the first day of spring) increased contributions over the next eight months relative to a neutral future date, though it also lowered the share who chose to start immediately. — [Beshears, Dai, Milkman & Benartzi 2021, OBHDP](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jobhdp:v:167:y:2021:i:c:p:72-87); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/fresh-start-framing-boosts-retirement-plan-participation/)
- [V] LAB plus archival. Fresh starts hurt people who were doing well (Dai 2018, above). — [Dai 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v148y2018icp12-29.html)
- [V] FIELD. Monday and Tuesday attendance predicted gym-going for 57% of members: the week's start carries the week. — [Buyalskaya et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500/)

**Getting started and small first steps**
- [R] META. Steel (2007, Psychol Bull 133:65-94; 691 correlations): the strongest correlates of procrastination are task aversiveness, task delay (distant deadlines and rewards), low self-efficacy and impulsiveness, plus low conscientiousness and its facets (self-control, distractibility, organization). Procrastination is a starting problem tied to how unpleasant and how distant the payoff is. — [Steel 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.133.1.65)
- [R] META, clinical. Behavioral activation, which schedules small, concrete, graded activities to get action going before motivation returns, has a large effect on depression against controls (16 studies, 780 participants, d = 0.87). Clinical population and outcome; the transferable principle is "act first, in small scheduled steps". — [Cuijpers, van Straten & Warmerdam 2007, Clin Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2006.11.001)
- [R] Review. Foot-in-the-door (agreeing to a small request raises agreement to a larger one) is real but small and conditional; it is strongest when the first act is actually performed, is attributed to the self, and the second request continues the first. It concerns compliance with someone else's request, so applying it to self-initiated habits is an extrapolation. — [Burger 1999, Pers Soc Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr0304_2)
- [V] Simpler behaviors become automatic faster (weeks for handwashing, months for the gym), and in Lally's data the earliest repetitions add the most automaticity. — [Buyalskaya et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500/); [Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)
- [R] OBS. Instigation habit (automatically starting) is what predicts exercise frequency. — [Phillips & Gardner 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/hea0000249)

**Cognitive load from choices and goals**
- [V] META, CONTESTED. Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd (2010): 63 conditions from 50 experiments (N = 5,036), mean choice-overload effect "virtually zero" with large variance between studies. Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman (2015): 99 observations (N = 7,202), overload appears reliably when the choice set is complex, the task difficult, preferences uncertain, and the person wants to minimize effort. A 2018 reanalysis found the effect reverses for some outcomes at low moderator levels, and a p-curve analysis questioned the evidential value of the positive studies. — [Scheibehenne et al. 2010, J Consum Res](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:v:37:y:2010:i:3:p:409-425); [Chernev et al. 2015, J Consum Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002); [review of the debate](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.800528)
- [R] LAB/FIELD. Too many goals: planning for six goals at once removed the benefit of planning that appeared for one goal. — [Dalton & Spiller 2012](https://doi.org/10.1086/664500)

### Inferences
- Treat the reminder as a memory aid placed at the moment of action. A notification tied to the person's own anchor, or a persistent visual cue on the Today screen, addresses forgetting; it should not be written as a motivational push.
- Show honest durations and pad them. Estimates for a session or a to-do should be unpacked into steps, and "running late" should not break a challenge that was done.
- Every challenge needs a visible "smallest version" that still counts (ten minutes, one set, one line). The evidence is indirect (behavioral activation, procrastination correlates, simpler behaviors automating faster, instigation habit), but all of it points the same way: the app should reward starting.
- Use Monday, the first of the month and the day after a break as natural sprint start dates, and offer them. Do not impose a reset on someone mid-run.
- Offer two or three pre-built challenges, not a catalog, and default to one active. The choice-overload effect is unreliable on average but appears exactly when people are unsure what they want and want to save effort, which describes a person opening a challenges screen.

### Gaps
- No controlled trial of "tiny habits" or minimum-viable-effort framing with behavioral outcomes was found; this is the weakest-evidenced popular idea in the set.
- "Time blindness" is a lay term; no study using it as a construct was found. The adult ADHD timing literature is small.
- Event-based versus time-based prospective memory was not compared in any source opened this session; the claim rests on the standard framework paper.
- Whether fresh-start framing helps a *restart after a lapse* specifically (as opposed to an initial start) was not tested in the studies found.

## 5. Individual differences a general app should respect without diagnosing anyone

### Takeaway
People differ reliably in how steeply they discount delayed rewards, how much they lean on immediate feedback, how they react to an imperfect record, and how much structure they already bring. The design that serves both ends is the same: immediate, certain, small feedback for the act itself; forgiving rules with visible limits; optional layers (points, challenges, reminders) that a self-directed person can turn down or off; and no penalty language.

### Cited Findings

**Reward sensitivity and delay aversion**
- [V] META. People with ADHD discount delayed rewards more steeply than controls: d = 0.43 across 25 case-control comparisons (N = 3,913), with no significant moderation by age, reward type or comorbidity. — [Jackson & MacKillop 2016, Biol Psychiatry Cogn Neurosci Neuroimaging (PubMed)](https://www.pubmed.gov/27722208/)
- [V] META. A second meta-analysis (37 group comparisons, 3,763 participants) found small-to-medium effects on both simple choice-delay tasks and temporal discounting tasks; the group difference on choice-delay tasks almost doubled when rewards were real, not hypothetical. — [Marx, Hacker, Yu, Cortese & Sonuga-Barke 2021, J Atten Disord](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054718772138)
- [R] Theory. Sonuga-Barke's delay-aversion account treats the preference for immediate reward as partly a motivated escape from the experience of waiting, alongside separate inhibitory and timing pathways; the three dissociate in children with ADHD. — [Sonuga-Barke, Bitsakou & Thompson 2010, J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2009.12.018)
- [V] Review. Luman, Oosterlaan & Sergeant (2005, Clin Psychol Rev 25:183-213): 22 studies, 1,181 children. Reinforcement improved task performance and motivation in both ADHD and control groups, with a somewhat larger effect in ADHD; psychophysiological data suggested lower sensitivity to reinforcement, which fits a need for stronger or more immediate reinforcers. Children only. — [Luman, Oosterlaan & Sergeant 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2004.11.001)
- [R] META (neuroimaging). Reduced ventral striatal response during reward anticipation in ADHD, medium effect size. — [Plichta & Scheres 2014, Neurosci Biobehav Rev](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2013.07.012)
- [V] Gamification evidence for ADHD is thin and mostly pediatric. A 2025 systematic review of 22 ADHD serious games (37 studies) found symptom changes "range widely without an obvious pattern of advantage for any game element"; a scoping review of 35 commercial ADHD apps found levels and statistics the most common elements and only two apps with medical endorsement. — [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2025 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1605744/full); [Behaviour & Information Technology 2025 scoping review](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/tbitxx/v44y2025i16p4037-4064.html)
- [V] Timing differences: see section 4 (Marx et al. 2022; Mette 2023). — [Mette 2023 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9962130/)
- [V] In the general population, less habitual (less predictable) gym-goers were the ones who responded to incentives; habitual ones barely did. — [Buyalskaya et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500/)

**Perfectionism and all-or-nothing thinking**
- [R] META. Perfectionistic concerns (fear of mistakes, self-criticism) relate to more procrastination (about r = .23) and perfectionistic strivings to less (about r = -.22), across 43 studies. — [Sirois, Molnar & Hirsch 2017, Eur J Pers](https://doi.org/10.1002/per.2098)
- [R] META (cohort). Self-oriented, socially prescribed and other-oriented perfectionism rose among college students from 1989 to 2016 (164 samples, N = 41,641). — [Curran & Hill 2019, Psychol Bull](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000138)
- [R] OBS. Dichotomous thinking predicted weight regain. — [Byrne, Cooper & Fairburn 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2003.10.004)
- [V] LAB. A broken streak demotivated most when the person blamed themselves. — [Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)
- [V] Exercise dependence risk is estimated at roughly 3 to 9% of regular exercisers depending on the instrument (8.1% of general exercisers in a 2020 meta-analysis that excluded indicated eating disorders, with wide confidence intervals), and is 3.7 times more likely where an eating disorder is indicated. — [Marques et al. systematic review](https://www.springermedizin.de/prevalence-of-risk-for-exercise-dependence-a-systematic-review/16234606); [Trott et al. 2020 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32496431/)

**Conscientiousness and trait self-control**
- [V] OBS (six studies). People high in self-control do well mainly because they have stronger beneficial habits, not because they resist more temptation: habits mediated the link between self-control and exercise, sleep, study and meditation outcomes, including GPA and college persistence. — [Galla & Duckworth 2015, JPSP (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4731333)
- [R] META. Trait self-control relates more strongly to automatic, habitual behaviors than to deliberate ones (102 studies, N = 32,648). Experience sampling shows high self-control people report fewer temptations and conflicts in the first place. — [de Ridder et al. 2012, Pers Soc Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868311418749); [Hofmann, Baumeister, Förster & Vohs 2012, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026545)
- [V] OBS, 90-day field study. Self-control capacity did not speed up the habit formation curve itself, a mixed result against the simple "disciplined people form habits faster" reading. — [van der Weiden, Benjamins, Gillebaart, Ybema & de Ridder 2020, Front Psychol](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00560/full)
- [R] META. Conscientiousness has a small positive association with physical activity (about r = .10). — [Wilson & Dishman 2015, Pers Individ Dif](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2014.08.023)

**Intrinsic versus extrinsic orientation**
- [R] META. Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999, 128 studies): expected tangible rewards reduced free-choice intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks (engagement-contingent d = -0.40, completion-contingent d = -0.36, performance-contingent d = -0.28); unexpected rewards did not; positive feedback raised it (d = 0.33 free-choice, 0.31 self-reported interest). — [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999, Psychol Bull](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627); [authors' 2001 summary](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf)
- [R] META. Intrinsic motivation and incentives both predict performance and can coexist; intrinsic motivation matters more for quality and incentives for quantity. — [Cerasoli, Nicklin & Ford 2014, Psychol Bull](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035661)
- [R] Systematic review (66 studies). For exercise, identified regulation (valuing the outcome) predicts early adoption and intrinsic motivation (enjoyment) predicts long-term adherence. — [Teixeira, Carraça, Markland, Silva & Ryan 2012, Int J Behav Nutr Phys Act](https://doi.org/10.1186/1479-5868-9-78)

### Inferences
- Immediate, certain, small feedback at the moment of completion serves people at the steep-discounting end and costs nothing at the other. A reward that arrives at the end of a week is effectively invisible to someone with strong delay aversion; a daily or per-action tick with a weekly roll-up serves both.
- Informational feedback ("That is 3 this week") is the safe reward: it is the one form that raises intrinsic motivation in the Deci meta-analysis. Expected tangible-style rewards that are contingent on doing an already-enjoyed activity carry the undermining risk, so points should read as a record of what was done, not as pay.
- A forgiving streak protects the perfectionistic end; a visible limit on forgiveness (priced passes) keeps it meaningful for the person who needs the structure. Partial credit ("did the short version") gives all-or-nothing thinkers a third option between perfect and failed.
- People who already have strong routines gain little from prompts and incentives, so the gamified layer should be quiet by default for established behaviors and adjustable: reminders, challenges and celebration intensity as settings.
- Do not infer or label traits. Offer the same choices to everyone (reminder timing, challenge length, rest rules) and let behavior show what helps.
- Exercise-specific caution: because a meaningful minority of regular exercisers show dependence markers, a training streak must never reward training through a planned rest day, illness or injury, and should not escalate targets automatically.

### Gaps
- No trial was found of gamification, streaks or immediate feedback for *adults* with ADHD traits in a habit or to-do app. The claim that immediate reinforcement helps is an extrapolation from lab discounting and reinforcement studies, mostly in children.
- A 2010 follow-up review by Luman, Tripp and Scheres on reinforcement sensitivity is widely cited but was not located in this session.
- No study was found that measures perfectionism against streak mechanics directly; the link is inferred from the self-blame moderator and the dichotomous-thinking literature.
- Adult-only effect sizes for delay discounting were not retrieved; the pooled estimates mix ages (one review notes weaker adult evidence in some analyses).

## 6. Motivation over time: self-determination theory, identity, progress and measurement effects

### Takeaway
Supporting autonomy, competence and relatedness produces small but reliable changes in health behavior (around d = 0.2 at the behavior level), monitoring progress is among the better-supported techniques (d about 0.4), and gamification effects on physical activity shrink by roughly two thirds after the intervention ends. Identity wording is attractive but its headline studies have failed replication and it can backfire after a setback, so "I am someone who" framing should be earned by evidence of behavior, never asserted.

### Cited Findings

**Self-determination theory**
- [V] META. Ntoumanis et al. (2021, Health Psychol Rev 15:214-244): 73 experimental studies of SDT-informed health interventions. Effects on need support, need satisfaction, autonomous motivation, health behavior and physical and psychological health were positive but "modest, heterogeneous", and increases in need support and autonomous motivation (not controlled motivation or amotivation) were associated with positive behavior change. — [Ntoumanis et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2020.1718529)
- [V] META. Sheeran et al. (2020): sample-weighted average effect of SDT interventions on health behavior d+ = .23, "significant but small". — [Sheeran et al. 2020, J Consult Clin Psychol (Birmingham record)](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/self-determination-theory-interventions-for-health-behavior-chang/)
- [V] META. Gillison et al. (2019, Health Psychol Rev 13:110-130): 74 studies, 80% RCTs. Interventions raised perceived autonomy support (g = 0.84), autonomy satisfaction (g = 0.81), competence (g = 0.63), relatedness (g = 0.28) and motivation (g = 0.41). Competence gains were larger in adults (g = 0.95) than children (g = 0.11) and in one-to-one than group delivery (g = 0.96 vs 0.28). No single technique carried the effect: need support "requires the combination of multiple co-acting techniques". — [Gillison et al. 2019 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30295176/)
- [R] LAB. Game elements map onto needs: badges, leaderboards and performance graphs raised competence satisfaction and perceived task meaningfulness; avatars, stories and teammates raised relatedness. — [Sailer, Hense, Mayr & Mandl 2017, Comput Human Behav](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2016.12.033)
- [R] META. Gamified interventions increased physical activity with Hedges g = 0.42 at the end of intervention and g = 0.15 at follow-up (16 RCTs, 2,407 participants). — [Mazeas, Duclos, Pereira & Chalabaev 2022, J Med Internet Res](https://doi.org/10.2196/26779)
- [R] RCT. STEP UP (N = 602 adults with overweight, 24 weeks plus 12 weeks follow-up): gamification with points and levels that could be lost raised daily steps in all three arms during the intervention (competition arm about +920 steps a day over control); only the competition arm kept a significant gain at follow-up (about +569). — [Patel et al. 2019, JAMA Intern Med](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.3505)
- [V] META. Financial incentives raise daily steps during an intervention (pooled +607 steps, 95% CI 422 to 792; 23 studies, 6,074 participants), with post-intervention results mixed (only 4 of 18 studies showed a benefit by vote count); a second meta-analysis of 51 trials found slight sustained effects (0.10 to 0.11). — [Mitchell et al. 2020, Br J Sports Med](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/bjsports/54/21/1259.full.pdf); [Luong et al. 2020 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32672050/)

**Identity**
- [R] Theory. Identity-based motivation: people prefer actions that feel congruent with a currently salient identity, and read difficulty as either "this matters" or "this is not for me" depending on that fit. — [Oyserman & Destin 2010, The Counseling Psychologist](https://doi.org/10.1177/0011000010374775)
- [R] META (correlational). Exercise identity correlates with physical activity at about r = .44 (32 studies). Direction of causation is not established. — [Rhodes, Kaushal & Quinlan 2016, Health Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2016.1143334)
- [V] CONTESTED. Noun wording ("be a voter" vs "vote") raised turnout in the original study (95.5% vs 81.8%); a large replication by Gerber and colleagues found no difference, and a second replication in competitive states also failed. — [FORRT Replication Atlas entry](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1073/pnas.1103343108/); [Gerber et al. replication](https://povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/voting-behavior-unaffected-subtle-linguistic-cues-evidence)
- [R] LAB, children. Identity wording can backfire after failure: children asked to "be helpers" (noun) who then experienced a setback helped less afterwards than children asked "to help" (verb). — [Foster-Hanson, Cimpian, Leshin & Rhodes 2020, Child Dev](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13147)

**Progress, small wins and monitoring**
- [R] OBS. Amabile and Kramer's diary study (238 knowledge workers, 26 teams, 7 companies, nearly 12,000 daily entries): the most common event on people's best inner-work-life days was making progress in meaningful work (76% of best-mood days), and setbacks were the most common event on the worst days; small wins had outsized effects and setbacks outweighed equal-sized progress. — [Amabile & Kramer 2011, Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/05/the-power-of-small-wins)
- [R] META. Monitoring goal progress promotes attainment: d+ = 0.40 across 138 studies (N = 19,951); effects were larger when progress was physically recorded or reported to someone else. — [Harkin et al. 2016, Psychol Bull](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025)
- [R] Theory. Weick's "small wins": recasting large problems as a series of concrete, completed, modest outcomes lowers arousal and builds momentum. — [Weick 1984, Am Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.39.1.40)

**Mere measurement and the question-behavior effect**
- [V] META. Asking people about a behavior changes it slightly: g = 0.14 (95% CI 0.11 to 0.18) across 94 tests in 66 studies; g = 0.07 in low risk-of-bias studies versus 0.22 in unclear or high risk. For health, g = 0.16 for increasing healthy behaviors and 0.08 for reducing risk behaviors. — [Wilding et al. 2016, Eur Rev Soc Psychol (White Rose)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/108516/); [health-focused review](https://ehps.net/ehp/index.php/contents/article/view/1093)
- [R] META. A second meta-analysis of intention and self-prediction questions found d+ = 0.24 across 116 tests. — [Wood et al. 2016, Pers Soc Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868315592334)

### Inferences
- Autonomy support in a streak system means the person sets the rules: which behaviors count, how many days a week, how many passes, whether a challenge is on at all. Imposed challenges are the controlled-motivation case that did not predict behavior change in Ntoumanis.
- Competence support means the reward is evidence of capability (a count, a trend, "longest run this month"), sized so most days are winnable. Adults respond more to competence support than children do.
- Expect any gamified lift to decay (g 0.42 to 0.15). Plan for the layer to hand over to habit and enjoyment, not to carry motivation indefinitely.
- Use identity language only as a reflection of logged facts ("You have run on 9 of the last 12 planned days") and avoid noun labels that a miss can falsify ("You are a runner" on the day someone skipped is the backfire case).
- Asymmetry of setbacks (Amabile) argues for making wins small and frequent and making the miss screen quiet and brief.
- Asking "Will you train tomorrow?" has a real but very small effect (g about 0.1). It is cheap, so a light end-of-day prompt is defensible, but it is not a mechanism to build on.

### Gaps
- Exact pooled effect sizes by outcome for Ntoumanis et al. 2021 were not retrieved; only the abstract-level description.
- No meta-analysis specific to SDT need support delivered by apps (as opposed to people) was found; the delivery-format finding (one-to-one beats group) hints that human-delivered support is stronger.
- The progress principle rests on one large diary study in workplaces; no replication in personal habit contexts was found.
- No adult replication of the "helper" backfire was found.

## 7. Recovery, rest and overreach

### Takeaway
Recovery is an active requirement for sustained effort: mental detachment has small-to-medium links to well-being and performance, flexible plans with planned days off sustain goals at least as well as rigid ones, and a minority of regular exercisers show dependence markers that a streak could aggravate. The direct evidence that scheduled rest days improve exercise adherence is thin, so the case for "rest counts" rests on adjacent findings.

### Cited Findings
- [R] Sonnentag and Fritz define four recovery experiences (psychological detachment, relaxation, mastery, control) and the stressor-detachment model, in which detachment both mediates and buffers the effect of demands on strain. — [Sonnentag & Fritz 2007, J Occup Health Psychol](https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-8998.12.3.204); [Sonnentag & Fritz 2015, J Organ Behav](https://doi.org/10.1002/job.1924)
- [V] META. Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah (2017): 86 publications, 91 samples, 38,124 employees. Detachment was linked to better mental health, state well-being and task performance with small-to-medium effects, was unrelated to physiological stress markers and work motivation, and negatively related to creativity and contextual performance. Heavy work investment had a medium negative relationship with detachment. Mostly cross-sectional. — [Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2017, Front Psychol](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.02072)
- [V] META. Bennett, Bakker & Field (2018): 299 effect sizes, 54 samples (N = 26,592). Detachment had a stronger negative relationship with fatigue than relaxation or control did; challenge demands eroded detachment more than hindrance demands. — [Bennett, Bakker & Field 2018, J Organ Behav](https://doi.org/10.1002/job.2217)
- [R] Consensus statement. The joint European College of Sport Science and American College of Sports Medicine statement on overtraining syndrome identifies insufficient recovery relative to training load, monotony of training and non-training stressors as contributors, and rest as the treatment. — [Meeusen et al. 2013, Med Sci Sports Exerc](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e318279a10a)
- [V] FIELD. Flexibility beat rigidity for exercise persistence in a 2,508-person field experiment. — [Beshears et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3706)
- [V] LAB/FIELD. Planned deviations and priced slack sustained motivation and goal performance (Coelho do Vale et al. 2016; Sharif & Shu 2017, 2021; see section 3). — [Coelho do Vale et al. 2016](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/the-benefits-of-behaving-badly-on-occasion-successful-regulation-/); [Sharif & Shu 2017](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.15.0231)
- [V] Prevalence of exercise dependence risk: about 3 to 7% of regular exercisers and students and 6 to 9% of athletes in one systematic review; 8.1% of general exercisers in a 2020 meta-analysis; odds 3.71 times higher with an indicated eating disorder (95% CI 2.00 to 6.89). Estimates vary with the instrument and exercise addiction is not a formally recognized disorder. — [Marques et al. systematic review](https://www.springermedizin.de/prevalence-of-risk-for-exercise-dependence-a-systematic-review/16234606); [Trott et al. 2020 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32496431/); [Trott et al. 2021 comparative meta-analysis](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/a-comparative-metaanalysis-of-the-prevalence-of-exercise-addictio/)
- [R] Risk markers used by validated screens: salience, mood modification, tolerance (needing more), withdrawal when unable to exercise, conflict with other life areas and relapse (Exercise Addiction Inventory); and avoidance or rule-driven exercise, exercising for weight control, lack of enjoyment and rigidity (Compulsive Exercise Test). — [Terry, Szabo & Griffiths 2004, Addict Res Theory](https://doi.org/10.1080/16066350310001637363); [Taranis, Touyz & Meyer 2011, Eur Eat Disord Rev](https://doi.org/10.1002/erv.1108)
- [V] A meta-review proposes ten candidate criteria including increasing volume, negative affect when unable to exercise, inability to reduce and preoccupation. — [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2020 meta-review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.521572/pdf)

### Inferences
- In a training app the unit of the streak should be "followed the plan", so a scheduled rest day advances it. This removes the conflict between the streak and the plan, which is where compulsive patterns (rule-driven, exercising despite needing rest) would otherwise be rewarded.
- Rest should be named as an action with its own completion state ("Rest day. Done."), not as an absence or a freeze. Attribution research (section 3) says a planned, external reason for not training does not carry the cost of a self-blamed break.
- Design guardrails drawn from the risk markers: no reward for extra sessions on rest days, no automatic escalation of streak targets, no loss-framed warnings at night, and a blameless pause for illness or injury that preserves the record.
- Detachment research suggests evening prompts should close the day, not open new demands; a day that is finished should look finished.

### Gaps
- No randomized trial was found that compares exercise adherence with and without scheduled rest days in recreational adults. The claim "planned rest supports adherence" is an inference from flexibility, planned-deviation and slack studies.
- No study was found linking app streak features to exercise dependence or to training through injury; this is a plausible risk without direct evidence.
- No evidence was found on how to *word* rest; the recommendations are inferences from attribution and self-determination findings.

## 8. Short challenges: time-boxed commitments, goal proximity, sprint intervals, novelty and surprise

### Takeaway
Short, bounded commitments work through deadlines, proximity to a visible finish and concentrated repetition, and they reliably raise behavior while they run; almost none of that lift survives the end of the challenge on its own, so a sprint's job is to seed repetitions and hand over to something durable. Streak-shaped incentives motivate more effort than larger flat ones, uncertainty in a reward adds repeat behavior when it resolves immediately, and novelty effects fade within about a month.

### Cited Findings

**Commitment devices and deadlines**
- [R] LAB/FIELD. Ariely & Wertenbroch (2002): people willingly impose costly deadlines on themselves; self-imposed deadlines improved performance over a single end deadline but less than evenly spaced external deadlines (99 executive-education students; 60 proofreaders). People do not set their own deadlines optimally. — [Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002, Psychol Sci](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.00441)
- [R] Viewpoint. Rogers, Milkman & Volpp define commitment devices as voluntary arrangements that restrict future choices or attach a cost to failing, and summarize supporting trials (savings, smoking, weight). — [Rogers, Milkman & Volpp 2014, JAMA](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.3485)
- [R] FIELD. Royer, Stehr & Sydnor (2015): gym incentives for employees of a Fortune 500 company raised attendance while paid with little persistence, but offering a self-funded commitment contract when the incentive ended produced effects that persisted for years. — [Royer, Stehr & Sydnor 2015, Am Econ J Appl Econ](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20130327)
- [R] FIELD. Paying students to attend a gym eight times in a month raised attendance after payments stopped, mainly among people who had not been regular attenders. — [Charness & Gneezy 2009, Econometrica](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA7416)
- [V] FIELD, caution. Carrera, Royer, Stehr, Sydnor & Taubinsky (2022, Rev Econ Stud): among 1,248 gym members, take-up of commitment contracts was high and raised exercise, but take-up was unrelated to measured time-inconsistency, about half of those who chose a contract for *more* visits also chose one for *fewer*, and the contracts lowered estimated welfare relative to a simple per-visit subsidy. People pick commitment devices for reasons other than a real need for them. — [Carrera et al. 2022 (NBER)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26161)
- [V] FIELD. Four-week programs: 45% of 53 raised gym visits by 9 to 27% during the program, 8% showed any detectable effect in the four weeks after. Among programs that worked, each extra visit during the program was associated with about 0.28 extra visits over the following ten weeks (95% CI 0.07 to 0.59). Experts' forecasts of which would work were uncorrelated with results (r = 0.02) and about 9 times too optimistic. — [Milkman et al. 2021, Nature (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/)

**Streak-shaped rewards**
- [V] LAB (six preregistered studies, N = 4,493). Mehr, Silverman, Sharif, Barasch & Milkman (2025, OBHDP 187): incentives that increase for consecutive completions led people to complete more effortful work than larger flat incentives, including when the streak scheme paid less per task (six cents vs nine). The mechanism was commitment to the goal created by the consecutive requirement, not the escalating amounts alone. — [Mehr et al. 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v187y2025ics0749597825000032.html); [OBHDP summary](https://obhdp.substack.com/p/new-article-in-obhdp-may-7th-2025)
- [V] FIELD. A same-weekday streak positively predicted gym attendance for 69% of members. — [Buyalskaya et al. 2023 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10151500/)

**Goal proximity**
- [R] FIELD. Goal-gradient: effort accelerates as people near a reward. Coffee-card customers bought more frequently as the card filled, and a 12-stamp card with two stamps pre-filled was completed faster than a plain 10-stamp card (illusory progress). — [Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006, J Mark Res](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)
- [R] FIELD. Endowed progress: car-wash customers given a 10-stamp card with two stamps already filled redeemed at 34% versus 19% for an 8-stamp card with none (N = 300). — [Nunes & Drèze 2006, J Consum Res](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)
- [R] LAB. Motivation sags in the middle of a goal and is higher near the start and the end ("stuck in the middle"); attention to whichever is smaller, progress so far or progress remaining, raises motivation (small-area hypothesis). — [Bonezzi, Brendl & De Angelis 2011, Psychol Sci](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611404899); [Koo & Fishbach 2012, J Consum Res](https://doi.org/10.1086/663827)
- [R] LAB/FIELD. Subgoals motivate most early in a pursuit; near the end, focus on the overall goal works better. — [Huang, Jin & Zhang 2017, Organ Behav Hum Decis Process](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2017.05.001)
- [R] LAB, children. Proximal subgoals built self-efficacy, interest and performance better than distal goals. — [Bandura & Schunk 1981, JPSP](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.41.3.586)

**Sprint-like work intervals (Pomodoro)**
- [R] Quasi-experiment. Biwer, Wiradhany, oude Egbrink & de Bruin (2023, Br J Educ Psychol), 87 students in real study sessions: pre-set breaks (24 min work/6 min break or 12/3) versus self-chosen breaks. Students taking self-regulated breaks studied in longer stretches, took longer breaks, and reported more fatigue and distraction and lower concentration and motivation; task completion did not differ. So the benefit was to how the session felt, not to output. — [Biwer et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12593)
- [V] A 2025 scoping review of the Pomodoro literature (BMC Medical Education, focused on anatomy study) concludes time-structured intervals "appeared to improve focus, reduce fatigue, and boost sustained performance relative to self-paced breaks", from a small and heterogeneous set of studies. Low evidence strength; popular figures such as "15 to 25% focus gains" could not be verified. — [Ogut 2025, BMC Med Educ](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-025-08001-0)
- [R] META. Micro-breaks of up to ten minutes: small improvements in vigor (d = 0.36) and fatigue (d = 0.35), no reliable overall effect on performance (d = 0.16), across 22 studies (N = 2,335). — [Albulescu et al. 2022, PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272460)

**Novelty and surprise**
- [V] FIELD (education). Gamification's effect on 756 students over 14 weeks followed a U shape: it began to fall after four weeks and recovered between weeks six and ten ("novelty effect" then "familiarization effect"). A two-year study of a gamified course also found weekly engagement declining over time. — [Rodrigues et al. 2022, Int J Educ Technol High Educ](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6); [Tsay et al. 2020, J Comput Assist Learn](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcal.12385)
- [R] META. Gamification in learning: g = 0.49 cognitive, 0.36 motivational, 0.25 behavioral outcomes, with high heterogeneity. — [Sailer & Homner 2020, Educ Psychol Rev](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)
- [V] LAB plus FIELD (four experiments). Reinforcing-uncertainty effect: people repeated an activity more when the reward for each repetition was uncertain in size than when it was certain, even if the certain reward was larger (in a running study, about 14 laps versus 7.5 per the Chicago Booth summary). It appeared only when the uncertainty was resolved immediately after each repetition and only once people had begun repeating; the authors explain it as the resolution being rewarding in itself. — [Shen, Hsee & Talloen 2019, J Consum Res](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:v:46:y:2019:i:1:p:69-81.); [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/how-offering-random-rewards-can-shape-customer-behavior)
- [R] LAB. Unexpected rewards do not undermine intrinsic motivation, unlike expected contingent ones. — [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627)

### Inferences
- A sprint of one to four weeks with a named end date is the evidence-shaped unit: long enough to stack early repetitions (when automaticity grows fastest), short enough that the finish is always in sight. Seven days keeps the whole challenge inside the high-motivation start and end zones and avoids the middle sag; for longer sprints, show progress as "days done" early and "days left" late.
- Say what a sprint is honestly: a start, not a habit. Follow each sprint with a default handover (keep the anchor, drop the bonus, or roll into the next one) because the lift decays fast once the structure is removed.
- Let the person choose length and start date, but propose evenly spaced check-ins; people set their own deadlines suboptimally and externally spaced ones work better.
- Reward consecutiveness modestly (Mehr et al.), and pair it with priced passes and repair (section 3) so the mechanic that motivates is not also the one that ends participation.
- Give a head start. Counting the sign-up day or a just-completed session as "1 of 7" uses endowed progress without deception, as long as the step is real.
- Use surprise sparingly and at the moment of completion: a varied acknowledgement right after check-off is the form the evidence supports. Unexpected rewards are also the kind that do not crowd out intrinsic interest. Avoid delayed or lottery-style prizes.
- Expect a dip around week four of any new mechanic and do not respond by adding more mechanics. Rotating the challenge content is the low-cost answer.
- Timed focus intervals with built-in breaks make work feel easier without proving more output; offer them as an optional aid for starting, not as a productivity claim.

### Gaps
- No study was found comparing challenge lengths (7 vs 14 vs 30 days) head to head for habit or adherence outcomes.
- The Mehr et al. streak studies are online work tasks over a single session or a few days, not health behaviors over weeks; the transfer to training is untested.
- The reinforcing-uncertainty studies are short; whether surprise sustains engagement over months, and whether it interacts with delay aversion, is unknown.
- Pomodoro evidence is small, mostly student samples, and mixed in quality; no adult workplace RCT with objective output was found.
- Whether commitment contracts help the people who choose them is contested (Carrera et al. 2022), so stake-based challenges are not recommended on this evidence.
