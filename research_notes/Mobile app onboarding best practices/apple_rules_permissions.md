# Apple platform rules and best practice for onboarding, permissions, accounts and accessibility (health/fitness/habit app on HealthKit, local notifications, AI coach, Capacitor)

Research date: 10 November 2026. Apple sources: HIG pages were pulled from Apple's own JSON data behind developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines (the same text the site renders). The App Store Review Guidelines were fetched live. Their page shows no "last updated" date, but the text includes the November 2025 "third-party AI" wording.
Source-type labels used below: **[Apple rule]** = App Store Review Guidelines or a hard API constraint; **[Apple guidance]** = HIG or developer documentation advice; **[Industry dataset]** = benchmark with a stated method; **[Vendor claim / practitioner report]** = blog, forum or secondary reporting.

## 1. HIG Onboarding, Launching, Loading: length, skippability, teaching by doing, delaying setup, splash/launch screens, restoring state

### Takeaway
Apple wants onboarding to be "fast, fun, and optional". It should come *after* launch, teach by doing (or with contextual tips instead of a deck), skip nonessential setup, and ask for a permission inside onboarding only if the app can't function without it. The launch screen must look like the first screen and carry no branding or text. Restore state on relaunch.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple guidance]** HIG Onboarding, opening line: "Ideally, people can understand your app or game simply by experiencing it, but if onboarding is necessary, design a flow that's fast, fun, and optional. When available, onboarding occurs after launching is complete — it isn't part of the launch experience." — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Teach through interactivity… As much as possible, provide an interactive onboarding experience where people can safely test an action, discover a feature, or try out a game mechanic." — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Consider providing a collection of context-specific tips instead of a single onboarding flow… display these instructions near that area." (Points to TipKit.) — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** Length: "If you need to present a prerequisite onboarding flow, design a brief, enjoyable experience that doesn't require people to memorize a lot of information… if you try to teach too much, people can feel overwhelmed and may be less likely to remember what they learned." Apple gives no screen count or time limit. — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** Skippability: "If it makes sense to offer a separate tutorial, consider making it optional. If you let people skip the tutorial when they first launch… don't present it again on subsequent launches, but make sure it's easy for people to find if they want to view it later… in a help, account, or settings area." — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Keep onboarding content focused on the experience you provide… they don't need to learn how to use the system or the device." — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** Splash: "Briefly display a splash screen if necessary… just long enough for people to absorb the information at a glance without feeling that it's delaying their experience." — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Don't let large downloads hinder onboarding" and "Avoid displaying licensing details within your onboarding flow. Let the App Store display agreements and disclaimers…" — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** Delaying setup: "Postpone nonessential setup flows or customization steps. Provide reasonable default settings so most people can immediately start interacting…" — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** Permissions inside onboarding: "If your app or game needs access to private data or resources before it can function, consider integrating the permission request into your onboarding flow… Otherwise, present a permission request when people first access the specific function that relies on private data or resources." — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Prefer letting people experience your app or game before prompting them for ratings or purchases." This bears directly on onboarding paywalls. — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** Change log: Onboarding page revised June 10, 2024 ("Clarified different approaches to onboarding and added a guideline on displaying a splash screen"). — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** Launching: "Launch instantly. People want to start interacting with your app or game right away, and sometimes they don't want to wait more than a couple of seconds." — [HIG: Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching)
- **[Apple guidance]** "If you need a splash screen, consider displaying it at the beginning of your onboarding flow… If you don't provide an onboarding experience, you might display your splash screen as soon as launching completes." — [HIG: Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching)
- **[Apple guidance]** Restoring state: "Restore the previous state when your app restarts so people can continue where they left off… scroll the view to people's most recent position…" — [HIG: Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching)
- **[Apple guidance]** Launch screen: "A launch screen isn't part of an onboarding experience or a splash screen, and it isn't an opportunity for artistic expression"; "Design a launch screen that's nearly identical to the first screen"; "Avoid including text on your launch screen"; "Don't advertise… don't include logos or other branding elements unless they're a fixed part of your app's first screen"; match orientation and light/dark appearance. — [HIG: Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching)
- **[Apple guidance]** Loading: "Show something as soon as possible… consider showing placeholder text, graphics, or animations as content loads"; use determinate/indeterminate progress indicators when loading takes "more than a moment or two". Revised June 9, 2025. — [HIG: Loading](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading)

### Inferences
- A branded "orb" splash is acceptable only as the *first screen of onboarding* (or right after launch), shown briefly. The native iOS launch storyboard should be a plain solid color or a copy of the first screen's background, with no logo or text.
- For a habit app, "teach by doing" maps to letting the user log a first habit or check-in during onboarding rather than reading feature slides. TipKit-style contextual tips (in Capacitor, custom in-web tips) can replace per-area tours.
- A skipped tour must not reappear on every launch and should be reachable from Settings/Help.
- For a returning user, restoring state means reopening the last tab/scroll position rather than replaying onboarding.

### Gaps
- Apple publishes no number for "brief" (screens or seconds). I found no WWDC session titled "Design great onboarding". The HIG page lists videos, but the JSON did not expose their titles.

## 2. Permission requests: priming screens, timing, purpose strings; Review Guidelines 5.1.1, 5.1.2 (incl. 2025 third-party AI rule), 5.1.3, 4.5.4

### Takeaway
A pre-permission screen is allowed. It must have exactly one button (titled "Continue"/"Next", not "Allow") that always opens the system alert, and no close/cancel or other diversion. Incentives, imitation alerts, alert images and annotations are explicit rejection triggers in the HIG's tracking section. Ask at the moment of use unless the app can't function without the permission. Purpose strings must be specific, active-voice, full sentences. 5.1.2(i) now requires a clear disclosure plus *explicit permission* before personal data goes to third-party AI (added 13 Nov 2025). Apps may not require system features such as notifications to use the app or to earn rewards.

### Cited Findings
**HIG Privacy: timing and purpose strings**
- **[Apple guidance]** "Request permission only when your app clearly needs access to the data or resource… Ideally, wait to request permission until people actually use an app feature that requires access." — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Request access only to data that you actually need. Asking for more data than a feature needs — or asking for data before a person shows interest in the feature — can make it hard for people to trust your app." — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- **[Apple guidance]** Purpose strings: "Aim for a brief, complete sentence that's straightforward, specific, and easy to understand. Use sentence case, avoid passive voice, and include a period at the end." Good example: "The app records during the night to detect snoring sounds." Bad: "Microphone access is needed for a better experience." (passive, vague) and "Turn on microphone access." (no justification). — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Process data on the device where possible." — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)

**HIG Privacy: pre-alert ("priming") screens**
- **[Apple guidance]** "Ideally, the current context helps people understand why you're requesting their permission. If it's essential to provide additional details, you can display a custom screen or window before the system alert appears." This applies to camera, microphone, location, contact, calendar and tracking. — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Include only one button and make it clear that it opens the system alert… Another type of manipulation is using a term like 'Allow' to title the custom screen's button… Use a term like 'Continue' or 'Next'…" — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Don't include additional actions in your custom screen or window, unless needed to obtain a legal consent. For example, don't provide a way for people to leave the screen or window without viewing the system alert — like offering an option to close or cancel." — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- **[Apple guidance → review enforcement]** On tracking pre-alerts: "A custom messaging screen… that takes advantage of such behaviors to influence choices will lead to rejection by App Store review. There are several prohibited custom-screen designs that will cause rejection. Some examples are offering incentives, displaying a screen or window that looks like a request, displaying an image of the alert, and annotating the screen behind the alert." — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- **[Apple guidance]** Privacy page consolidated June 21, 2023. No later change log entry. — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)

**App Review Guidelines (current text)**
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.1(i)**: privacy policy link required in App Store Connect *and* "within the app in an easily accessible manner". It must say what data is collected and how, confirm that third parties give "the same or equal protection", and explain retention/deletion and how to revoke consent. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.1(ii)**: "Apps that collect user or usage data must secure user consent for the collection, even if such data is considered to be anonymous… Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data. Apps must also provide the customer with an easily accessible and understandable way to withdraw consent. Ensure your purpose strings clearly and completely describe your use of the data." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.1(iii)**: "Apps should only request access to data relevant to the core functionality of the app…" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.1(iv)**: "Apps must respect the user's permission settings and not attempt to manipulate, trick, or force people to consent to unnecessary data access… Where possible, provide alternative solutions for users who don't grant consent." (Example: manual address entry when Location is declined.) — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.1(ix)**: apps in "highly regulated fields (such as… healthcare…) or that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.1(x)**: basic contact info (name, email) may be requested "so long as the request is optional for the user, features and services are not conditional on providing the information". — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.2(i)** (current wording): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so… Your app may not require users to enable system functionalities (such as push notifications, location services, tracking) in order to access functionality, content, use the app, or receive monetary or other compensation, including but not limited to gift cards and codes." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Vendor claim / secondary reporting]** The "including with third-party AI" wording was added on **13 November 2025**. Reports say a privacy-policy line alone does not satisfy it; an in-app consent is expected. Sources disagree on whether this is new or a clarification of the existing consent rule. — [TechRepublic (DE)](https://www.techrepublic.com/de/article/news-apple-app-review-guidelines-ai-data-sharing/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/arshtechpro/apples-guideline-512i-the-ai-data-sharing-rule-that-will-impact-every-ios-developer-1b0p); [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/apple-app-store-ai-data-disclosure-guidelines)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.2(ii)**: "Data collected for one purpose may not be repurposed without further consent…" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.2(vi)**: data from HealthKit (and similar APIs) "may not be used for marketing, advertising or use-based data mining, including by third parties." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.3(i)**: health/fitness data (HealthKit, Motion and Fitness, etc.) may not be used or disclosed to third parties "for advertising, marketing, or other use-based data mining purposes other than improving health management, or for the purpose of health research, and then only with permission… You must disclose the specific health data that you are collecting from the device." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.3(ii)**: "Apps must not write false or inaccurate data into HealthKit… and may not store personal health information in iCloud." 5.1.3(iii)–(iv) cover human-subject research consent and ethics board approval. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 4.5.4**: "Push Notifications must not be required for the app to function, and should not be used to send sensitive personal or confidential information. Push Notifications should not be used for promotions or direct marketing purposes unless customers have explicitly opted in to receive them via consent language displayed in your app's UI, and you provide a method in your app for a user to opt out…" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 2.5.1**: "HealthKit should be used for health and fitness purposes and integrate with the Health app"; apps should "indicate that integration in their app description." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- The pre-permission screen should read like an explanation card with one "Continue" button that always triggers the system sheet. No "Not now"/"Skip" on that card (per HIG), no badge/streak/reward for granting (incentives are named as a rejection pattern, and 5.1.2(i) bans requiring system features for "monetary or other compensation"), and no mock-up of the alert.
- Keep the user's option to decline *before* reaching the priming card by placing it at a natural moment: e.g. "Set a reminder for this habit?" → Yes leads to the priming card, and No leaves the flow without ever reaching it. This stays within the one-button rule because the priming card is only shown after the user opts into the feature. (Inference, not Apple text.)
- The AI coach needs its own explicit in-app consent before any personal data (health samples, journal text, habits) is sent to a third-party model provider. It must name what is sent and to whom, and be withdrawable (5.1.1(ii), 5.1.2(i)). Sending HealthKit data to an AI provider must also fit 5.1.3(i): only to improve the user's health management, never for ads or data mining. Apple's HealthKit doc also limits third-party sharing to parties that "also provide a health or fitness service to the user" (see section 3).
- Health reminder notifications must not reveal sensitive health details in their text (4.5.4; HIG Notifications).
- If the app is published by an individual rather than a company, 5.1.1(ix) is a risk for a "healthcare" positioning. Framing it as fitness/habits lowers this risk, but this is a reviewer judgment.

### Gaps
- I couldn't fetch Apple's own news post announcing the 13 Nov 2025 change. The date comes from secondary reporting (consistent across several outlets).
- Apple publishes no example of a compliant "AI consent" screen.

## 3. HealthKit specifics

### Takeaway
Say "Apple Health" (never "HealthKit") in UI. Ask for each data type when the feature needs it, with a clear purpose string. Don't build a screen that replicates the Health permission sheet, or in-app toggles that control Health data flow. Design for the fact that read denial is invisible: "denied" and "no data" look identical. getRequestStatusForAuthorization only tells you whether the sheet *would* appear. Background delivery needs a specific entitlement (iOS 15+) and is throttled (step count at most hourly).

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple guidance]** "If your app doesn't provide health and fitness functionality, don't request access to people's private health data." — [HIG: HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Request access to health data only when you need it. It makes sense to request access to weight information when people log their weight, for example, but not immediately after your app launches… people can change the permissions they grant, so your app needs to make a request every time it needs access." — [HIG: HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Clarify your app's intent by adding descriptive messages to the standard permission screen… Write a few succinct sentences that explain why you need the information and how people can benefit… Avoid adding custom screens that replicate the standard permission screen's behavior or content." — [HIG: HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Manage health data sharing solely through the system's privacy settings… Don't confuse people by building additional screens in your app that affect the flow of health data." — [HIG: HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit)
- **[Apple guidance]** Editorial: "Refer to the Health app as Apple Health or the Apple Health app." "Don't use the term HealthKit. HealthKit is a developer-facing term…" Suggested copy: "works with the Apple Health app" / "uses data from the Apple Health app". Capitalize "Apple Health" as two words. — [HIG: HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit)
- **[Apple guidance]** Apple Health icon: use only the Apple-provided icon, show the name "Apple Health" near it, don't use it as a button, don't alter or mask it, keep clear space of 1/10 its height, and don't show Health app screenshots. Activity rings are only for Move/Exercise/Stand, for one person, and not for decoration or branding. — [HIG: HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit)
- **[Apple rule – API behavior]** "Your app doesn't know whether someone granted or denied permission to read data from HealthKit. If they denied permission, attempts to read data from HealthKit return only samples that your app successfully saved to the HealthKit store." People can also grant only "a limited window of recent data". `getEarliestAuthorizedSampleDate(for:)` detects that limited state, but "you can't distinguish between full access and denied access for specific types; both appear the same to your app." — [Apple: Authorizing access to health data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)
- **[Apple guidance]** The authorization sheet groups types into categories (Activity, Heart, Nutrition) with per-type toggles. A second screen now asks how much history to share (recent limited window vs full history). After prompting, the app appears in Health > Sources "even if the person didn't allow permission". — [Apple: Authorizing access to health data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)
- **[Apple guidance]** "If your app makes inferences on partial data, consider informing people that granting full access improves your app's experience." — [Apple: Authorizing access to health data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)
- **[Apple rule – API]** `NSHealthShareUsageDescription` (read) and `NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription` (write) are both required. "Apps must include usage descriptions, or it will crash when you request authorization." Call `isHealthDataAvailable()` first. — [Apple: Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy); [Apple: Authorizing access](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)
- **[Apple rule – API]** `getRequestStatusForAuthorization(toShare:read:completion:)` "Indicates whether the system presents the user with a permission sheet if your app requests authorization for the provided types". It reports whether you have *asked*, not whether the user said yes. `authorizationStatus(for:)` covers *write (share)* status only: "your app cannot determine whether or not a user has granted permission to read data. If you are not given permission, it simply appears as if there is no data." — [Apple: getRequestStatusForAuthorization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/getrequeststatusforauthorization(toshare:read:completion:)); [Apple: authorizationStatus(for:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/authorizationstatus(for:))
- **[Apple rule – API]** Background delivery: "For iOS 15 and watchOS 8 and later, you must enable the HealthKit Background Delivery by adding the `com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery` entitlement… If your app doesn't have this entitlement, the `enableBackgroundDelivery(for:frequency:withCompletion:)` method fails with an errorAuthorizationDenied error." On iOS, stepCount has an hourly maximum frequency. Observer queries must be set up in `application(_:didFinishLaunchingWithOptions:)`, and the completion handler must be called, or "If your app fails to respond three times, HealthKit assumes your app can't receive data and stops sending background updates." — [Apple: enableBackgroundDelivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:)); [Apple: background-delivery entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery)
- **[Apple rule – API]** The Health store is encrypted when the device is locked, so "your app may not be able to read data from the store when it runs in the background", though writes are cached. — [Apple: Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)
- **[Apple rule]** HealthKit usage rules: no ads based on HealthKit data; no disclosure to third parties without express permission, "Even with permission, you can only share information to a third party if they also provide a health or fitness service to the user"; no selling to ad platforms or data brokers; you must clearly disclose how HealthKit data is used; a privacy policy is mandatory. The use must be "clear in both your marketing text and your user interface." — [Apple: Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)
- **[Apple rule]** What reviewers check (from the guideline text): health/fitness purpose evident and described in the app description (2.5.1); privacy policy in metadata and in-app (5.1.1(i)); specific health data disclosed (5.1.3(i)); no health data in iCloud and no false writes (5.1.3(ii)); no ads/marketing use (5.1.2(vi), 5.1.3(i)); explicit consent for third-party (incl. AI) sharing (5.1.2(i)). — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Onboarding cannot show a reliable "Apple Health connected ✓ / denied ✗" state for reads. Use getRequestStatusForAuthorization for "asked / not asked". After asking, infer success from whether recent samples arrive, and show a neutral state ("No step data yet. If you turned off access, you can change it in Settings > Health > Data Access & Devices"), not an accusation.
- A priming card before the Health sheet is allowed (one "Continue" button). It must not look like the Health sheet or include per-type toggles: the HIG says not to replicate the sheet or build screens that "affect the flow of health data".
- Request only the types the first feature needs (e.g., steps + workouts). Request sleep, HRV etc. later when the user opens those features. HealthKit supports incremental requests.
- Copy should say "Connect Apple Health", not "Enable HealthKit".
- In a Capacitor app, the native plugin must register observer queries at native launch (AppDelegate) for background delivery to work. A JS-only registration after the web view loads would miss wake-ups.

### Gaps
- **I found no published dataset on HealthKit permission opt-in rates.** Apple doesn't expose read-grant status, so apps can't easily measure it, and no vendor benchmark surfaced.
- Apple does not publish a reviewer checklist specific to HealthKit beyond the guideline text.

## 4. Notifications: provisional authorization, time-sensitive, opt-in benchmarks, best time to ask

### Takeaway
Apple explicitly recommends asking in context (e.g., after the user schedules a first reminder). Provisional authorization can be requested at first launch without a prompt and delivers quietly to Notification Center with Keep/Turn Off buttons. Time-sensitive interruption level breaks through Focus/Summary and is user-controllable. Industry benchmarks put the iOS push opt-in rate around 49–56% overall, with health & fitness near the average (~54% in Pushwoosh's 2024 data). The methods differ, so compare within one source.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple guidance]** "Make the request in a context that helps people understand why your app needs authorization. In a task-tracking app that sends reminder notifications, you might make the request after the person schedules a first task. Sending the request in context provides a better experience than automatically requesting authorization on first launch…" — [Apple: Asking permission to use notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications)
- **[Apple rule – API]** "The first time your app makes this authorization request, the system prompts the person… Subsequent authorization requests don't prompt the person." The system prompt is one-shot, so a wasted ask is permanent until the user goes to Settings. — [Apple: Asking permission](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications)
- **[Apple guidance]** Provisional: "Use provisional authorization to send notifications on a trial basis… The system delivers provisional notifications quietly — they don't interrupt the person with a sound or banner, or appear on the lock screen. Instead, they only appear in the notification center's history. These notifications also include buttons that prompt the person to keep or turn off the notification." "Keep" offers Deliver Immediately (still quiet: no alerts/sounds/badges until settings change) or Deliver in Scheduled Summary. "If you request provisional authorization, you can request authorization when your app first launches." Status stays `.provisional` until the user acts. — [Apple: Asking permission](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications); [UNAuthorizationOptions.provisional](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unauthorizationoptions/provisional)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Always check your app's authorization status before scheduling local notifications" via `getNotificationSettings`. — [Apple: Asking permission](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications)
- **[Apple rule – API]** Time-sensitive: "The system presents the notification immediately, lights up the screen, can play a sound, and breaks through system notification controls… can break through system controls such as Notification Summary and Focus. The user can turn off the ability for time sensitive notification interruptions." — [UNNotificationInterruptionLevel.timeSensitive](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationinterruptionlevel/timesensitive)
- **[Apple guidance]** HIG Notifications: be concise; "Avoid sending multiple notifications for the same thing"; "Avoid including sensitive, personal, or confidential information in a notification"; provide generic text for hidden previews (e.g., "Reminder"). — [HIG: Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)
- **[Industry dataset]** Pushwoosh 2025 benchmarks: "We analyzed data from over 600 apps", period Q1–Q4 2024; opt-in rate = "percentage of users who have enabled push notifications out of all app users". Per search summaries of the Pushwoosh report: all-industries iOS average 56.36%; Health & Fitness iOS 53.98%; Business 79.87%; Hypercasual games 23.01%. (Category figures not individually verified on the fetched page.) — [Pushwoosh: Push notification benchmarks 2025](https://www.pushwoosh.com/blog/push-notification-benchmarks/)
- **[Industry dataset]** Airship 2026 benchmarks: 15 industries including "Medical, health, and fitness", reported as 10th/50th/90th percentiles. Airship says the Android–iOS opt-in gap "has permanently closed" with Android 13+. Its 2025 report put the iPhone median at ~48.85%, with categories ranging ~39–61% (via search summary; the full PDF is gated). — [Airship 2026 guide](https://www.airship.com/blog/your-guide-to-airships-mobile-app-push-notification-benchmarks-for-2026/); [Airship 2025 PDF](https://growth.airship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/Airship-2025-Push-Notification-Benchmarks-EN.pdf?version=0)
- **[Secondary aggregator]** Business of Apps collects push statistics (cites Airship: Education highest on iOS ~76%, Media lowest ~39%). Treat as secondary. — [Business of Apps: Push notification statistics](https://www.businessofapps.com/marketplace/push-notifications/research/push-notifications-statistics/)
- **[Industry dataset, historical]** Urban Airship 2015 study of ~3,000 apps focused on iOS opt-in. The average iOS opt-in fell from 45% to 43% in 2014. — [Airship newsroom 2015](https://www.airship.com/newsroom/New-Urban-Airship-Study-Finds-Massive-Difference-in-Opt-In-Rates/); [Mobile Marketing Magazine](https://mobilemarketingmagazine.com/average-opt-in-rates-for-push-notifications-down-to-43-per-cent/)

### Inferences
- For a habit app, the natural in-context moment is "Remind me at 7:00?" right after the user creates their first habit. That matches Apple's own task-tracker example almost exactly.
- A hybrid works within the rules: request `.provisional` at first launch so reminders land quietly from day one, then upgrade to full alerts in context when the user sets a reminder time. Calling `requestAuthorization` with alert/sound/badge while status is provisional should then show the full prompt. (Behavior should be verified on device; Apple's doc does not spell out the upgrade path in detail.)
- Time-sensitive suits only genuinely time-bound items (e.g., a workout window starting). Misuse risks users disabling it. It requires the Time Sensitive Notifications capability/entitlement (see gaps).
- A benchmark of ~50–56% iOS opt-in is the baseline. Contextual asking and provisional delivery are the levers, but I found no rigorous public A/B data quantifying the lift.

### Gaps
- **Pre-permission priming lift:** I didn't retrieve a primary case study with a method (frequently cited anecdotes, e.g., Cluster's 2014 post, were not fetched or verified). Treat any "priming doubles opt-in" claim as an unverified vendor claim.
- I found no published method-backed data on the "best time" (session number/day) to ask. Airship's public pages don't report time-to-prompt.
- I couldn't load the Time Sensitive entitlement page body (JS-rendered). That it requires the `com.apple.developer.usernotifications.time-sensitive` entitlement is from my background knowledge, not verified here.
- The Capacitor `@capacitor/local-notifications` docs don't show a `provisional` option in `requestPermissions()`, so provisional authorization would likely need a small custom native call (verify against current plugin source).

## 5. App Tracking Transparency

### Takeaway
ATT is not relevant unless the app links its data with other companies' data for ads or shares it with data brokers. A habit app with no ad SDKs should not show the ATT prompt at all, because showing it without tracking needlessly lowers trust.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.2(i)**: "You must receive explicit permission from users via the App Tracking Transparency APIs to track their activity." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple guidance]** The HIG tracking section: must show the system alert before collecting tracking data; incentives, imitation requests, alert images and annotations "will cause rejection". — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- **[Apple rule]** HealthKit data cannot be used for advertising in any case. — [App Review Guidelines 5.1.2(vi), 5.1.3(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- If analytics stay first-party (no cross-app ad attribution SDKs), skip ATT. Re-evaluate if any marketing attribution SDK is added.

### Gaps
- None material for this app.

## 6. Accounts: delay sign-in, Sign in with Apple (4.8), account deletion (5.1.1(v)), guest/local-first, passkeys

### Takeaway
Apple says to require an account only if core functionality needs it, and to delay sign-in "for as long as possible". If you offer Google (or another social) login for the primary account, you must also offer a privacy-preserving equivalent. Since January 2024 that no longer has to be Sign in with Apple, but SIWA is the standard way to meet it. Any app that creates accounts must offer in-app account deletion (not just deactivation), and must revoke SIWA tokens. Apple pushes passkeys over passwords.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple guidance]** "Ask people to create an account only if your core functionality requires it; otherwise, let people enjoy your app or game without one." — [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Delay sign-in for as long as possible. People often abandon apps when they're forced to sign in before they can do anything useful." "Explain the benefits of creating an account… Display this message in your sign-in view." — [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- **[Apple guidance]** Name the method ("Sign In with Face ID", not "Sign In"). Avoid an in-app biometric opt-in toggle. Don't use the word "passcode" for account auth. — [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.1(v)**: "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login. If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app. Apps may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality of the app or required by law." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple guidance]** Deletion: "you must also help them delete it, not just deactivate it"; if not fully in-app, "provide a direct link to the webpage… don't bury it in your Privacy Policy or Terms"; keep in-app and web flows equally simple; scheduled deletion is allowed if immediate deletion is also offered; tell people when deletion completes; explain that Apple subscription billing continues until cancelled; "If people used Sign in with Apple to create an account… you revoke the associated tokens when they delete their account." Health-record retention required by law must be explained. — [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- **[Apple rule] 4.8** (current text): apps using "a third-party or social login service (such as Facebook Login, Google Sign-In…) to set up or authenticate the user's primary account… must also offer as an equivalent option another login service" that limits data to name and email, lets users keep their email private, and doesn't collect interactions for advertising without consent. Exempt: apps that use only the company's own account system, enterprise/education accounts, government eID, or clients for a specific third-party service. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Secondary reporting]** The January 2024 revision removed the explicit requirement that the equivalent option be *Sign in with Apple*. — [9to5Mac, 27 Jan 2024](https://9to5mac.com/2024/01/27/sign-in-with-apple-rules-app-store/); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240129-apple-sign-in-with-apple-remove)
- **[Apple guidance]** Passkeys: HIG Privacy says "Avoid relying solely on passwords for authentication. Where possible, use [passkeys] to replace passwords… Avoid inventing custom authentication schemes… prefer system-provided features like [passkeys], Sign in with Apple or Password AutoFill." Managing accounts links to Authentication Services "Supporting passkeys". — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy); [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Store sensitive information in a keychain… Never store passwords or other secure content in plain-text files." — [HIG: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.1(x)**: asking for name/email must be optional, with no features conditional on it. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- The guidance points to local-first/guest use for a habit app: let users create habits, log, and see Today without an account. Ask for an account only when it has a clear benefit (sync across devices, backup, AI coach history on server, subscription restore), framed with that benefit.
- If the AI coach runs server-side and needs identity, a reviewer may accept sign-in at the moment of first coach use, but probably not before *any* use of the app (5.1.1(v)).
- If Google login is offered, also offer Sign in with Apple (the simplest compliant "equivalent"). Email + passkey as the company's own system is also compliant.
- A "Delete account" entry in Settings is mandatory once accounts exist. Pair it with the subscription-billing explanation.

### Gaps
- I didn't fetch the HIG "Sign in with Apple" page's button rules in detail (downloaded but not extracted for this note). Standard SIWA button styling requirements apply.

## 7. Accessibility in onboarding (Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, Reduce Motion, contrast, haptics, captions) and Accessibility Nutrition Labels

### Takeaway
Onboarding is explicitly one of the "common tasks" Apple uses to judge Accessibility Nutrition Label claims ("first launch experience, login, purchase, and settings"). It must work with VoiceOver, text at 200%+, Reduce Motion and sufficient contrast if the app wants to claim those labels. Labels are voluntary for now, but Apple says they will become mandatory for submissions "over time". Haptics must be optional; video needs captions and playback controls.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple guidance]** Larger text: "Ideally, give people the option to enlarge text by at least 200 percent… Your interface can support font size enlargement either through custom UI, or by adopting Dynamic Type." — [HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Make sure your app's layout adapts to all font sizes… turn on Larger Accessibility Text Sizes… Keep text truncation to a minimum… consider using a stacked layout… Reduce the number of columns when the font size increases." — [HIG: Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- **[Apple guidance]** Contrast: Accessibility Inspector uses WCAG AA, with 4.5:1 for text up to 17 pt (the table continues with lower ratios for larger/bold text). "If your app doesn't provide this minimum contrast by default, ensure it at least provides a higher contrast color scheme when the system setting Increase Contrast is turned on… check the minimum contrast in both light and dark appearances." — [HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[Apple guidance]** Hit targets: 44×44 pt default, 28×28 pt minimum on iOS. — [HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[Apple guidance]** Motion: "Be cautious with fast-moving and blinking animations… When [Reduce Motion] is active, ensure your app or game responds by reducing automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion", e.g., "Replacing transitions in x-, y-, and z-axes with fades." — [HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[Apple guidance]** Media: "Avoid autoplaying audio and video content without also providing controls to start and stop it"; respect Dim Flashing Lights; captions give "the textual equivalent of audible information in video or audio-only content." — [HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[Apple guidance]** VoiceOver: "Describe your app's interface and content for VoiceOver" (detail moved to a dedicated VoiceOver page, March 2025). Voice Control and Switch Control need appropriately labelled elements. — [HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[Apple guidance]** Haptics: "Make haptics optional. Let people turn off or mute haptics, and make sure people can still enjoy your app or game without them"; "Avoid overusing haptics"; "Use system-provided haptic patterns according to their documented meanings"; prefer short haptics for discrete events and pair them with visual/audio feedback. — [HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)
- **[Apple guidance]** Accessibility page change log: March 7, 2025 overhaul (Dynamic Type moved to Typography, VoiceOver to its own page); June 9, 2025 added Accessibility Nutrition Labels links. — [HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **[Apple rule / program]** Accessibility Nutrition Labels (announced May 13, 2025): the declarable features are **VoiceOver, Voice Control, Larger Text (200% or more), Dark Interface, Differentiate Without Color Alone, Sufficient Contrast, Reduced Motion, Captions, Audio Descriptions**. Shown on product pages on iOS 26+ devices, declared per device type, with an optional accessibility URL. — [App Store Connect Help: Overview of Accessibility Nutrition Labels](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels); [Apple newsroom via BusinessWire, 13 May 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250513425545/en/Apple-unveils-powerful-accessibility-features-coming-later-this-year)
- **[Apple rule / program]** "To indicate support for an accessibility feature… users must be able to complete all of the common tasks of your app using that feature." "Common tasks consist of the primary functionality… plus functionality that's fundamental to using an app in general: first launch experience, login, purchase, and settings." — [App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)
- **[Apple rule / program]** "providing these labels will be voluntary to start… over time, you'll be required to share accessibility support details to submit new apps and app updates." Undeclared devices still show a section saying support isn't indicated. Misleading labels fall under 2.3 and App Review may ask for corrections. Search queries like "running apps with Larger Text" favor apps that declare support. — [App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)

### Inferences
- In a Capacitor web view: support Dynamic Type by mapping the system text size to CSS (e.g., `-apple-system-body` font or a native-bridge text-scale value), avoid fixed-height containers, and test at AX5. Use `prefers-reduced-motion` to replace orb and tour animations with fades. Give each onboarding step a heading with logical DOM order, so VoiceOver focus moves correctly on step change (move focus to the new step's heading programmatically). Label icon-only buttons. Don't trap focus behind animations.
- Animated per-area tours should honor Reduce Motion and be skippable. Any intro video needs captions and a pause control.
- A haptics on/off toggle in Settings is the simplest way to meet "Make haptics optional".
- The future mandatory requirement means onboarding, login and the paywall will be audited as "common tasks". Build them to the label criteria now.

### Gaps
- I did not extract the per-feature evaluation criteria pages (VoiceOver, Larger Text, etc.). They exist under App Store Connect Help, and a designer should read them.
- Apple has not announced a date when labels become mandatory (as of the fetched page).

## 8. Paywalls and subscriptions in onboarding (3.1.2)

### Takeaway
3.1.2(c) requires clearly describing what the user gets for the price before asking them to subscribe, and following Schedule 2 disclosure terms. In practice, reviewers reject paywalls that make the free trial more prominent than the billed price, that use a trial on/off toggle (mass rejections since January 2026, per practitioner reports), or that hide or disguise the close control. The HIG prefers letting people experience the app before a purchase prompt.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple rule] 3.1.2(a)**: subscriptions must provide "ongoing value", last at least seven days, and be available across all the user's devices. Free trials are offered via App Store Connect. "Apps that attempt to scam users will be removed… This includes apps that attempt to trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch…" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 3.1.2(c)**: "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price… Ensure you clearly communicate the requirements described in Schedule 2 of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 5.1.1(ii)**: "Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data." This means a paywall can't be unlocked by granting Health access. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple guidance]** "Prefer letting people experience your app or game before prompting them for ratings or purchases." — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **[Apple guidance]** On account deletion screens, explain that subscription billing continues through Apple until cancelled. — [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- **[Practitioner report]** Rejection under 3.1.2(c) where the paywall "promotes the free trial, introductory pricing, or introductory period for the subscription more clearly and conspicuously than the billed amount" (developer forum thread). — [Apple Developer Forums thread 841626](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/841626)
- **[Vendor claim]** From mid-January 2026, Apple began rejecting paywalls that include "a toggle to add or remove a free trial", with no guideline text change announced. — [RevenueCat: RIP toggle paywall](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/rip-toggle-paywall); [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/your-toggle-paywall-is-about-to-get-rejected.md)
- **[Practitioner report]** A one-time "special offer" shown after an onboarding soft paywall was dismissed was rejected under 5.6 (Developer Code of Conduct). Outcome unknown. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 768912](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/768912)

### Inferences
- Onboarding paywall checklist: show the full billed price and period as the most prominent price (not just a per-week equivalent); state the trial length and when billing starts; show a visible, real close/"Not now" control (unless deliberately running a hard paywall, which carries 4.2/5.1.1(v) risk for an app whose core is free-usable); include Restore Purchases, Terms and Privacy links; no trial toggle; no stacked post-dismissal guilt offers.
- Since the HIG prefers experience-first, a soft paywall after the user has set up a first habit (value shown) fits the guidance better than a paywall before any use.

### Gaps
- Schedule 2 text was not fetched. The specific disclosure items (title, length, price, renewal terms) are standard but not quoted here.
- No official Apple statement on the toggle-paywall policy. Only vendor reports.

## 9. Capacitor / web-view considerations

### Takeaway
Apple's 4.2/4.2.2 bar ("elevate it beyond a repackaged website") is the main risk for web-wrapped apps. Native HealthKit, local notifications, haptics and widgets are the strongest evidence of "app-like" functionality. On the Capacitor side: control the splash so it hides as soon as the web app is ready, not on a fixed timer. Haptics and local notifications come via official plugins, whose permissions still trigger the same system prompts and must follow the same priming rules.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple rule] 4.2**: "Your app should include features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website. If your app is not particularly useful, unique, or 'app-like,' it doesn't belong on the App Store." **4.2.2**: "Other than catalogs, apps shouldn't primarily be marketing materials, advertisements, web clippings, content aggregators, or a collection of links." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Apple rule] 2.5.1**: use public APIs; HealthKit "should integrate with the Health app" and be indicated in the app description. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Capacitor docs]** Splash Screen: "By default, the Splash Screen is set to automatically hide after 500 ms. If you want to be sure the splash screen never disappears before your app is ready, set `launchAutoHide` to `false`; the splash screen will then stay visible until manually hidden. For the best user experience, your app should call `hide()` as soon as possible." `launchShowDuration` sets a fixed time instead. — [Capacitor: Splash Screen API](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/splash-screen)
- **[Capacitor docs]** Haptics API offers `impact`, `notification`, `vibrate`, `selectionStart/Changed/End`. "On devices that don't have Taptic Engine or Vibrator, the API calls will resolve without performing any action." — [Capacitor: Haptics API](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/haptics)
- **[Capacitor docs]** Local Notifications: `checkPermissions()` / `requestPermissions()`; `presentationOptions` (badge/sound/alert) is iOS-only config; Android 12+ exact alarms need `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`; structured errors since v8.3.0. No provisional option is documented. — [Capacitor: Local Notifications API](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/local-notifications)
- **[Apple guidance]** Launch "instantly", within "a couple of seconds"; the launch screen should match the first screen; show placeholder content as soon as possible. — [HIG: Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching); [HIG: Loading](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading)

### Inferences
- Splash flow: native launch storyboard (solid background color matching the web app's first paint) → Capacitor splash with `launchAutoHide: false` → JS calls `SplashScreen.hide()` on first meaningful paint. Avoid a long branded splash. Put any animated brand moment at the start of onboarding instead (HIG Launching).
- Bundle the web assets locally rather than loading a remote URL. This supports instant launch and avoids "repackaged website" perception under 4.2 (inference; Apple does not name Capacitor).
- Haptics via `@capacitor/haptics` should be gated behind a user setting (HIG "Make haptics optional") and should use `notification(success)` only for real successes.
- Permission prompts triggered from JS (Health, notifications) still show the system sheets, so the same priming and in-context rules apply. For HealthKit background delivery, native AppDelegate setup is required (see section 3).
- Web-view text does not automatically follow Dynamic Type. Explicit work is needed (see section 7).

### Gaps
- Apple publishes no hard launch-time threshold in the HIG ("a couple of seconds" only). The often-cited 400 ms first-frame target from WWDC launch-performance sessions was not verified in this research.
- I found no Apple statement specific to Capacitor/Cordova. 4.2 is applied case by case.
