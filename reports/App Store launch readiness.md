# Close these gaps before submitting Logbook

Logbook is not submittable today, but every gap is fixable in the build, on the server and in four public documents, and none of them changes what the app is. The governing text is the App Review Guidelines revision of October 16, 2026, and four of its rules carry most of the risk for a Capacitor app that reads Apple Health and calls Claude: **2.1 completeness** (Apple ties over 40% of unresolved review issues to it), **5.1.2(i) explicit permission before personal data reaches third-party AI**, the HealthKit limits in 5.1.3 and the Developer Program License Agreement, and **4.2 on web-wrapped apps**. A spot check of the repo found concrete blockers: no HealthKit entitlements file, no app privacy manifest, a committed Swift package list that omits the Health and notifications plugins, no hosted privacy policy or support page, no "check with a doctor" reminder, an AI consent that is on by default and does not cover every flow to Anthropic, and dev screens and feature flags that the new guideline 2.5.19 treats as dormant features. Outside App Review, two things bind harder than Apple does. Strava's API terms of June 1, 2026 cap cached data at seven days, ban any AI use of Strava data including derived values, and hold a new app to 10 athletes until Strava approves it. US health-data law (Washington, Nevada, the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule) reaches a solo developer with no size threshold, and Washington adds a private right of action. The launch shape the evidence supports is iPhone only, free, United States storefront first, light appearance declared honestly, Strava either compliant and approved or out of the 1.0 binary, and preferably an LLC as the seller. Almost every Apple, Strava and Anthropic claim below was re-read on its primary source on 2026-11-10; what remains open is a short list of questions Apple has never answered in writing, chiefly whether a health summary sent to a general AI provider satisfies the HealthKit third-party rule.

## Four review rules carry most of the rejection risk

Source types matter in this report, so each claim is labelled in passing. An **Apple rule** is binding text: the App Review Guidelines, the Developer Program License Agreement (DPLA), or an App Store Connect upload check. **Apple guidance** is documentation, the Human Interface Guidelines (HIG) and help pages. **Law** is statute or regulator text. **Vendor terms** are Strava's and Anthropic's own pages. **Practitioner** means forum threads, law firm summaries and vendor blogs. **Repo** means a spot check of /home/user/LogBook on 2026-11-10, not an audit.

The guidelines page reads "Last Updated: October 16, 2026" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Two revisions since late 2025 touch Logbook directly. On November 13, 2025 Apple added third-party AI to 5.1.2(i) and added 4.1(c) on other developers' brands ([Apple news, Nov 13, 2025](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ey6d8onl)). On October 16, 2026 it added 2.5.19 on hidden, dormant or undocumented features and 2.3.14 on creative assets ([Apple news, Oct 16, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=92th7e5i)). Any checklist older than November 2025 misses the AI rule, which is the single most relevant change for an app whose assistant calls Anthropic from the server. The scale of review is worth knowing: in 2025 Apple reviewed **9,100,620 submissions and rejected 2,093,244**, with 1,354,418 rejections citing a Performance guideline, the section that holds 2.1 and 2.3 ([2025 App Store Transparency Report](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025/)). Apple reviews at least 50% of submissions within 24 hours and 90% within 48 hours, and says "over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1: App Completeness" ([App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)). The cheapest wins are therefore mechanical.

### Completeness and the new ban on dormant features

Guideline 2.1(a) (Apple rule) requires final builds with working URLs, no placeholder content, a live backend and demo account details when the app has a login ([Guideline 2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.1)). Apple's guidance adds that the demo credentials must work through review, that an authentication code step can cost three to five business days, and that review notes must list "the external services, tools, or platforms the app uses to deliver its core functionality (for example ... AI services)" ([Complete your review information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review)). The reviewer's phone will hold little Apple Health data and no Strava account. Logbook therefore needs a **permanent review account with weeks of seeded plan, habit, goal, journal and server-side health data**, Health and Strava screens that look finished when empty, and a short screen recording of the Health connect flow, since Apple says to "be prepared to provide a demo video" for features that are hard to replicate ([App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)).

Guideline 2.5.19 (Apple rule) says "Don't include any hidden, dormant, or undocumented features in your app" and names removal from the store and termination of the developer account as the penalty ([Guideline 2.5.19](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.19)). The three adjectives are separate tests, so code that ships switched off is still dormant and a line in the review notes does not cure it. The repo holds a dev gallery (apps/web/src/app/dev/UiGallery.tsx), feature flags such as `log.features.claude` (apps/web/src/features/plan/PlanBuilder.tsx), and, per the project's task history, COROS groundwork, a mock Health bridge and a debug build label. Each one must be removed from the release bundle or shipped fully on and described. This rule also decides the Strava question later in this report: an integration that cannot be offered to the public belongs out of the binary, not behind a flag.

### Explicit permission before anything reaches Anthropic

Guideline 5.1.2(i) (Apple rule) reads: "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so" ([Guideline 5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.2)). Developers report the rejection template that enforces it: the app "does not clearly explain what data is sent, identify who the data is sent to, and ask the user's permission before sharing the data", and one developer who had built a consent screen was still rejected repeatedly, the reviewer writing "We were not presented with the consent prompt on launch or anywhere else in the app" ([Apple Developer Forums 816140](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/816140); [Forums 820209](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/820209)). Those threads are practitioner evidence read through a summarising fetch, because the forum pages return a bot check.

Logbook already does the hard part. The privacy page (apps/web/src/features/auth/PrivacyFacts.tsx) names "Claude, by Anthropic" and separates what is sent from what is never sent, and the Health settings screen has a separate coach permission. Three gaps remain against the words "explicit permission before doing so". First, a code comment in apps/web/src/features/auth/Start.tsx describes the choice as "on by default"; **the consent must be an affirmative act, off until the person turns it on**. Second, the privacy page lists flows that sit outside the training-data switch: one old journal entry or learning a day for "Remember when", and files, photos and links handed to the assistant. Chat text typed to the assistant is also personal data, so the disclosure has to come before the first message. Every call to Anthropic needs to sit behind a recorded permission, enforced on the server, and with permission off nothing personal should leave. Third, the reviewer signs in with a pre-made account and skips signup, so the permission step must also appear on first use of the assistant for any account with no recorded answer, and the review notes must say where it is.

### Apple Health data: disclose every type, send the minimum

Guideline 5.1.3 (Apple rule) bars health data from advertising, marketing and use-based data mining, requires that "You must disclose the specific health data that you are collecting from the device", and forbids storing personal health information in iCloud ([Guideline 5.1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.3)). DPLA section 3.3.3(H) (Apple rule, version dated October 29, 2026) bars disclosure to a third party "without prior express end-user consent, and then only for purposes of enabling the third party to provide health, motion, fitness ... services" ([DPLA](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/apple-developer-program/Apple-Developer-Program-License-Agreement-English.pdf)). Apple's HealthKit documentation says the same in plainer words: "Even with permission, you can only share information to a third party if they also provide a health or fitness service to the user" ([Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)).

Sending a health summary to Anthropic is the sharpest edge in the whole launch. The defensible position is that Anthropic acts as Logbook's processor to produce the user's own coaching answer, the user gave express consent on a screen that names Anthropic and the data, Anthropic's terms bar training on it, and only a derived summary is sent, never raw samples. That is an interpretation. No Apple source confirms it, and no 2026 rejection report for this exact pattern was found.

One disclosure gap is concrete. The Health purpose string names "sleep, heart rate, HRV, workouts and activity", but the plugin (packages/capacitor-health, HealthReader.swift) also requests resting heart rate, respiratory rate, blood oxygen, VO2 max, walking heart rate, body mass, wrist temperature, energy, distances and workout routes, and no route-reading code exists. Either trim the requested set to what 1.0 uses or widen the sentence, the connect screen and the policy to match. The string should also say the data is stored on Logbook's server, because 5.1.1(ii) asks that purpose strings "clearly and completely describe your use of the data" ([Guideline 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.1)). The HIG (Apple guidance) adds that the app's own Health sheet should explain and then hand off to Apple's permission sheet, not replicate it, and that copy should say "Apple Health", never "HealthKit" ([HIG HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit)).

### Scores, the coach and the word "medical"

Guideline 1.4.1 (Apple rule) says apps "must clearly disclose data and methodology to support accuracy claims relating to health measurements" and "should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions" ([Guideline 1.4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#1.4.1)). Logbook shows recovery, strain and sleep scores, and a repo search for "medical advice", "doctor" and similar found no such reminder anywhere. The fix has two parts: a "How this is worked out" note on each score that names Apple Health as the source and gives the method in plain words, and a short standing line in the Health screens, the assistant, onboarding and the terms. Suggested copy in house style: "Logbook is not medical advice. Check with a doctor before you change training for a health reason." Apple does not publish what wording or placement satisfies the sentence. Choosing the Health & Fitness category also triggers a declaration that has been required for new apps since March 26, 2026: regulated medical device status, where Logbook answers No and must keep its copy free of diagnosis and treatment claims so that the answer stays true ([Apple news, Mar 26, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=nyqbfz1y)).

### The web shell, the individual seller and the smaller rules

Guideline 4.2 (Apple rule) asks for "features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website" ([Guideline 4.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#4.2)), and Apple's common-issue list names "Websites served in an iOS app, web content that is not formatted for iOS" ([App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)). Apple publishes no objective threshold. One practitioner report describes a Capacitor app with native plugins rejected under 4.2 in January 2026, but its core flow ran inside a third-party web page ([Forums 812889](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812889)). Logbook differs where it counts: the web build is bundled (`webDir: 'dist'`, no `server.url`, no live-update plugin, which also satisfies 2.5.2), there is no public website the app merely frames, and Apple Health sync is something a browser cannot do. The review notes should say that in plain words. The page-by-page audit should remove every browser tell: a WebKit error or blank page, selection handles on buttons, a grey tap flash, pinch zoom, a `window.alert` titled with a localhost name, the keyboard covering an input.

Guideline 5.1.1(ix) (Apple rule) says apps "that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer" ([Guideline 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.1)). Apple's App Review page words it more strongly: such apps "must be submitted by the legal entity" ([App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)). Apple Health is optional in Logbook and the app is not healthcare, which lowers the risk, but health data on a server fits the phrase. An individual enrollment also shows the developer's personal legal name as seller ([Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)). Forming an LLC before the first upload removes both problems.

The remaining rules are satisfied or nearly so. Account deletion (5.1.1(v)) exists; it must delete the whole record without email or a support flow, state timing, and reach Apple Health records, AI chats, uploads and Strava tokens ([Account deletion guidance](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion)). The mandatory account needs one sentence of justification in the notes: data lives on the server and the assistant runs there. Sign in with Apple is not required, because 4.8 exempts an app that "exclusively uses your company's own account setup and sign-in systems" ([Guideline 4.8](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#4.8)). Reminders are local notifications, and DPLA Attachment 1 binds those by name: optional, no "sensitive personal or confidential information" on the lock screen, no promotion of the app or new features without explicit opt-in ([DPLA](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/apple-developer-program/Apple-Developer-Program-License-Agreement-English.pdf)). Guideline 5.2.5 and the HIG forbid ring-like elements that show Move, Exercise or Stand data, so every ring in the chart kit needs a check ([Guideline 5.2.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.2.5)). Guideline 4.1 forbids copying another app's look, which matters because internal task names describe "Bevel-faithful" components and a "Strava-style" strip. With no purchases, Section 3 asks only for a line in the notes; a later subscription would have to use In-App Purchase under 3.1.1 and could never gate reminders, Health access or Strava sync.

## The build and the store record each have named blockers

### Four files stand between the repo and a valid archive

The toolchain floor (Apple rule) is Xcode 26 with the iOS 26 SDK since April 28, 2026, and a deployment target of iOS 13 or later since September 9, 2026 ([Upcoming requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/)). Apple's Submitting page adds that from April 2027 uploads must be built with the iOS 27 SDK and target iOS 15 or later, with no day given ([Submitting](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/submitting/)). Capacitor 8 itself requires Xcode 26.0 or later and a 15.0 target ([Capacitor 8 update guide](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/updating/8-0)). Logbook's project already meets all of this. What it lacks is in the committed state of apps/web/ios.

**No entitlements file exists**, and the project sets no `CODE_SIGN_ENTITLEMENTS`. HealthKit authorization needs the `com.apple.developer.healthkit` entitlement, which Xcode adds when the capability is enabled ([Configuring HealthKit access](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-healthkit-access)). Commit the file so an archive does not depend on one Mac's local state. Leave Clinical Health Records unticked, since "App Review may reject apps that enable the Clinical Health Records capability if the app doesn't actually use the health record data", and delete the `healthkit` entry Xcode adds to required device capabilities, because Logbook works without Health ([Setting up HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/setting-up-healthkit)). The background-delivery entitlement belongs in the release that ships observer queries; no such code exists today.

**No app privacy manifest exists.** Since May 1, 2024 App Store Connect does not accept apps that use a required-reason API without declaring it ([Describing use of required reason API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api)). The one such use found is file timestamps in `@capacitor/filesystem`, whose README names category FileTimestamp with reason `C617.1`. The manifest needs `NSPrivacyTracking` false, no tracking-domains key, that one API entry, and collected data types that mirror the privacy label. Apple's technote lists what makes a manifest invalid, including an empty `NSPrivacyAccessedAPITypes` array ([TN3181](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3181-debugging-invalid-privacy-manifest)). Capacitor and Cordova are on Apple's list of SDKs that must carry their own signed manifests ([Third-party SDK requirements](https://developer.apple.com/support/third-party-SDK-requirements/)). The 8.5.3 binaries do carry them and are signed, but their manifests contain exactly that empty array. Whether App Store Connect flags it is unverified, so read the first upload email.

**The committed Swift package list is stale.** apps/web/ios/App/CapApp-SPM/Package.swift lists eight plugins and omits `@capacitor/local-notifications` and the local Health plugin, although both are in package.json. A clean checkout archived without `npx cap sync ios` builds an app whose Health and reminder calls fail with "Plugin not implemented" ([Capacitor troubleshooting](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/ios/troubleshooting)), which is a 2.1 rejection.

**Info.plist and build settings need five edits.** `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY` is "1,2", so the app is universal today and owes a 13-inch iPad screenshot; set it to 1 for an iPhone-only launch, and still test on an iPad because 2.4.1 says "iPhone apps should run on iPad whenever possible" and developers report iPhone-only apps being reviewed on iPad ([Guideline 2.4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.4.1); [Forums 802147](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/802147)). `UIUserInterfaceStyle` is `Dark` while the web UI is light, so keyboards, alerts and the Health sheet come up dark over a pale app; Apple's documented opt-out value for a light-only app is `Light` ([Choosing a specific interface style](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/choosing-a-specific-interface-style-for-your-ios-app)). `UIRequiredDeviceCapabilities` holds the template value `armv7`; it filters no current device and `arm64` is the accurate value, but updates "can only maintain or relax capability requirements", so pick one before the first upload ([UIRequiredDeviceCapabilities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/uirequireddevicecapabilities)). `MARKETING_VERSION` is 1.0 where Apple's key reference asks for three integers, so use 1.0.0 ([CFBundleShortVersionString](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/cfbundleshortversionstring)). And no signing team is committed. Everything else checked out: the launch storyboard key that iOS 27 SDK uploads now require, the scene manifest, default App Transport Security, dSYM output, an opaque 1024 px icon, and `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` set to false for an app that uses only OS-provided HTTPS ([Complying with encryption export regulations](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/complying-with-encryption-export-regulations)).

### What a web view adds that no checklist covers

Three WKWebView behaviours need device tests, not plist edits. Web storage is evictable: Capacitor's own guide says Local Storage "must be considered transient" and that IndexedDB behaves the same on iOS ([Capacitor storage guide](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/guides/storage)), and WebKit deletes an origin's data "as a whole" under storage pressure ([WebKit storage policy](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)). Logbook keeps its offline outbox in IndexedDB and its onboarding flags, reminder switch and pre-signup consent choice in localStorage, so each needs a safe default when missing; a reminder switch that silently falls back to off is the kind of bug this causes. Streaming is undocumented: the CapacitorHttp page says nothing about incremental response bodies ([Capacitor Http](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/http)), so the live assistant answer, a cancel mid-answer and a large photo upload must be proven on a release build. And the iOS 27 SDK changes resizing: `UIRequiresFullScreen` "no longer opts your app out of resizing on either platform", iPhone included ([TN3192](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3192-migrating-your-app-from-the-deprecated-uirequiresfullscreen-key)), and iOS 27 lets a person grant Health access to "only a limited window of recent data" ([Authorizing access to health data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)). Baselines must tolerate short history, and the layout must reflow when the viewport changes.

Logbook has no crash or error reporting. Apple's Organizer covers native crashes, hangs and launch time at no privacy cost, but a JavaScript exception or a blank web view never appears there. A small first-party error report from the existing ErrorBoundary closes that blind spot, and if those reports are kept they count as collected diagnostics on the privacy label ([App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)). Reminders are safe against the system cap of 64 pending notifications, but since plugin 8.3.0 `schedule()` requests permission by itself, so every call must sit behind the stored opt-in ([Capacitor Local Notifications](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/local-notifications)). The API host must also work on an IPv6-only network ([Guideline 2.5.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.5.5)); test it rather than assume it.

The DSA question is asked "Even if you don't distribute apps in the EU", and the same page says a developer who distributes only outside the EU is not acting as a trader on the App Store. Health & Fitness is the accurate primary category, with Productivity as secondary. The age rating should come out at 9+ for "Health or Wellness Topics", or 13+ if "infrequent medical or treatment information" is declared, which is the honest answer for an open-ended coach; Apple's questionnaire has no AI question at all, and from November 16, 2026 a "virtual companion that engages a user in sexual or romantic content" forces 18+, so the system prompt should rule that persona out ([Apple news, Nov 2, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=yxkh08us)). The privacy label should declare email and name, Health, Fitness, other user content, photos if attachments are kept, and a user ID, all linked to the user for App Functionality, with no tracking; Anthropic and Strava are reached from the server, not through embedded code, so they add no rows and belong in the policy and consent text. The first external TestFlight build goes through beta review, which makes it a cheap rehearsal, and it means the demo account, notes and policy must exist before that build, not only before store submission. The review notes should cover sign-in and why an account is needed, where Apple Health connects and every type read, the AI permission step and that the assistant is not user-to-user chat, Strava as optional, local reminders, and the deletion path.

## Health-data law and Strava's terms reach further than App Review

### One policy for Apple, a second page for Washington and Nevada

Apple 5.1.1(i) (Apple rule) requires a policy that states what is collected, how, all uses, that third parties give "the same or equal protection", retention and deletion, and how to revoke consent ([Guideline 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.1.1)). Strava's API Policy (vendor terms) adds that the policy must meet GDPR and UK GDPR requirements, be reachable by prominent links, and state that Strava may collect usage data ([Strava API Policy](https://www.strava.com/legal/api_policy)). One hosted policy written to a GDPR-style outline covers both if it names Anthropic, Strava and Fly.io and gives a retention period for each data class, including Fly's volume snapshots, kept five days by default ([Fly volumes](https://docs.fly.io/volumes/overview)).

The sentence most likely to be read as deceptive is already in the app: `PRIVACY_PROMISE = 'Your data is yours. It is never sold or shared.'`, in the same file that lists data "Sent to Claude, by Anthropic". Apple's own rule calls that sharing, and the FTC (law) treats a mismatch between privacy statements and data flows as deceptive ([FTC health privacy guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/collecting-using-or-sharing-consumer-health-information-look-hipaa-ftc-act-health-breach)). A narrower promise is true and still strong: "Your data is yours. It is never sold. It goes to Claude only when you say so."

Washington's My Health My Data Act (law) has no revenue or user floor. It covers "vital signs" and measurements, requires opt-in consent to collect and a separate consent to share, sets a 45-day clock for access and deletion requests, and requires a consumer health data privacy policy linked from the homepage, which for an app includes its settings ([RCW 19.373](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373&full=true)). The Washington Attorney General says the link must be separate and that violations are enforceable "through private action" ([WA AG FAQ](https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy)). That private right of action is the largest single legal exposure for a solo developer in this report. Nevada's law is similar without the private action, and its deletion clock is shorter: 30 days after authenticating the request, with processors notified ([NRS 603A](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-603A.html)). One short stand-alone page serves both states. Whether an individual is a "legal entity" under the Washington definition is a question for a lawyer; the cautious reading is yes.

The FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (law) probably covers Logbook: FTC guidance says a fitness app that can draw from the user and a wearable is "likely a vendor of personal health records", and that a breach "is not limited to cybersecurity intrusions" ([FTC HBNR guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-ftcs-health-breach-notification-rule-0)). An AI call made with consent off, or an error tracker that captures request bodies, is a reportable event under that reading. Notice runs to individuals within 60 calendar days, and the current penalty is up to $53,088 per violation. The CCPA does not apply at launch: none of its thresholds is near, the first two being revenue over $26,625,000 and the personal information of 100,000 California residents or households ([CPPA](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html)). GDPR applies to a non-EU controller that offers a service to people in the Union "irrespective of whether a payment ... is required" and then demands explicit consent for health data, an EU representative, an impact assessment and 72-hour breach notice ([GDPR Art. 3](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/); [Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/); [Art. 27](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-27-gdpr/); [Art. 33](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-33-gdpr/)). **A United States only launch removes that whole layer** and reduces the DSA step to a declaration.

### Minimum age is a real decision, and state age laws are already live

COPPA (law) does not require a general-audience app to ask ages, but an operator that learns a user is under 13 must get parental consent or delete the data, and a checkbox such as "I am over 12" is not a neutral screen ([FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)). Logbook asks no age today. It needs a stated minimum in the terms, the policy and at signup, and Apple requires the store rating to be overridden upward when the terms set a higher minimum age ([Set an app age rating](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/set-an-app-age-rating)). The choice is 18+ or 13+. Anthropic's rules define a minor as anyone under 18 and attach safeguards (age verification, content filtering, monitoring, a public COPPA statement) to products that serve them ([Anthropic guidance on minors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9307344-responsible-use-of-anthropic-s-models-guidelines-for-organizations-serving-minors)). 18+ is the simplest position for health data plus AI; 13+ widens the audience at the cost of those safeguards.

State app-store age laws now put duties on developers. Apple reports that new Apple Accounts in Texas have been subject to SB 2420 since June 4, 2026 ([Apple news, Jun 3, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=sg176nne)), and that age categories are shared for new accounts in Utah from May 6, 2026 and Louisiana from July 1, 2026 ([Apple news, Feb 24, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=f5zj08ey)). Apple says that in those regions "you must check the age of the people using your app" through the Declared Age Range API, and to "consult your legal counsel" ([Age assurance](https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance)). The statutes themselves were not read, so what they require of a free, non-social app is unverified. This is not an App Review gate; it is a legal one, and it would need a small native plugin.

### Anthropic: four small duties and one accurate sentence

Anthropic's Commercial Terms (vendor terms) allow an API customer to power products for its own end users, bar Anthropic from training on customer content, and require the customer to notify users "that factual assertions in Outputs should not be relied upon without independently checking their accuracy" ([Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)). The agreement is with "you or the organization ... that you represent", so an individual can sign. The Usage Policy, effective November 12, 2026, requires consumer chatbots to disclose that the user is talking to AI at the start of each session or in the interface, and bars medical "High-risk AI Recommendation" output (diagnosis, treatment, interpreting symptoms) without qualified human review, while excluding "Wellness content, such as education on sleep, stress, nutrition, or exercise" ([Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)). In practice: a visible "AI" label on the assistant, one line that it can be wrong, system-prompt rules that refuse diagnosis and send symptoms to a doctor, and tests for those prompts. The same label covers the state chatbot disclosure laws that law firm summaries describe, though those statutes were not read.

The policy's sentence about retention must be exact. Anthropic deletes API inputs and outputs "within 30 days of receipt or generation", keeps content flagged for policy violations up to 2 years and safety scores up to 7 years ([Anthropic privacy center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)). Zero data retention exists only by arrangement with Anthropic sales ([API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). So the policy says: sent to answer you, not used to train, deleted within 30 days unless flagged or required by law. It does not say nothing is stored.

### Strava is the tightest constraint and the least met

Strava's API Agreement and Policy took effect June 1, 2026 and enter App Review through guideline 5.2.2, which requires that use of a third-party service be "specifically permitted" by its terms ([Guideline 5.2.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.2.2)). Policy 5.3 bans using Strava data "directly or indirectly" in the "development, training, evaluation, or operation of any AI Application", including "ingestion into a context window", and extends to "Any data derived from, aggregated from, anonymized from, or generated using Strava Data". Section 5.10 bars transfer to AI providers "even if a user of your Developer Application consents". Section 5.4 bars analytics and says "You may not combine Strava Data with other customer data". Section 6.2 limits caching to seven days, 6.3 requires deletions on Strava to be reflected within 48 hours, and 2.5 and 7.4 require deletion on disconnect or revocation within 30 days with written confirmation to the user ([Strava API Policy](https://www.strava.com/legal/api_policy)).

Logbook's rule that Strava data never reaches the AI matches 5.3, and assistant.ts states it. The rest does not yet match. apps/api/src/services/strava.ts pulls **120 days of activities** into a table and soft-deletes vanished ones; the webhook handler reacts only to activity events and ignores Strava-side deauthorization, which Strava signals with `"authorized": "false"` and expects answered within two seconds ([Strava webhooks](https://developers.strava.com/docs/webhooks/)); disconnect removes the account row but was not seen deleting stored activities; tokens sit in plain columns and the webhook verify token defaults to `logbook-verify`; no official "Connect with Strava" button, attribution or "View on Strava" link appears in the source ([Strava Brand Guidelines](https://developers.strava.com/guidelines/)). The AI wall also needs a test, not a comment: if a plan session is marked done because a Strava run matched it and that mark is sent to Claude, the mark is derived data.

Two facts make Strava a launch gate in its own right. A new Strava API app has an athlete capacity of 1, self-upgradable to 10, and anything beyond needs Strava's review with screenshots, where "increased access is not guaranteed" ([Strava rate limits](https://developers.strava.com/docs/rate-limits/)). Strava also says "A Strava subscription is a prerequisite for creating an app" ([Strava getting started](https://developers.strava.com/docs/getting-started/)). And the product plan to merge Strava runs with Apple Health workouts and plan sessions runs straight into 5.4 and 5.5; how Strava applies those to a user's own charts is stated nowhere and should be asked of Strava in writing. The lowest-risk design makes Apple Health the system of record for training analysis and AI, and shows Strava as a recent, unmerged list with a link back. Until the fixes land and Strava approves the capacity, Strava connect should not be in the 1.0 binary, because 2.5.19 makes a hidden switch the wrong answer. Strava also bars charging users for Strava-based functionality, which constrains any later paid tier.

### Apple's marks, the licence, and where a lawyer earns the fee

Apple's marketing rules (Apple guidance) say to write "the Apple Health app" or "Works with the Apple Health app", and "Do not refer to Apple Health data. Health data belongs to the user, not Apple" ([Works with Apple Health](https://developer.apple.com/health-fitness/works-with-apple-health/)). Apple, Strava and Claude names stay out of the app name, subtitle, icon and keywords under 2.3.7 and 4.1(c). Apple's standard EULA licenses the binary automatically; a custom EULA must carry Apple's ten minimum terms, including the developer's name and address ([Minimum terms](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/dev/minterms/)). The practical set is Apple's standard EULA plus Logbook's own Terms of Service accepted at signup, with health and AI consents kept out of the terms because Washington rejects consent taken through "a general or broad terms of use agreement".

Security expectations are "reasonable" measures: encryption in transit and at rest, salted slow password hashing, least privilege ([FTC mobile health best practices](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/mobile-health-app-developers-ftc-best-practices)). Logbook's scrypt hashing meets the password point and Fly volumes are encrypted at rest by default. What is missing is written: a one-page breach runbook with its clocks (Strava 24 hours, GDPR 72 hours if ever relevant, the FTC rule 60 days, state laws by residence) and a retention table the policy can quote. A lawyer is worth paying for six things: forming the entity, the Terms of Service, the Washington and multi-state health-data position, the minimum age and state age-law decision, the Strava combination question if the product depends on it, and any EU or UK launch. The rest is engineering and plain writing.

## A web view has to earn the polish UIKit gives away

The HIG is guidance, not a rule set, but App Review lists "Substandard user interface" among common issues and tells developers to follow "our design guides" ([App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)), and Apple's featuring factors name user experience, UI design and accessibility ([Getting featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)). For a web-view app the weight falls on what native apps get for free. The page-by-page audit should test each screen against the following, all Apple guidance unless marked.

Every tappable control needs a hit region of at least **44 by 44 pt** and a press state ([HIG Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)); the CSS has a 44 px token but also `min-height` values of 20, 28, 32, 36 and 40 px. No text should be smaller than 11 pt, with body at 17 pt ([HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)); the CSS has one 9 px size and fifteen 10 px sizes. Contrast must reach 4.5:1 for text up to 17 pt and 3:1 for larger or bold text ([HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)); Logbook's pale palette with muted secondary text has not been measured. Nothing may be carried by colour alone, which touches status chips, heart rate zones, done and missed sessions and chart series. No content may sit under the Dynamic Island, the home indicator or the app's own floating bars, and the keyboard may never hide the focused field; Logbook owns every inset itself (`viewport-fit=cover`, `Keyboard.resize: 'none'`), so each screen and sheet needs checking on a home-button phone and a Dynamic Island phone. Sheets need an obvious exit, with Cancel on the leading edge and Done on the trailing edge under the HIG update of March 24, 2026, one sheet at a time, and a discard prompt when text was typed ([HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)). The tab bar must keep labels on all six tabs at the narrowest width; the HIG says "it's generally easier to navigate among fewer tabs", and six is the one structural navigation question worth settling before launch ([HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). Every screen shows something at once, every empty screen says what belongs there and offers the next action, and errors sit next to the problem without "oops", codes or "we" ([HIG Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing)). Numeric fields need the number pad; no `inputMode="numeric"` or `"decimal"` was found. A permission is asked for only when its feature is used, and a custom screen before a system alert gets one button ([HIG Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)).

A light-only app passes review; no guideline mentions Dark Mode. Apple still calls the opt-out temporary and "strongly" encourages support, and a light-only app cannot claim the Dark Interface label. A dark theme is the largest single gap against featured quality and is not needed for 1.0.

### Accessibility labels are voluntary today and must be true

Accessibility Nutrition Labels are "voluntary to start", and Apple says "over time, you'll be required" to provide them, with no date. A feature may be declared only if people can complete all common tasks with it: the app's primary functions plus first launch, login and settings. An undeclared section still appears on the product page and says support has not been indicated ([Overview of Accessibility Nutrition Labels](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels)). Accuracy falls under guideline 2.3. For Logbook the realistic first set, after fixes and device testing, is Sufficient Contrast, Differentiate Without Color Alone, Reduced Motion and Larger Text.

Larger Text is the hardest of those. The claim needs body text to reach "at least 200% or the maximum font size for the system" without overlap or severe truncation, and Apple says a web-view app must provide the scaling itself ([Larger Text criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/larger-text-evaluation-criteria)). Logbook already scales its root font from an `-apple-system-body` probe (apps/web/src/app/dynamicType.ts), with no ceiling, so the largest setting gives about 310%; roughly 40 CSS rules use fixed pixel sizes that will not scale, and fixed-height rows, the tab capsule, charts and sheets are the likely breakpoints. VoiceOver and Voice Control need the most work: real button and switch roles, dialogs that trap focus, a text alternative for every chart and ring, and a tap or menu route for every drag, long press and swipe, which covers the draggable day timeline, hold menus and the /start pager ([VoiceOver criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/voiceover-evaluation-criteria)). WCAG 2.2 is a W3C standard, not an Apple requirement, though Apple borrows its contrast numbers. No study of failures in web-view apps exists; the best proxy is WebAIM's 2026 scan, where six error types, led by low-contrast text on 83.9% of pages, made up 96% of detected failures ([WebAIM Million](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)). Running the label evaluation now doubles as the page audit.

### Product page, ratings and support

The first one to three screenshots appear in search results, so they carry the pitch ([Product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)). App Review approves only submissions where "more than half of screenshots show the app in use" ([2.3.3 guidance](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/2-3-3-screenshots)), and 2.3.9 requires fictional account data. Capture from the seeded account with no Strava activities on screen, so nothing implies Strava data feeds the coach. The keyword field is 100 bytes and may not contain other apps' or companies' names, which rules out Strava, Claude, Whoop and Apple Health.

Rating requests must use Apple's API, "and we will disallow custom review prompts" ([Guideline 5.6.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#5.6.1)). A "Do you enjoy Logbook?" gate that routes unhappy people away is filtered feedback, which the guidelines name as manipulation. The system shows the prompt at most three times in 365 days ([RequestReviewAction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/requestreviewaction)); ask only after proven engagement, never in onboarding. Guideline 1.5 requires an easy way to reach the developer in the app and at the Support URL, and Settings has no contact row today. A featuring nomination is free and should be filed at least three weeks before launch ([Nominate your app](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring)).

### The test matrix and what to watch after launch

Apple's release-testing document is close to a ready-made matrix: the release build, on real devices, launched from the Home Screen with no debugger, as a fresh install and as an update, on a slow link, offline, on IPv6 only, in Low Power Mode, under memory pressure, and across 12-hour and 24-hour regions ([Testing a release build](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/testing-a-release-build)). Add each permission denied, each accessibility setting one at a time, and interrupted flows such as a force quit during plan build, an AI answer or the Strava round trip. The biggest scoping lever is the deployment target. At 15.0 the matrix includes 4-inch phones and iOS 15 WebKit, yet Apple measured **iOS 26 on 79% and iOS 18 on 14% of iPhones** on June 7, 2026 ([App Store usage](https://developer.apple.com/support/app-store/)), and Apple's own tip is that raising the target shrinks the matrix.

After launch, Xcode Organizer and App Store Connect Analytics give crashes, hangs, launch time and peer benchmarks, but Apple publishes no pass mark. One vendor report puts the median app at 99.95% crash-free sessions ([Luciq 2026 benchmarks](https://www.luciq.ai/mobile-app-performance-benchmarks-2026)); it is practitioner data from large apps and directional only.

## What is verified, what Apple contradicts, and what nobody has answered

The research was fact-checked on 2026-11-10 against primary sources. Six interpretive questions have no primary answer anywhere. ## Conclusion

One consent design answers four regimes at once. Apple's 5.1.2(i), Washington's opt-in standard, the FTC's reading of unauthorized disclosure as a breach, and the HealthKit third-party rule all ask the same thing in different words: a specific, affirmative, recorded choice before each kind of data leaves for a named recipient, enforced where the request is built. Building that once on the server, with every Anthropic call routed through it and Strava-derived data structurally unable to enter it, is the highest-value change in the launch, because it turns the app's largest risks into a single testable invariant. The second pattern is that 2.5.19 rewards a smaller 1.0: what cannot be finished, approved or explained is better removed than hidden, and that applies to Strava as much as to the dev gallery.

Sequencing matters more than effort. The long-lead items sit outside the code: forming an entity before the bundle ID locks at first upload, Strava's capacity review, a legal read on age and health-data duties, and writing the policy, the health-data page, the terms and the support page. The page-by-page audit that comes next should run as the Accessibility Nutrition Label evaluation, so one pass yields the fixes, the labels and the screenshots. And two of the unanswered questions have a cheap route to an answer: Apple offers 30-minute App Review appointments, which is the place to ask about a health summary sent to an AI provider and about an individual seller with health data, and the Strava question can be put to its developer support in writing before any code depends on the answer.
