# Clear screens propose one next move

The best day planners, goal and habit trackers and journals make a screen readable in the same way. They draw today as **one shape that is both the status and the way in** (a short ordered list, a timeline with a "now" marker, a path, three numbers, a single "+"). They **propose the next move instead of asking for it**, through suggestions, defaults and pre-filled candidates. They keep one colored action, and they pay the reward at the moment of completion as something that fills. The evidence behind these habits is uneven, and this report keeps the grades apart. Rigorous research supports four things: simple, familiar screens win the first impression within 17 to 50 milliseconds; specific goals, when-and-where plans and recorded progress raise follow-through (meta-analytic d of roughly 0.3 to 0.65, lower after bias correction); a log that shows an intact streak motivates while one that shows a broken streak demotivates; and decoration that does not carry the content hurts comprehension. Practitioner belief, not research, carries the "one primary button" rule, the "top three" rule, the 5-second test and every claim about completion animations, sounds and haptics, and the vendor numbers (mostly Duolingo's) show streak mechanics moving retention by single-digit percentages. For Orby this means: Plan your day becomes one card that offers today's candidates as one-tap picks; Goals leads with today's goal and stops showing five "Not set" slots; the learned box keeps its question as a visible label, offers starters from the day and lights today's bulb on save, with a count in place of a chain; Fitness leads with today's session, one action and the week as a labeled gauge. Fitness gets one switch in Settings that removes the whole area and hands the Today hero to the day's goal, and home sections get an edit mode on the home screen itself with the hero locked. Two limits apply throughout: no researcher ran any of these apps, so every screen description comes from makers, reviewers or search snippets, and no public study links any of these patterns to outcomes in a lifestyle app.

## The clearest screens draw today as one shape, and position names the next move

Reviewers reach for the same words when a today screen works: calm, easy to scan, white space. Behind the words sits one structural choice. Each praised app gives the day a single organizing form that can be read without text. Things 3 uses a short ordered list with calendar events grouped on top and a "This Evening" section at the bottom, and its maker tells people to "pick whatever is at the top when you come back throughout the day" ([Cultured Code support guide](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/4001304/)). Structured, a 2026 Apple Design Awards finalist that Apple calls "visually sharp and easy to scan", draws one vertical column with a marker at the current moment, and its first-run screen holds **three elements: today's date, an empty timeline and a plus button** ([Apple Design Awards](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/); [Cool Curation review](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk)). Opal replaced a score its own users found "too complex and hard to read" with three numbers over one green-and-red hourly chart ([Opal blog](https://opalapp.com/blog/introducing-the-new-opal-home-screen-track-your-screen-time-and-improve-your-focus)). In every case the next action is answered by where something sits (top of the list, at the "now" marker, in red at the head of Todoist's Today) and not by a sentence of help ([Todoist help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/plan-your-day-with-the-today-view-UVUXaiSs)).

Goal, habit and wellbeing apps converged on the same answer after diagnosing the same problem. Duolingo rebuilt its home as a single path in November 2022 because learners were not sure they were using the app the "correct" way, and moved quests and challenges to another tab so the home screen had one job ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)). Oura cut five tabs to three in 2024 because members had "to sift through multiple data points", and its 2025 redesign leads Today with one insight called "One Big Thing" ([Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/new-oura-app-experience/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/10/20/oura-app-redesign/)). Headspace's 2026 Today tab brought back a three-item checklist "missed by many", a detail seen only in a search snippet because the help page refused the request ([Headspace help center](https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/51443842967195-Refreshed-Today-Tab-Experience-June-2026)). Streaks shows up to six circles and nothing else, a cap its reviewer says exists to "constrain users to an achievable number of goals" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/streaks-3-review/)). Apple Journal is a scroll of entries behind one prominent "+" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/apples-journal-app-journaling-for-all/)). Across these, the first object on the screen is both the answer to "how am I doing" and the thing to tap. The earlier measurement pass in this project found the same convention in 12 of 14 peer apps, with the focal element starting 11 to 32 percent of the way down the screen against 43 percent on Orby's Today (measured from App Store screenshots, accurate to about 5 to 8 percent; `research_notes/Mobile layout and visual hierarchy/app_teardowns.md`).

The complaints mirror the praise. A design critique of Finch says the daily checklist "visually competes" with shops, journeys and upgrade prompts on one home screen ([Pratt IXD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/)). A Whoop reviewer calls the home screen "daunting" for new users, and a member wrote in May 2026 that "several features and metrics I do not use at all" take up "prominent real estate" ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2023/03/28/new-whoop-home-screen-looks-pretty-but-is-it-as-intuitive/); [WHOOP community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/ability-to-simplify-home-page-in-app/14960)). TickTick's reviewer names "the sheer number of features" as the main cost ([TidBITS](https://talk.tidbits.com/t/ticktick-provides-a-focused-daily-task-list-and-more/31705)). Even Structured breaks when the day has no shape: a dozen short tasks compress into "an unreadable ribbon of colour" ([Cool Curation review](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk)). Simplifying has a cost too. Duolingo's path drew a petition from long-time users who lost the freedom to choose lessons, which argues for keeping an escape hatch when a screen is narrowed ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2022/10/13/duolingo-app-update-with-path-ui-faces-backlash-from-users/)).

Apple states the same standard as a test. A clear screen answers three questions without effort: "where am I", "what can I do" and "where can I go from here" ([WWDC25 359](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/359/)). Apple's tools for passing it are a plain title, one colored action ("refrain from adding color to the background of multiple controls") and a summary that states its point ([HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). Apple's own example of anticipating the next step is Workout's "one large button clearly labeled 'Record Outdoor Walk'" ([WWDC22 10037](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10037/)). Apple also warns about the exact screen Orby is redesigning: a Home tab becomes "the tab where every feature is fighting for real estate", and repeating other tabs' actions there "creates confusion" ([WWDC22 10001](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10001/)). GOV.UK's design system reaches the same rule from service research: more than one main call to action "reduces their impact, and makes it harder for users to know what to do next" ([GOV.UK Button](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)). Orby's Plan your day card currently presents a question, a button, a help paragraph, an add row and a link at similar weight, five elements where Structured's first screen has three. Its Goals page shows two chips, a card, a review row and a list as peers.

All of this is convention and expert guidance, not proof. No researcher could confirm element counts, button colors or placements in 2026 builds. The most detailed sources for Things 3 and Microsoft To Do are 2017 launch reviews. Five apps in the planner set (Akiflow, Amie, Motion, Notion Calendar, Fantastical) have no current primary description of their iPhone today screen and are left out of the argument. A hands-on screenshot pass should come before anyone quotes a number from these teardowns.

## The best apps propose a move; they do not ask an open question

The lowest-friction planning ritual in the set is an empty slate with suggestions one tap away. Microsoft To Do empties My Day every night, and yesterday's unfinished tasks "appear in your suggestions the next day" behind a light bulb ([Microsoft support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/ToDo/my-day-and-suggestions)). Any.do resets My Day each morning and offers Smart Suggestions that are accepted or dismissed with one tap ([Any.do help](https://support.any.do/en/articles/8609724-getting-started-with-my-day)). Structured uses on-device models "to suggest and pre-fill tasks", which Apple's judges singled out ([Apple Design Awards](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/)). Tiimo, Apple's iPhone App of the Year for 2025, opens the blank moment with "Not sure where to start?" and turns a typed or spoken brain dump into steps ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/ai-finds-its-way-into-apples-top-apps-of-the-year); [Tiimo](https://www.tiimoapp.com/)). Sunsama's 2026 flow replaces four named planning steps with one prompt ("describe your day") and a reviewable plan with undo, though the documented version runs on desktop and takes two to four minutes ([Sunsama help](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/usage-guides/daily-planning)). The first-add cost a reviewer bothered to count is Structured's **four taps**; suggestions bring it to one ([Cool Curation review](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk)).

The recurring complaint is planning that demands work before the app gives anything back. Users of Microsoft To Do ask for My Day to fill itself, and one says it "doesn't auto populate which seems rather redundant", a complaint seen in search snippets and not on an opened page ([Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/to-do/feature-request-auto-fill-my-day/3116274/replies/3511502)). Competitor-run blogs say of Tiimo that "before Tiimo can run your day, you have to build the plan", a claim seen only in snippets and to be weighed accordingly ([Habi comparison](https://habi.app/insights/best-adhd-planner-apps/)). A Bevel reviewer names the closest published match to Orby's "What would make today count?" card: the app "just gives you a target strain each day, which you don't really know how to implement" ([Autonomous review](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/bevel-app-review)). An open question with a button is a target without a next step.

Goals follow the same rule. None of the apps studied shows a ladder of empty horizon rows. Apple assigns ring goals and lets a person "Change for Today" without editing the week ([Apple Watch User Guide](https://support.apple.com/en-bw/guide/watch/apd29b30023c/watchos)). Whoop proposes a daily target from recovery ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2023/03/28/new-whoop-home-screen-looks-pretty-but-is-it-as-intuitive/)). Strides makes the horizon a property of one goal, shown as a pace line that turns green when on pace ([Strides](https://www.stridesapp.com/)). Longer horizons arrive as recaps: Gentler Streak's Monthly Summary is "less about hard comparisons and more about progress" ([Apple Design Awards 2024](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2024/)). Stoic links the horizons by time of day: a user who set a morning focus on reading was asked that evening what they had read ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stoic/id1312926037)). The claim that these apps avoid "not set" rows is an inference from what sources describe. No source shows the empty state of an unset goal in any of them.

Journals lower the bar the same way. Apple Journal's "+" opens suggestions built from the day's photos, workouts and places, and a suggestion can be saved with "Save Without Writing" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/apples-journal-app-journaling-for-all/)). Reflectly chains a mood slider, reasons picked by tap and an optional text step, a sequence its reviewer credits with removing "blank-page syndrome" (screens captured in 2018) ([Appcues GoodUX](https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/reflectlys-easygoing-daily-journaling)). Daylio's two-tap entry "sits at the extreme low-friction end of the market" ([Headway review](https://makeheadway.com/blog/daylio-review/)). MacStories called Apple Journal's plain entry field "bare bones, to say the least", which is the nearest published verdict on a text box with a Save button.

The usability guidance agrees and adds the few numbers available. Apple: "When possible, offer choices instead of requiring text entry", and prefill "reasonable default values" ([HIG Entering data](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/entering-data)). Nielsen Norman Group gives an empty area three jobs: state the status, teach in place, and offer a direct way to start ([NN/g empty states](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)). Shopify's and IBM's design systems cap an empty state at one primary action with a verb-plus-noun label ([Polaris](https://github.com/Shopify/polaris/blob/main/polaris.shopify.com/content/components/layout-and-structure/empty-state.mdx); [Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/empty-states-pattern/)). For a prompt above a field, both NN/g and Baymard report from their testing that placeholder-only labels hurt, because the question vanishes once typing starts; the fix is a visible label outside the field ([NN/g placeholders](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/); [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-forms-avoid-inline-labels)). Defaults matter because few people change them: **fewer than 5 percent** of surveyed Word users had changed any setting, in a practitioner survey whose sample size and year are not given, and in an NN/g search study the top result drew 42 percent of clicks against 8 percent for the second, and still drew 34 percent when the two were swapped ([Spool](https://archive.uie.com/brainsparks/2011/09/14/do-users-change-their-settings/); [NN/g, The Power of Defaults](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/the-power-of-defaults/)). The default state of each Orby screen is the design.

## Reward lands at completion, as a fill and never as a broken chain

The apps keep two tiers of reward apart. Every item gets a fast, quiet confirmation: Structured's completed tasks "strike through and fade", and Things animates everything "for pop" ([Cool Curation review](https://coolcuration.com/structured-app-review-uk); [Cultured Code](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/)). The day gets something rarer. Sunsama's request for a more satisfying completion has been open since 2019 with 38 votes, and in February 2026 the team shipped a celebration only for finishing all tasks or finishing a planning or shutdown ritual; users in the thread still ask for the per-task tier ([Sunsama roadmap thread](https://roadmap.sunsama.com/improvements/p/completing-a-task-should-feel-more-satisfying)). When the small tier is missing, people doubt the save: Finch's journaling "sometimes fail[s] to trigger animations, leaving users unsure whether their input was recorded" ([Pratt IXD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/)). Readwise's 2026 update puts "a completion screen worthy of your streak" after the daily review, and Reflectly congratulates after the entry ([Readwise](https://readwise.io/reader/update-aug2026); [Appcues GoodUX](https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/reflectlys-easygoing-daily-journaling)). Apple's guidance is to confirm completion "for activities that are sufficiently important" and to treat a finished list as a moment for "celebration" ([HIG Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback); [WWDC22 10037](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10037/)). **No rigorous public study tests completion animation, confetti, sound or haptics against repeat use.** The half-second timing sometimes quoted for Things' checkbox comes from a design study that does not say how it was measured ([Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/design-study-things)). This tier rests on user requests and reviewer taste.

Streaks are where research speaks directly. Across seven studies in the Journal of Consumer Research, people shown an intact streak did more of the behavior afterward than people shown a broken one, even when the underlying behavior was identical; the effect grew when people blamed themselves for the break and shrank when they could "repair" it ([Silverman and Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029); [abstract](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb)). The researchers read the abstract and press coverage, not the full paper; one press summary gives about 66 percent against 58 percent choosing to exercise again in one study. Habit research points the same way: missing one opportunity "did not significantly impact the habit formation process" in a 96-person study where automaticity took a median of 66 days ([UCL on Lally et al.](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)). A seven-slot row with an unlit bulb in the middle is precisely a log that highlights a broken streak.

The vendor numbers show the mechanics work and how small the effects are. Duolingo reports that letting one lesson extend the streak raised day-14 retention by 3.3 percent and daily active learners by 1 percent, with a stated downside: fewer learners reached their daily goal ([Duolingo, Improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). Its post on streaks says a second Streak Freeze raised daily active learners by 0.38 percent without more days off, and that a new streak animation raised seven-day retention of new learners by 1.7 percent ([Duolingo, streaks and habit](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/)); one researcher read those figures on Duolingo's blog and a second could not trace the animation figure to a primary source. These are self-reported A/B tests, not peer-reviewed. A widely repeated "CHI 2020 study" claiming streak trackers are 63 percent more likely to quit after one missed day has no findable paper behind it and should not be used. The apps Apple has honored forgive: Gentler Streak counts rest days toward the streak and its status setting (sick, injured, on a break) "removes Activity Path from sight and mind", and Apple's rings pause for up to 90 days "without breaking your award streak" ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gentler-streak-fitness-tracker/id1576857102); [Apple Watch User Guide](https://support.apple.com/en-bw/guide/watch/apd29b30023c/watchos)). For an app with one owner and no retention target, the relevant risk is the one a qualitative study of the Lift app found: habits built on streaks and reminders depended on the app and often stopped with it ([Renfree et al. 2016](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/dont-kick-the-habit-the-role-of-dependency-in-habit-formation-app/)).

"Visually engaging" has a research answer, and it is narrower than decoration. Appeal at first sight is driven mainly by low complexity and familiarity, and colorfulness "is not nearly as important as the overall visual complexity" ([Reinecke et al. 2013](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2013/reinecke13predicting.shtml)). A meta-analysis of 58 studies found that interesting but irrelevant additions lower learning, while a second found small positive effects when pleasant color and shape are applied to the content itself (effect sizes for both seen through secondary sources) ([WSU on Sundararajan and Adesope](https://news.wsu.edu/2020/03/19/seductive-details-inhibit-learning/); [Wong and Adesope 2021](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Meta-Analysis-of-Emotional-Designs-in-Multimedia/99900601054701842)). In small field studies, glanceable displays where the status itself was the picture (a garden on a phone wallpaper, a watch face) were associated with maintained activity ([UbiFit](https://www.consolvo.org/ubifit); [Gouveia et al. 2016](https://ktisis.cut.ac.cy/handle/20.500.14279/9337)). A prettier screen does not become easier to use: in a controlled experiment aesthetics did not change perceived usability, while poor usability lowered perceived beauty, and the pooled effect of aesthetics on actual performance is g = 0.12 ([Tuch et al. 2012](https://research.google/pubs/is-beautiful-really-usable-toward-understanding-the-relation-between-usability-aesthetics-and-affect-in-hci/); [Thielsch et al. 2019](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/owms/2019_thielsch_et_al.pdf)). The transfer from learning materials and websites to app screens is an inference. Its direction is consistent: make the status object the engaging thing (a plan filling in, a bulb lighting, a week taking shape), keep motion short and tied to a change the person caused, and honor the reduce-motion setting ([W3C SC 2.3.3](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions)).

## What the research supports, and where belief fills the gaps

The table grades the claims that carry the recommendations. "Rigorous" means a meta-analysis, a large or replicated experiment, or a peer-reviewed review. "Mixed" means a real but moderator-dependent, single-study or bias-sensitive effect. "Belief" means guidance, convention or vendor claim.

| Claim | Best evidence | Grade | Caveat the researchers attached |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple, familiar screens win the first impression | Appeal judged in 50 ms; complexity and prototypicality act within 17 ms ([Lindgaard 2006](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060109/full/news060109-13.html); [Tuch 2012](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/38315.pdf)) | Rigorous | Measures appeal, not comprehension. Desktop websites. The one phone-app study explained less variance and was seen at abstract level |
| One strongly different element guides the eye | Attention follows differences only "if those differences are large enough" ([Wolfe and Horowitz 2017](https://search.bwh.harvard.edu/new/pubs/FiveFactors_Wolfe-Horowitz_2017.pdf)) | Rigorous | General visual search, not app tests |
| One primary button per screen | Apple, GOV.UK and Google guidance | Belief | No published experiment isolates one call to action against several on app screens |
| Fewer options are always better | Choice overload averages "virtually zero" across 50 experiments; it appears with complex options, hard decisions, uncertain preferences and an effort-minimizing goal ([Scheibehenne 2010](https://abcwest.cogs.indiana.edu/pmwiki/pdf/scheibehenne.options.2010.pdf); [Chernev 2015](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual/)) | Mixed | Hick's law "speaks against, not for" naive "less is better" ([Liu et al. 2020](https://archivesic.ccsd.cnrs.fr/INFRES/hal-02867301v1)). Opening Orby to plan or jot fits the overload conditions |
| When-and-where plans raise follow-through | d = 0.65 over 94 tests; d of .27 to .66 over 642 tests ([Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes); [Sheeran et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563)) | Rigorous | Bias-corrected estimates of d = 0.15 to 0.35 come from a blog summary of the 2024 paper and need checking |
| Specific goals and recorded progress help | Goal setting d = .34; prompting progress monitoring d = 0.40, larger when physically recorded ([Epton 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29189034/); [Harkin 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91437/)) | Rigorous | Mostly health, work and lab settings |
| Near subgoals beat distant goals | Proximal subgoals built mastery and interest; distal goals "had no demonstrable effects" ([Bandura and Schunk 1981](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/59faaf5b01b9500001e95457/5bc552d85141987915dab842_Bandura%20&%20Schunk,%201981.pdf)) | Mixed | One classic experiment with children |
| A head start and nearness to the end speed people up | Coffee and car wash loyalty cards ([Kivetz 2006](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration-illusionary-goal); [Nunes and Drèze 2006](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/32/4/504/1787425)) | Mixed | Real rewards, not self-tracking. No replication found. Exact figures unverified. The reason given for the head start matters |
| A plain progress bar helps | Constant-speed bars did not cut drop-off in 32 experiments; bars that move fast early did ([Villar 2013](https://research.google/pubs/where-am-i-a-meta-analysis-of-experiments-on-the-effects-of-progress-indicators-for-web-surveys/)) | Rigorous | Web surveys |
| Unfinished tasks pull people back | People resume tasks; they do not remember unfinished ones better ([Ghibellini and Meier 2025](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w)) | Rigorous | The popular "Zeigarnik effect" failed; the resumption effect holds |
| Writing a plan quiets an unfinished goal | Plan making removed intrusive thoughts ([Masicampo and Baumeister 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024192)) | Mixed | Lab studies, no independent replication found |
| Pick a top three | None. Theses took 55.5 days against 33.9 predicted ([Buehler 1994](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/buehler-et-al-1994.pdf)) | Belief | Inferred from the planning fallacy. No study compares three, five and ten |
| Fresh starts motivate | Gym visits up 33.4 percent at the start of a week ([Dai 2014](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)) | Mixed | Correlational; a field experiment found no New Year effect |
| Points and badges motivate | Behavioral effect g = 0.25 and unstable; points raised output, not intrinsic motivation ([Sailer and Homner 2020](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/109056); [Mekler 2017](https://glassmanlab.seas.harvard.edu/annotated_works/mekler17.pdf)) | Mixed | Effect sizes via a secondary summary |
| A daily line on what you learned helps | Trainees who wrote for 15 minutes scored about 23 percent higher ([Di Stefano et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2414478)) | Mixed, leaning weak | Unreplicated working paper; a co-author's data-integrity record was not checked for this paper. Expressive writing averages r = .075 ([Frattaroli 2006](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/2006-frattaroli.pdf)). "Three good things" did not beat a placebo ([Mongrain 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24469930/)) |
| Customization fixes a busy screen | Many users never customize ([Mackay 1991](https://www.lri.fr/~mackay/pdffiles/CHI91.Triggers.pdf)); it should "enhance an already good experience, rather than try to fix a poor one" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/customization-personalization/)) | Belief plus one small study | No study of hiding features in a personal app |

Three readings follow. First, the strongest levers are about content, not chrome: a specific goal, a when, a recorded result. The Fogg behavior model, often cited for "make it easy and prompt at the right moment", has almost no peer-reviewed validation ([The Behavioral Scientist](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/?p=3058)); implementation intentions and defaults are the supported versions of the same idea. Second, the case for reducing visible options on Orby's four screens rests on the specific overload conditions and on visual search, not on Hick's law. Third, effects are small. In a megastudy of 61,293 gym members, fewer than half of expert-designed interventions raised visits ([Milkman et al. 2021](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/prediction/2021-milkman.pdf)). Every recommendation below is a hypothesis to check against the owner's own use.

## Turning an area off takes a switch in Settings and an edit mode on the home screen

The apps that handle this well offer two separate controls. Bevel is the closest model for Orby's Fitness switch. Its Customization screen, reached from the profile picture on Home, enables or disables Nutrition, Cycle and Bevel Intelligence, and "doing so also affects their related features and UI"; a feature can also be switched off from inside it through a Visibility setting, so the control is findable from either end ([Bevel help center](https://help.bevel.health/en/articles/10417089)). Separately, "Edit Home" at the bottom of the Home tab hides sections with a minus button, restores them through "Add card", reorders by drag and offers "Reset to default". Apple's Fitness and Health use the same in-place edit mode ("Edit Summary" at the bottom of the page; "Edit next to Pinned"), and third-party guides report that the Activity rings card at the top cannot be moved or deleted ([iDrop News](https://www.idropnews.com/how-to/how-to-customize-your-iphones-fitness-summary/227225); [iPhone User Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/view-your-health-data-iphe3d379c32/ios); [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2025/01/02/customize-your-fitness-app-summary-tab/)). Floors recur: Habitify requires at least one time-of-day block to remain ([Habitify help](https://intercom.help/habitify-app/en/articles/7990118-time-of-day)). Whoop shows the cost of stopping halfway: it lets members hide and reorder metrics, yet they still ask to move or remove whole sections ([WHOOP community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/feature-request-make-my-plan-my-journal-movable-above-my-day/14411)).

Apple's guidance sorts the two controls by place. Showing, hiding and reordering parts of a view belong "in the screens they affect, where they're discoverable and convenient", while Settings holds "general, infrequently changed settings", and there should be few of them ([HIG Settings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings)). Apple's rule "don't disable or hide tab bar buttons, even when their content is unavailable" targets an app that changes its own tabs unpredictably ([HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). A tab the person chose to turn off is closer to Apple's iPadOS customization guidance, where "people expect to hide non-essential tabs", choices persist and core tabs stay fixed ([WWDC24 10147](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10147/)). Apple gives no iPhone rule for user-hidden tabs, so removing the Fitness tab when Fitness is off is a reading of the guidance, not a quote from it.

Three gaps shape the recommendation. No source describes what Bevel's tab bar looks like after an area is disabled. No source says how a home screen with a single hero should fill in when its hero's subject is switched off; in card-grid apps the remaining cards close the gap and nothing is promoted, which does not work for a screen built around one focal element. And no app in the set documents an onboarding question that chooses which areas exist. Apple says to "postpone nonessential setup flows or customization steps" and ship "reasonable default settings" ([HIG Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)). Orby has one owner who is motivated to configure, so the "almost nobody changes settings" figure applies loosely, but the complexity cost of every visible toggle applies in full.

## What Orby should change, in priority order

The tables rank changes by how much clarity each buys and how well it is supported. Priority 1 items remove competing elements or supply the missing next move. Priority 2 items add the reward and the proposal. Priority 3 items are refinements. "Support" names the grade from the section above. Every UI change still goes through the brand guide, the layout standard and the brand-guardian check.

### Plan your day: one card, candidates on offer, the goal on top

The section should read as a short ordered list with one obvious entry, in the manner of Things and Microsoft To Do. It may summarize other tabs but should not repeat their actions or switch tabs on a tap, per Apple's Home tab warning.

| Priority | Change | Why | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | When today's goal is unset, show the question and the one Sky button only. Remove the help paragraph. Keep the add row and the "All to-dos" link as quiet rows. | No praised app relies on a help paragraph. One element must differ for the eye to find it. | Guidance and visual search (rigorous for attention, belief for the single-button rule) |
| 1 | Offer two to four one-tap candidates under the question: today's session, yesterday's unfinished goal or to-dos, a goal from this week. Accepting one sets the goal or adds the to-do. | Empty slate plus suggestions is the lowest-friction ritual; "a target without a next step" is the published complaint. | Teardowns and defaults (practitioner); recognition over recall (NN/g) |
| 2 | Once set, the goal is the top row with clear emphasis. To-dos sit beneath as verb-first rows in the order they will be done. Done rows go quiet (strike and fade). Show "2 of 3 done" as text or a segmented fill. | Position names what is next. Recorded progress is the best-supported reward. Unfinished, resumable items draw people back. | Rigorous (Harkin; Ghibellini and Meier) plus GOV.UK checklist guidance |
| 2 | Let an item carry an optional when, reusing Orby's Morning and Afternoon choices as one-tap chips. | When-and-where plans are the best-supported lever in the review, and this costs one tap. | Rigorous for existence, size uncertain |
| 2 | Mark the moment the plan is set (a brief state change on the card) and reserve a larger all-done state for the day. Keep per-item feedback short. Honor reduce motion. | Planning itself relieves the pull of unfinished goals; apps keep two reward tiers. | Mixed (Masicampo and Baumeister); belief for animation |
| 3 | Keep the limit soft: top three visible, the rest behind "3 more", never a block. Describe it as a convention. | No study supports a cap of three; opt-in lists and resets are the shipping norm. | Belief, with the planning fallacy as rationale |
| 3 | Start each day clean. Yesterday's leftovers appear in the carry-over row as candidates, never as an overdue wall. | Fresh-start framing; Microsoft To Do and Any.do reset on purpose. | Mixed (correlational) |
| 3 | Consider "tell the assistant about today, get a proposed plan, accept or edit" from the existing ask bar. | Sunsama and Tiimo moved this way. | Vendor direction only; Sunsama's version is slow and desktop-first |

### Goals: today's goal as the hero, no wall of "Not set"

The page answers "am I on track", and today it shows five empty slots at once (Tomorrow, Next week, This week, the month, the year). The apps studied show one horizon up front and bring the others in by pull or recap.

| Priority | Change | Why | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Today's goal card is the only hero and holds the only Sky button. Chips, review row and history stay visibly quieter. | One shape, one action. | Guidance; earlier measurement pass |
| 1 | Stop rendering unset horizons as "Not set". Show set horizons as rows in one card and collapse the unset ones into a single add row. This changes the current standard's three-row "Further out" card, so it needs the owner's approval. | Empty placeholders read as loud as content; no studied app shows a ladder of empty rows. | Teardown inference (not observed first-hand); complexity research (rigorous) |
| 2 | In place of a blank prompt, propose a goal: from the training plan, from yesterday's unfinished goal, or from the assistant, with one tap to accept and one to write your own. | Unset goals are avoided by defaults (Apple, Whoop, Duolingo); self-set specific goals still matter. | Practitioner for the pattern; rigorous for specific goals (Epton, Locke and Latham) |
| 2 | Give each longer goal a specific target, recorded progress and a near next step. Use a capacity gauge with both ends labeled and a one-line headline in words; use whole segments for small counts. Let the first action move the fill visibly. | Monitoring and near subgoals; a bar that crawls at the start is worse than none. | Rigorous (Harkin, Villar); mixed (Bandura and Schunk); Apple gauge guidance |
| 3 | Treat week, month and year as review moments in the existing review row, with a clean start on Monday and the first of the month. | Recaps carry long horizons in Gentler Streak and Oura. | Vendor practice; mixed (fresh starts) |
| 3 | Carry today's goal into the evening: the learned prompt can name it. | Stoic's morning-to-evening link is liked by its users. | User reviews only |
| 3 | Keep any goal setup short enough to finish in one sitting. | The ownership effect appears only when the building is completed. | Mixed (Norton, Mochon and Ariely) |

### "What did you learn today?": a labeled question, a tap to start, a bulb that lights on save

The box has a plausible but thin evidence base, so the design should make it feel like recording a gain and should promise nothing. Private writing is enough: sharing added no benefit in the one study that tested it.

| Priority | Change | Why | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep the question visible above the field as its label; use the field's hint only for a short example. Size the field for a sentence or two. Enable Save once there is text. | Placeholder-only prompts vanish on typing; field size signals effort; Apple gates the continue action. | NN/g and Baymard testing (research firm); HIG |
| 1 | Confirm the save in place: the field becomes the finished line, shown as a complete card, and today's bulb lights in the same moment. | Reward belongs after the action; saves without feedback leave people unsure. | Teardowns (Reflectly, Readwise, Finch critique); belief for the animation itself |
| 2 | Move the seven-day row to sit with the box as its result, and replace "N days in a row" with a count that cannot break, such as "5 of the last 7 days". Never show a reset to zero. Let a missed day be filled late. Print the count as text so color is not the only signal. This revises the current standard, which places a streak "in a row" above the prompt. | A log that highlights a broken streak lowers later engagement; repair softens it; one miss does not harm habit formation. | Peer-reviewed (Silverman and Barasch, read at abstract level; Lally) |
| 2 | Offer two or three starters drawn from the day (the session, the goal, a habit ticked). A tap inserts an opening; typing stays optional after it. | Apple Journal seeds entries from the day; recognition needs fewer cues than recall. | Practitioner; the only prompt-design study found (prompts that build on the person's previous answer work best) is small ([Kocielnik et al. 2018](https://faculty.washington.edu/garyhs/docs/kocielnik-UBICOMP2018-reflection.pdf)) |
| 3 | Keep the main wording stable and tie the box to a fixed moment, such as the evening after the plan is closed. | Habits form through repetition in a consistent context; repeated or oddly worded prompts are a named complaint in Stoic. | Mixed (Lally); user reviews |
| 3 | Later, resurface old lines ("A month ago you learned"). | Readwise's daily review; recorded progress is the better-supported reward. | Vendor practice |

### Fitness: today's session, one action, the week as a labeled gauge

The research notes do not describe the current Fitness screen in detail, so these are targets. The page answers "what is today's session and how is the week going" in the first screenful.

| Priority | Change | Why | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lead with today's session as the hero, with one verb-first action and enough detail to act on it. On a rest day, say so plainly and count it. | Priority | Change | Why | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Redesign the default Today as if no customization existed. | "Customization should enhance an already good experience, rather than try to fix a poor one." | Practitioner research (NN/g, Spool); one small peer-reviewed study |

### Checking the result

Each redesigned screen can be checked on a screenshot before anyone else sees it. Squint or blur it and confirm the eye lands on the hero. Cover the body text and read only the title and buttons; purpose and next step should survive ([WWDC22 10037](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10037/)). Count: one control with a colored background, one primary action in any empty state, no icon without a label, no field without a visible label. Then run a 5-second test and a first-click test with two or three people who have not seen the app, on single-purpose crops, since the method's best-known write-up says home pages "don't yield as valuable results" ([Perfetti](https://articles.centercentre.com/five_second_test/)). No source publishes a pass mark for any of these tests, and the documented duration is five seconds, not the three the current standard names. First-click results are best used to compare the old and new design on the same task: on live sites, a correct first click led to 78 percent success against 70 percent after a wrong one, far from the two-to-one ratio usually quoted ([MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/?p=193)).

## Conclusion

The owner's two requests, screens that are "visually engaging" and screens that are simple, turn out to be one requirement. Attention research says only one element can stand out, and the appeal research says added color and decoration buy little while added elements cost a lot. So engagement has to come from the status object itself: a plan that fills, a goal that advances on the first tap, a bulb that lights on save, a week that takes shape. Because Orby serves one person and has no retention number to move, it can skip the loss-framed mechanics that vendors tune for return visits and rely on the reward the evidence supports best, which is seeing that what was planned got done.

The research also relocates the problem. Three of Orby's four screens currently ask something (what would make today count, what are your goals, what did you learn) and wait. The apps people call clear answer first and let the person accept, change or ignore the answer. Orby already holds the material to do this: the training plan, yesterday's leftovers, the habits ticked, the day's goal. The riskiest assumptions sit where no one looked first-hand: the teardowns were never checked against the running apps, the "no empty horizons" pattern is an inference, and the reflection study behind the learned box is unreplicated. A one-hour screenshot pass through Things, Structured, Bevel and Apple Journal, and a few weeks of the owner's own use, will settle more than further reading.
