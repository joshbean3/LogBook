# Forgiving streaks make Orbs worth collecting

Orby should build one forgiving "days kept" run, small whole-number Orbs that appear at once and are never taken away, fixed multipliers by goal horizon capped at ten, and short opt-in sprints that the assistant words while code sets every number. The best evidence in this field is about forgiveness, not points. In randomized experiments, showing a broken streak cut follow-through below showing no log at all (45% against 61%), a repair path won back about two thirds of the loss, and a goal of "seven days with two emergency skips a week" beat both a strict and an easier goal (4.0 against 2.8 days a week). The most successful products converged on the same shape from their own data: Duolingo's largest published streak win came from lowering the bar to one lesson (day-14 retention up 3.3%, 40% more learners on a 7-day streak), while its milestone animation, the part most often copied, is its smallest (up 1.7%), and every one of those figures is company-reported with no published method. The honest expectation is modest. Game mechanics added to plain tracking give a small lift (about 0.2 standard deviations) that shrinks within three to six months, nearly every positive trial had a social arm that Orby lacks, and no peer-reviewed study tests point multipliers, collectibles, a single-user reward system or challenges written by a language model for their effect on behavior. Apple's rules leave room for an earned-only Orb with no purchase path, require explicit in-app permission before personal data goes to a third-party AI, and forbid urging people into challenges that risk physical harm. The Capacitor Haptics plugin offers three impact weights and three notification types and nothing richer, so the Orb's haptic today is one light tap on the pop's peak frame. Two decisions come before any build: Orby's brand guide forbids "an Orb as a habit coin", and the app already ships a points-and-levels game for goals with a strict daily streak that the new system should replace, not sit beside.

## The winning apps lower the bar, forgive misses and ration the big moment

### Duolingo's biggest wins were structural, and its animation was the smallest

Duolingo's streak today is "consecutive days with at least one completed lesson". Until November 2020 a learner had to hit a self-set daily XP goal. Splitting the two raised day-14 retention by 3.3%, daily actives by 1% and the number of learners on a 7-day streak by more than 40%, and the company found that almost 40% of learners active two days running with no streak had chosen the "intense" goal: the strict rule was denying streaks to its most ambitious users ([Duolingo blog, 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). Around that low bar sits layered forgiveness. Up to two Streak Freezes are applied automatically on a missed day, two are given free when a streak starts, and "Earn Back" restores a lost streak for a few lessons in place of the gems that repair once cost. The team reports that **three freezes were "no better than two"** and trained people to take longer breaks, that giving two free freezes at the start was "one of our biggest wins", and that dropping the bar below real work (one exercise in place of one lesson) moved daily actives "not one bit" ([Lenny's Podcast transcript](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/main/episodes/jackson-shuttleworth/transcript.md)). A streak with no freezes used is shown in gold and earns nothing extra, so forgiveness changes the look of a streak and never its count.

The published effect sizes rank the levers in an order that surprises people who copy the flame. Equipping two freezes in place of one raised daily actives by **0.38%**, and the redesigned post-lesson streak animation with its milestone version raised a new learner's chance of being active a week later by **1.7%** ([Duolingo blog, 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). Against that, about eight words explaining the rule were worth "over 10,000" daily actives, a button relabeled "Commit to my goal" was "a massive win", and a streak goal worked when the learner picked it and failed when it was preselected ([Lenny's Podcast transcript](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/main/episodes/jackson-shuttleworth/transcript.md)). All of this is company-reported: no sample sizes, intervals or holdouts are public, and Duolingo's former product chief says of its best-known streak correlation that "a lot of this was simply correlation and selection bias" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). A one-person app cannot detect a 0.38% lift. What transfers is the direction of each result: an easy, legible rule first, then forgiveness, then a chosen commitment, then polish.

Duolingo's corrections teach as much as its wins. Every reward counted in a raw, farmable unit was gamed. The monthly challenge moved from XP to quests because learners earned XP in bulk near month end, XP was rebalanced toward forward progress, and the company admits leaderboards invite grinding ([Duolingo blog, 2024](https://blog.duolingo.com/time-spent-learning-well/)). Players earned a thousand XP in hours by repeating easy exercises and stacking timed boosts ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/duolingo-app-cheats-hacks-leagues-xp-why-duohacker-1850506482)). The time-of-day chests that paid a timed double-XP boost were retired, and the boost that remains now starts by itself, which users dislike ([TechIssuesToday](https://techissuestoday.com/duolingo-xp-boost-and-daily-chests-changed)). Countdown multipliers are the least proven and most criticized part of the system. The team also turned down a metric win: XP ticking up during a lesson tested better and was removed to protect focus, so celebration sits at the end. On the first lesson of the day the streak screen appears once, with a counter tick and a buzz, and only round milestones (7, 30, 100, 365 days) get the full-screen animation ([Deconstructor of Fun teardown](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/streaks)).

### Fitness products count in weeks, cap every multiplier and keep adding rest

Strava chose the most forgiving unit available. Its streak needs one activity of at least 60 seconds per Monday-to-Sunday week, any activity type counts, and uploading a missed activity after the week ends repairs the streak automatically. Its celebration schedule thins with tenure: banners in weeks 2 through 5, then at 10, 15 and 25 weeks and at one, two and three years. The banners cannot be turned off, which Strava's own page concedes ([Strava Support](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401580-streaks-on-strava)). Strava has no currency and no multipliers. What a person keeps is a record: medals, a trophy case, personal bests.

Zwift is the only product in the set with a spendable currency and true multipliers, and every multiplier has a bound. Riding near a pacer raises the Drops rate from 1.2x to a stated ceiling of 2.5x and resets on leaving, and a Ride On doubles the rate for 15 seconds ([Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/how-drops-work/)). Its week streak pays a flat bonus that stops growing (300 XP, then 400, then 500 for every later week), and its Streak Savers are earned by consistency (one per 12 weeks), capped at two and applied automatically ([Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/week-streaks)). When riders coasted down long descents to earn equipment upgrades, Zwift changed the rule so upgrades needed "genuine efforts" and did not claw back what had been earned ([Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/upgrades-exploit-test/)). Peloton's lifetime points use the same restraint: a 10% or 20% boost for breadth across activity types and a single double payout at week 52 ([Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/what-is-club-peloton)). No product in this research multiplies by streak length without limit.

Apple is the reference case for a personalized challenge and for what goes wrong with one. The Fitness app generates a monthly goal from the wearer's history with no opt-in and no edit, and a decade of complaints describes a ratchet: goals that jump after any personal best, including one of 9,324 calories in a month ([iMore](https://www.imore.com/hey-apple-watch-can-you-give-it-rest); [Apple Support Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254056118)). Apple has since moved toward rest and toward counts that cannot be lost. watchOS 11 added ring pausing "for a day, week, month, or more" and per-weekday goals ([BGR](https://www.bgr.com/tech/watchos-11-activity-rings-have-big-changes-heres-whats-new/)), and iOS 18.2 added cumulative "All Rings Closed" awards at 100, 365, 500 and 1,000 days that are totals, not consecutive runs ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/11/20/apple-watch-all-rings-closed-awards/)). The pause was not carried through every time-boxed award: a user who paused for a flight lost a monthly challenge despite a three-day buffer ([Apple Support Community](https://discussions-kr-prz.apple.com/thread/256220397)). Whoop and Oura solve rest at the rule level. Whoop's strain target follows the day's recovery ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/the-locker/strain-coach/)). Oura's Rest Mode switches the activity goal off and eases it back over as long as the rest lasted, to a maximum of seven days, and its activity score counts how often the goal was met in the past seven days in place of a chain ([Oura Help](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/lv/articles/360057065433); [Oura](https://ouraring.com/activity-score/)).

### The gentlest habit apps hold the best retention figures found, and withdrawn rewards cost trust

Habit apps span the full range of consequences for a miss. Habitica deducts health and, at zero, takes a level, all gold and a piece of gear ([Habitica Wiki](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Death)). Forest leaves a dead tree in the forest "as a record" ([Forest](https://forestapp.cc/llms-full.txt)). Headspace resets the run to zero, and one user admitted to "fast-forwarding through his meditations simply to keep his streak going" ([Headspace Help](https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/215730567-How-does-the-run-streak-feature-work); [Mindful.org](https://www.mindful.org/what-does-headspace-want/)). Finch never harms the pet: a missed day can be repaired, a free repair saver is earned every three adventures with at most two held, and Pause Mode preserves the streak ([Finch Help](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/37780736136205-Understanding-Streaks); [Finch Help](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/37936144770701-Pause-Mode)). Finch separates three things other apps bundle: the streak (repairable), growth from tasks (never lost) and a soft currency (spendable). The only retention numbers found sit with this gentle end, and they are a third party's estimate, not Finch's: day-1 and day-7 retention of 54% and 37%, against 51% and 35% for Duolingo ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/x0hd2ssr80y5n7gv0w967pg7hwd7tl)).

Three cautionary cases set the outer limits. Fitbit removed challenges and trophies in 2023, and in the May 2026 move to Google Health told users that "historical badges will be deleted", handing celebration to an assistant-style coach ([Google Health Help](https://support.google.com/googlehealth/answer/17068213?hl=en)). Garmin's double badge points for subscribers were attacked as pay-to-win ([Gadgets and Wearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/?p=16585694)). Robinhood dropped its confetti in 2021 and in 2024 accepted a $7.5 million consent order that cites confetti and "digital scratch tickets" as game-like features tied to a risky, repeatable action ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/03/31/robinhood-gets-rid-of-confetti-feature-amid-scrutiny-over-gamification.html); [ThinkAdvisor](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/18/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-over-gamification-practices/)). People treat granted rewards as property, resent shortcuts to earned status, and regulators single out celebration that mimics games of chance. The well-run apps also ship an off switch: Todoist lets people turn goal celebrations and the whole Karma score off ([Todoist Help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/introduction-to-karma-OgWkWy)), while Asana users report celebration creatures still appearing after both switches were off ([Asana Forum](https://forum.asana.com/t/how-to-turn-off-childish-unicorns/578094)). A switch that does not work is worse than none.

## Trials back slack and own-baseline goals, and the lift is small and fades

### An intact streak pulls, a displayed break pushes away, and slack softens the fall

The core mechanism has independent, experimental support. Across seven studies, Silverman and Barasch found that highlighting an intact streak raises later engagement and highlighting a broken one lowers it, and that the effect follows what the log shows, not what the person did. In one study 92.5% continued when an intact streak was highlighted against 64.9% when it was not, while **45.2% continued when a break was highlighted against 60.9% when it was not**. A break blamed on the person did the most harm (28.9% continued, against 42.0% when the app was at fault and 53.2% with the streak intact), and a repairable break held 85.2% against 68.7% for a plain break and 93.1% for an intact streak. Streak length made no difference in the range tested (3 against 6, 4 against 20), and whether the log was public or private did not matter ([Silverman and Barasch, author manuscript](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb)). These are single-session online choices. The one field dataset, 980 employees in a 30-day step challenge, is correlational, and in it a just-broken streak hurt about four times as much as an intact one helped.

Built-in slack is the best-tested softener and it comes from a randomized field experiment. Sharif and Shu assigned 273 people a daily step goal at 120% of their own baseline under four weekly rules. People with "seven days, two emergency skips a week" hit the goal on **4.00 days a week, against 2.83 for a strict seven-day goal and 3.11 for an easier five-day goal**, and they were more likely to succeed the day after a miss (0.55 against 0.37). The weekly reserve helped at every level of self-control, while a larger monthly pool helped mainly people with high self-control ([Sharif and Shu, working paper](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Designing-More-Effective-Goals-by-Using-Emergency-Reserves-A-Field-Experiment.pdf)). Timing matters: slack applied right after a failure kept 78.2% going against 54.7% when applied beforehand, and a free bonus point at the start did almost nothing (66.3% against 60.2% for the strict goal) where an emergency point reached 84.7% ([Sharif and Shu 2021](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Sharif-Shu_EmergencyReserveFailure_OBHDP2019.pdf)). A small student replication in dieting found no advantage ([Utrecht thesis](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/44466)). A megastudy of 61,293 gym members adds the return side: the best of 54 four-week programs paid a bonus worth about nine cents for coming back after a missed workout and raised weekly visits by 27%, though only 8% of programs showed any change after they ended ([Milkman et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v600y2021i7889d10.1038_s41586-021-04128-4.html)).

Streaks also bend behavior toward the number. When GitHub removed streak counters without notice, long streaks were abandoned and single-contribution days became rarer, a sign the counter had been driving token effort ([Moldon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02371)). In Silverman and Barasch's pilot survey, 27% of app users had done something offline purely to keep a logged streak, including "a quick 7-minute workout in the back of a bar". For a training plan with rest days and deload weeks, that is the failure to design against. The reassurance is that one missed day did not measurably affect habit formation in the standard 12-week study ([UCL News](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)).

### Points raise quantity for a few weeks, and the proven recipes are social

The pooled effect of gamification on physical activity across 16 randomized trials is a Hedges g of 0.42, but against an active control such as a plain tracking app it is **0.23**, and 12 to 24 weeks after the game ends it is **0.15**. The effect is not significant once studies at high risk of bias are excluded, trials of 12 weeks or longer show no significant pooled effect, and the number of game mechanics did not predict the outcome. Fifteen of the 18 reviewed trials included social incentives, and the review "cannot determine" which element does the work ([Mazeas et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8767479/)). Orby already tracks, so the smaller figure is the fair expectation, and one or two well-made elements are as defensible as many. In the largest Penn trial every game arm raised steps, but only the competition arm held a clear gain after the game stopped (569 steps a day) ([Patel et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6735420/)). That recipe (points endowed each Monday and lost on a miss, weekly resets, lifelines for sick days, a partner who sees the result) is the most replicated one in the literature, and a one-person app cannot use its social half. Novelty decays on a similar clock: a 14-week classroom study found the gamification effect dropped after about four weeks and partly recovered later ([Rodrigues et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6)).

On motivation, the classic meta-analysis of 128 experiments found that expected, tangible rewards contingent on doing a task reduced later free-choice interest in tasks people already enjoyed (d of -0.28 to -0.40), while positive feedback about competence raised it (d of +0.33) ([Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/)). Symbolic points sit between those poles. In a controlled experiment, points, levels and leaderboards increased the quantity of output and left intrinsic motivation and quality unchanged ([Mekler et al. 2017](https://glassmanlab.seas.harvard.edu/annotated_works/mekler17.pdf)). Etkin's six experiments show the specific risk for a runner who already likes running: measuring an enjoyable activity raised output and lowered enjoyment, and the cost disappeared when the activity was framed as serving a goal the person held ([Duke Fuqua summary](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/etkin-counting-steps)). A direct test of autonomy-supportive wording in a digital health tool found no effect of language, and offering a real choice mattered more ([PubMed 31670693](https://www.pubmed.gov/31670693/)). The safest reward on this evidence is informational (it states what was done), chosen, and tied to the person's own goal.

### Immediate and own-baseline beat delayed and fixed, jackpots fail, and multipliers are untested

Timing and framing carry the incentive trials, not amounts. Immediate small rewards produced 746 more steps a day than delayed ones in a four-month trial, and adaptive goals declined at less than half the rate of static ones ([Adams et al. 2017](https://doaj.org/article/671a91b2123b4f438a87e367207c93f2)). Machine-set personal goals beat a fixed 10,000 steps by 960 steps a day over ten weeks, mostly by preventing decline ([Zhou et al. 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5806006/)). Range goals ("2 to 4") left performance unchanged and raised the chance of signing up again ([Scott and Nowlis 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-670766.html)). A stretch target that only about 20% reached lowered later engagement among those who missed it, most strongly among the most invested ([Wang et al. 2016](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mksc.2015.0966)). Paying people to exercise in a fixed two-hour window built weaker habits than paying for exercise at any time ([Beshears et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v67y2021i7p4139-4171.html)). Loss framing (points given up front and removed per miss) is the most effective frame in the Penn trials, at 45% of days against 30% for control ([Penn Medicine](https://www.pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2016/february/to-encourage-physical-activity)), but it is the same mechanism that makes a broken streak hurt, and those trials paired it with lifelines.

Randomness needs care. In a daily lottery trial, a 1-in-400 jackpot did worse than control over time, and only a mix of frequent small and occasional larger prizes beat control (goal met on 38% of days against 26%) ([Patel et al. 2018](https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/a-randomized-controlled-trial-of-lottery-based-financial-incentives-to-increase-physical-activity-among-overweight-and-obese-adults/)). Modest uncertainty about the size of a guaranteed reward raised effort in short lab tasks ([Shen, Fishbach and Hsee 2015](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/679418)). Prompts wear out fast: in the HeartSteps trial a walking suggestion raised steps by 24% in the next half hour on average, but the effect fell about 3% a day, was undetectable by day 29, and participants called the suggestions boring after two to four weeks ([Klasnja et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6401341/)).

**No peer-reviewed experiment tests point multipliers, double-points windows or bonus periods on health or habit behavior.** The nearest evidence is adjacent. Pay that rises with each consecutive task beat larger flat pay in six preregistered experiments with 4,493 participants ([Mehr et al. 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v187y2025ics0749597825000032.html)), which supports rewarding continuity. The stretch-goal study above shows the cost when a bonus is lost, and a coffee-card field study shows purchase speed resetting to its slow starting rate after a reward is redeemed, alongside a real head-start effect (a 12-stamp card with two stamps filled was finished in a median of 10 days against 15) ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf)).

### The documented harms follow pressure, not monitoring

The best-documented costs are lost enjoyment, token effort, fading and rebound. Links between tracking and eating-disorder symptoms or compulsive exercise are real as associations and mostly cross-sectional, and randomized studies in general samples found no causal effect of calorie tracking ([BJPsych Bulletin review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8485346)). A 2025 systematic review of 38 studies still found regular users of diet and fitness apps more likely to show problematic habits around food and exercise ([Flinders University](https://news.flinders.edu.au/blog/2025/02/22/fitness-apps-fuelling-disordered-eating/)). The variable that separates harm from benefit in the available data is pressure: among adolescents, feeling pressed to hit technology-set exercise goals was associated with compulsive exercise, while using technology to monitor one's own exercise was associated with positive outcomes ([International Journal of Adolescence and Youth](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/02673843.2024.2371397)). A systematic review of wrist trackers in healthy adults concluded that the effect on psychological wellbeing is understudied ([Scudds and Lasikiewicz 2024](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s41347-024-00442-6)).

| Claim | Grade | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Showing an intact streak raises follow-through, and showing a break lowers it below no log | Rigorous, short horizon | Seven online experiments, one correlational field dataset |
| Two skips a week, applied at the miss, beat strict and easy goals | Rigorous | One randomized field experiment, four lab studies, one small null replication |
| A small reward for returning after a miss raises attendance while it runs | Rigorous | Megastudy of 61,293 people, and 8% of programs lasted |
| Game mechanics add about 0.2 SD over tracking and fade to about 0.15 | Rigorous, fragile | Meta-analysis of 16 trials, not significant in low-bias studies |
| Own-baseline, adaptive goals hold activity better than fixed ones | Rigorous | Three randomized trials, all on steps or walking |
| One lesson a day, two freezes, a chosen goal and one line of explanation raise retention | Company-reported | Duolingo A/B results with no published methods |
| Gentle apps retain better than punitive ones | Third-party estimate | Sensor Tower figures for Finch against Duolingo |
| Multipliers should be fixed, capped and never tied to streak length | Inference | Zwift and Peloton practice, the stretch-goal and post-reward findings |
| Small integer Orbs, dated collection, widening thresholds | Inference | Economy-design practice and product teardowns |
| A code-bounded menu makes model-written sprints safe | Inference | Numeric-reasoning error rates and company practice, no direct test |

Evidence is missing in specific places, and the design below should be read with these holes in view. No randomized trial compares a days-in-a-row display with a never-reset total, or tests streak freezes in a health app. No study covers very long streaks or the size of the drop when one breaks. Nearly every trial measures steps, so evidence for strength training, running plans, journaling or to-dos is thin. No study follows symbolic points for months in a fitness app and then removes them. No research covers reward systems in single-user apps, which collectible mechanics outlast novelty, the best cap for a multiplier, or how often a surprise challenge can appear before it stops feeling special. No published timing exists for any leading app's completion animation or haptic, so every duration in this report is practitioner synthesis.

## Apple permits an earned Orb, the web view limits the haptic, and Orby's own guide objects

### An earned-only Orb stays outside the purchase and gambling rules

App Store Review Guideline 3.1.1 requires in-app purchase for in-game currencies that unlock features, forbids purchased currency from expiring, and requires apps selling "loot boxes" or other randomized virtual items "for purchase" to disclose the odds beforehand. Section 5.3 governs sweepstakes, contests and real-money gaming, with a warning of "extra time during the review process" ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Every one of those sentences speaks of things purchased, prizes or real money. An Orb that can only be earned, cannot be bought, unlocks nothing that is otherwise sold and wins nothing of real-world value sits outside them. That reading is an inference from the text, not an Apple ruling. The same text says what would change it: the moment Orbs or anything they unlock can be bought, in-app purchase, no-expiry and odds disclosure all apply. Guideline 1.4.5 is the hard line for challenges: "Apps should not urge customers to participate in activities (like bets, challenges, etc.) or use their devices in a way that risks physical harm to themselves or others." Guideline 1.4.1 adds that apps "should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions".

### Sending personal data to a model needs explicit, in-app permission

Guideline 5.1.2(i), revised on November 13, 2025, reads: "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." Section 5.1.1 requires an easy way to withdraw consent and collection of only the data "required to accomplish the relevant task". Sections 5.1.2(vi) and 5.1.3(i) bar HealthKit and fitness data from advertising, marketing and use-based data mining. Section 4.7 makes the developer responsible for chatbot output and calls for filtering and a way to report content ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai)). In the United States, the FTC says a fitness app that can sync with a wearable is likely a vendor of personal health records, that "sharing of covered information without an individual's authorization" triggers breach notification, and that penalties reach $53,088 per violation ([FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-ftcs-health-breach-notification-rule-0)). In the European Union, Article 50(1) of the AI Act has required since August 2, 2026 that people be told they are interacting with an AI system unless that is obvious ([EU AI Act, Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). Whether that article reaches a small app that wraps a third-party model was not researched. Orby already keeps an append-only consent log with switches for training, health, recall and reads (`packages/shared/src/schemas.ts`), so the machinery for a recorded, revocable permission exists.

### Apple's design guidance asks for brief, causal, optional feedback

The Human Interface Guidelines reduce to five rules for a reward moment. Use system haptics for their documented meaning: the Success notification "indicates that a task or action has completed", and impacts are "a physical metaphor" for things that land or collide. Keep a clear cause, because a haptic that does not reinforce cause and effect "can be confusing and seem gratuitous". Ration it, because a haptic "can feel just right when it happens occasionally, but become tiresome when it plays frequently". Prefer short haptics. Make it optional ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)). The motion guidance adds: "generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently", and "don't make people wait for an animation to complete before they can do anything, especially if they have to experience the animation more than once" ([Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)). With Reduce Motion on, Apple asks for fades in place of movement, tighter springs, and no "animating into and out of blurs", and it prefers "dismissing views with an explicit action" over timers ([Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)). A reward chime is a nonessential sound, so it must fall silent with the switch and must not stop the person's music ([Apple HIG: Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio)). Apple's own sample of a "significant moment" pairs one haptic and audio asset with a 500-millisecond animation and warns: "Don't add feedback just because you can" ([Apple WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10278)).

### The Capacitor plugin gives taps, not a designed swell

The Capacitor Haptics API has six calls: `impact` with Light, Medium or Heavy, `notification` with Success, Warning or Error, three selection calls, and `vibrate` with a duration ([Capacitor docs](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/haptics)). The version installed in this repo (8.0.2) creates a fresh, unprepared generator on each call, dispatches it asynchronously and resolves the promise before the haptic plays, so exact frame sync is not guaranteed. Rigid and Soft impacts, intensity, and Core Haptics patterns with synced audio are out of reach, and `vibrate` is one flat full-strength buzz that Apple's "prefer short haptics" rule argues against. The most admired habit-app checkbox, in (Not Boring) Habits, uses exactly what the plugin lacks: a sound that "swells slowly and then releases in a satisfying 'ka-thunk!'", with the haptic generated from the sound file and shipped as an AHAP pattern ([Andy Allen](https://notbor.ing/words/the-most-satisfying-checkbox)). Reaching that needs a small custom native plugin, a pattern this repo already uses for Apple Health (`packages/capacitor-health`). Haptics do not work in the simulator, so tuning needs a physical iPhone ([Apple WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10278)).

Orby's wrapper already makes the right mitigations (`apps/web/src/lib/haptics.ts`): named events, one per action, fired on the visual peak frame, a Settings switch, and a timed-phrase helper that drops any step more than 120 milliseconds late. It defines `orb` (a Light impact for a habit lit), `success` (documented as "exactly two a day") and `celebrate` (Heavy, then Success 120 milliseconds later, "at most once a week"). For smoothness in the web view, animate only `transform` and `opacity`, which stay on the compositor, and use `will-change` sparingly ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). Search-level evidence says Web Audio in iOS WebKit is treated as ambient sound that obeys the mute switch while HTML media elements ignore it ([WebKit bug 237322](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322)). That needs a device test before any chime ships.

### Orby's own documents set limits the research did not

Four local facts shape the design as much as any study. First, the brand guide's rule for the mark reads "One Orb per screen, the assistant's", and lists as a "Don't": "Two Orbs on one screen, or an Orb as a habit coin, a bullet or a decoration" (`docs/brand/brand-guide.md`, section 2.1). A collectible Orb that pops beside the ask bar breaks that rule as written, and the project's standing instruction is that the guide wins. Second, the experience principles already ration celebration ("Celebration is one or two moments a day", "Streaks in weeks, rest counts", "No zero as a headline") and the guide already defines a milestone stamp for a streak or a finished block (section 8.4) and bans the word "streak" from the Journal page (`docs/experience.md`; `docs/brand/layout-and-hierarchy.md`). Third, a points game already exists for goals (`packages/shared/src/goals.ts`): 50, 200, 500 and 2,000 points for day, week, month and year goals, 10 to 30 per to-do, a streak bonus of 5 points a day capped at ten days, levels, and milestones at 3, 7, 14, 30, 50, 100, 200 and 365 days. Its day streak is strict: a day with no goal, or a goal partly done, ends it. Fourth, the training side already has the forgiving rule the evidence favors: `weekStreak` counts weeks kept, with rest, floor sessions and sick days counted as kept, and reads "7 of the last 8 weeks" after a miss (`packages/shared/src/week.ts`).

## The design that follows: one kept-day run, whole Orbs, capped multipliers, bounded sprints

Everything in this section is a design derived from the evidence above. Where a number is a choice with no study behind it, the text says so.

### Streak rules: an easy bar, two rest passes a week, and no announced break

Orby should show one daily run and keep the weekly training run it has. The daily run counts **days kept**, and a day is kept when at least one Orb is earned that day. That is Duolingo's lesson in Orby's terms: the bar is one real thing done, the rule fits in a line, and a strict rule (today's "the day's goal must be done") denies a run to the most ambitious days. Because any Orb keeps the day, a planned rest day with one habit lit is a kept day, and no exertion is ever needed to save the run, which removes the "workout in the back of a bar" incentive at the rule level. A day on which nothing was due is passed over and is not a miss.

Forgiveness follows the reserve experiment directly: **two rest passes each Monday-to-Sunday week, with no rollover, applied automatically on the morning after a missed day**. Two a week is the arm that won the field trial and helped at every level of self-control, applying slack at the moment of failure is what the lab studies support, and two is also where Duolingo found the ceiling. A third miss in a week ends the run quietly. Nothing announces it, in the app or by notification, because a highlighted break did worse than silence. The headline moves to counts that cannot be zeroed: lifetime days kept, the best run, and a rolling "kept 5 of the last 7 days" line of the kind the training card already uses. The first Orb after any missed day carries one extra comeback Orb, the megastudy's best arm in symbolic form. A single Pause switch for illness, injury or travel freezes both runs, suspends training sprints, and must extend or cleanly close every open sprint and period goal, since Apple's pause failed exactly there. Activity should ease back over as many days as the pause lasted, to a maximum of seven, as Oura does.

Per-habit runs should leave the headlines. The habit sheet already shows "N of M" days, and that rolling count is the form that one miss cannot break. Milestones can keep the existing list (3, 7, 14, 30, 50, 100, 200, 365), which is front-loaded and then sparse like Strava's and Duolingo's. The length of a run should change the moment and never the payout, since the experiments found no added pull from longer streaks and the GitHub data found token effort. Whether to add an Earn Back style repair (do something the next day and the run resumes) is a second step to hold in reserve. Repair has experimental support, but repaired streaks motivated less than intact ones and Duolingo saw extra protection lengthen breaks, so the pass and the comeback Orb go first.

### What earns an Orb: one Orb for one real thing, paid at once and banked after Undo

The economy rules come from the exploits on record. Pay for the behavior and never for bookkeeping (Todoist pays for adding tasks, which is farmable). Pay the whole more than the parts. Keep awards as small integers so each Orb reads as an object (Zwift's millions of Drops a year stop meaning anything per unit). Cap the repeatable sources. Prefer verified sources for the larger awards. With no sink the Orb count is a score, and a single currency is enough, since dual currencies exist to separate paid from free players ([Unity game economy guide](https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide)). The existing goal points should convert into Orbs so that the app has one system.

Immediate reinforcement outperformed delayed in two trials, and an accidental tap must not mint an Orb. Both are met if the Orb appears on the tap and is banked into the collection when the Undo toast closes. Unchecking after that takes the Orb back. The values below are design choices. Their ratios follow the principles, and no study supports the specific numbers.

| Event | Orbs | Conditions that bound it |
|---|---|---|
| A to-do done, or a step on a longer goal | 1 | The first three a day earn |
| A habit lit | 1 | The habit was due today and existed before today |
| A journal line written | 1 | Once a day |
| The day's session done, run or lift | 2 | Once a day. The floor version counts. Confirmed from Apple Health or Strava where connected |
| An unplanned extra session | 1 | Once a day, never while paused, never a second hard session |
| The day's goal done | 1 x 2 = 2 | Partly done pays 1 |
| The whole day: everything due is done | +1 | Paid at Close the day |
| A weekly goal done | 1 x 3 = 3 | Set in the first half of the week |
| A monthly goal done | 1 x 5 = 5, as a rare dated Orb | Set in the first half of the month |
| A yearly goal done | 1 x 10 = 10, as a rare dated Orb | Set in the first half of the year |
| Every seventh kept day in a row | +1 | Flat. It never grows with the run |
| The first Orb after a missed day | +1 | Once per gap |
| A day sprint done | +1 | Stated when the sprint is offered |
| A week sprint done | +3 | Stated when the sprint is offered |

A full day pays roughly six to twelve Orbs, so a year is two to three thousand: large enough to build a collection and small enough to read. Training pays for the plan kept and never for miles, pace, calories or weight, which follows Etkin's finding that counting an enjoyed activity makes it feel like work unless the count serves the person's own goal, and keeps the reward clear of the eating-disorder risk factors. Rest days pay nothing by themselves and cost nothing, since any habit keeps the day.

### Multipliers: fixed by horizon, one per award, capped at ten

The multipliers the owner asked for (weekly tasks, all-day goals, long-term goals) fit the bounded forms that survive in the wild: a published ceiling, a flat bonus that stops growing, a one-time event. The table above uses **x2 for the day's goal, x3 for a weekly goal, x5 for a monthly goal and x10 for a yearly goal**, applied to a base of one, paid once at completion. Three bounds keep them honest. Only one multiplier applies to any award and multipliers never stack. The ceiling is ten. A multiplier attaches to what kind of thing was finished and never to streak length, because a multiplier that grows with a run makes late Orbs worth many times early ones and turns a miss into a cliff, the pattern the stretch-goal study warns about. The requirement that a period goal be set in the first half of its period blocks the end-loaded bulk earning Duolingo had to design out of its monthly challenge.

Long goals pay less per day of effort than daily ones by design. Proximal goals are where the motivational evidence sits, the steps toward a long goal earn along the way, and the finish is marked by rarity (a dated Orb of its own kind) and by the milestone moment, not by volume. No timed double-Orb windows and no countdown boosts: those are the least proven and most resented mechanics in the teardowns. The existing streak bonus in the goals code is bounded, but it rides on the strict streak, and it should give way to the flat seventh-day Orb. The arithmetic should always be shown ("1 Orb x 3 = 3") and the full rule set should fit one screen: what earns an Orb, the four multipliers, the caps, and the two passes.

### What Orbs are for: a dated collection that only grows

Orbs should buy nothing in the first version. A sink needs prices and balance, payment-for-protection is the direction the leading apps moved away from, and a collection gives lasting value in other ways the teardowns show. Each Orb is dated and carries what earned it, so the collection doubles as a record of the year. Thresholds at widening counts (a suggested 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500, 1,000, 2,500) change something visible, early ones close together, following the practitioner rule that "reward tiers should unlock quickly at first". A few rare Orbs exist for rare things (a monthly or yearly goal, a 100-day run, a personal record) and are earned, never rolled. A monthly and yearly look back is built from the same data. The dated record and the look back rest on pattern knowledge more than on sources.

Three guarantees protect the collection. Orbs never expire or decay. A rule change never claws Orbs back, as Zwift decided after its exploit. The collection is stored as durable user data and included in export, since Fitbit's deleted badges show what a collection is worth when the product can erase it. The whole layer needs one working switch in Settings that removes Orbs, the run and sprints together, plus a separate "Celebrations" setting that reduces every reward to its quiet form, apart from the existing haptics switch. If the look of an Orb ever varies by chance, publish the odds on the rules screen and avoid wheel, scratch-card or slot imagery. With no purchase path Apple's odds rule does not literally apply, and the disclosure costs nothing.

The one reward with direct support in the motivation literature is positive, informational feedback, and Google has just moved Fitbit's celebration from badges to a coach's words. A single factual line from the assistant at larger moments fits both, in the guide's existing voice ("Three mornings in a row. Nice work."). Its effect in an app is untested.

### The reward moment: a tick for most earns, a glowing Orb for some, a stamp for few

The research on game feel says moderate beats maximum: in a study of 3,018 players, medium and high levels of "juice" beat both none and extreme, per secondary summaries of a paper not read in full ([Kao 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcom.2020.100359)). Practitioner guidance puts most interface motion between 100 and 500 milliseconds and adds that "the more frequent the animation, the more subtle and shorter you'll want it to be" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)). The well-run apps use two tiers, a tiny acknowledgment on every completion and a large celebration at spaced milestones. Orby needs three, because the owner wants an Orb to appear on completion and the app's own rule allows one or two celebrations a day.

| Tier | When | What happens | Length | Haptic, using existing events |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tick | Every earn after the day's first | The control fills as it does today. A small flat Orb disc arcs to the counter, which adds one as it lands | Under 0.6 s, never blocks | `orb`: one Light impact on the pop's peak frame |
| Orb | The day's first Orb, the day's goal, a weekly goal, a day sprint, the whole day | A glowing Orb gathers on the finished item, pops, settles into the counter with a glow that fades. Any multiplier is stamped after the base lands | 0.6 to 1.1 s, any tap cuts it short | `orb` at the peak. `success` stays on Close the day, as now |
| Milestone | Run milestones, collection thresholds, monthly and yearly goals, a week sprint | A windup, a full-screen burst, the guide's milestone stamp, one line from the assistant | 2 to 4 s of motion, then it waits for a tap | `celebrate`: Heavy, then Success 120 ms later |

The everyday grammar is three beats: anticipation of about 100 to 150 milliseconds that starts on the touch frame, a payoff of 200 to 350 milliseconds with an ease-out or lightly damped spring and the haptic on its peak, and a settle of 300 to 600 milliseconds while the token lands. Those figures are a synthesis of NN/g's ranges, Apple's 500-millisecond sample and Allen's rule that "a big action needs a big wind up". No published standard exists, and they should be tuned on a phone with the owner. A multiplier is its own beat: show the base, hold for 150 to 250 milliseconds, stamp the multiplier, update the total once, so the cause stays readable as Apple's causality principle asks. Showing the fuller Orb once a day copies Duolingo, whose streak screen appears on the first lesson of the day only. When several completions land together, merge them into one flight with a count. Milestones are front-loaded by nature (runs of 3 and 7, thresholds of 10 to 100 all fall in the first weeks) and then rare, which matches the thinning schedules above. Outside the first two weeks they should not exceed the existing once-a-week budget for `celebrate`, with any extra falling back to the Orb tier.

Under Reduce Motion nothing scales, flies or blurs: the Orb cross-fades into its lit state, the number changes, and the haptic stays. Sound should wait for the custom haptic plugin and ship off by default, through Web Audio only and after the mute-switch behavior is confirmed on a device. Each of the visual and the haptic must carry the whole message alone. Skip screen shake, which the habit-app teardown left out and Reduce Motion argues against. Celebrate outcomes and never taps: Robinhood's confetti became a regulatory exhibit because it attached to a repeatable action, and the Orby equivalent would be an Orb for checking, unchecking and checking again. Expect the moment to lose its novelty within about a month. The lasting value has to sit in the collection and in the person's own reasons.

### Sprints: the assistant words them, and code decides what they may ask

The evidence on model-written coaching is encouraging on wording and thin on outcomes. Lay raters scored GPT-4 intervention messages above those written by laypeople and by healthcare professionals, in a study using simulated personas ([Haag et al., CHI 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.08658v3)). The weak point is numbers and completeness. Google's tool-using health agent, which writes and runs code, reached 84% exact-match accuracy on numerical questions about personal data, so **it was still wrong about one time in six** ([Merrill et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12855967)), and ChatGPT's exercise recommendations contained about 41% of the expected content ([UConn Today](https://today.uconn.edu/2024/02/are-chatgpt-exercise-recommendations-just-what-the-doctor-ordered)). No published randomized trial shows that model-written challenges or coaching change behavior over months. The products that do this best split the work. Google's Fitbit coach uses separate agents for conversation, data analysis and plan writing ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/how-we-are-building-the-personal-health-coach/)), Oura's rubric says a response "must not invent personal context" ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/how-oura-evaluates-generative-ai-to-earn-trust/)), and a 30-day study found that letting a simple algorithm pick the intervention type while the model writes the message kept the wording's acceptance with an explicit decision rule ([Song et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07275)).

For Orby that becomes a fixed pipeline. Code builds a short menu of candidate sprints from the person's data, each with its allowed parameter ranges. The model returns structured output only: a menu id, parameters from the supplied ranges, a title and a one-line reason. Code then checks the schema, the id, the bounds, that every number in the text matches a supplied fact, and that banned topics are absent. On any failure, timeout or refusal, a rule-based template for the same candidate is shown, so safety never depends on the model. All arithmetic stays in code. A "why this" line is built from supplied facts the person can verify. With little history the sprint is an easy starter and says so. No published study validates this menu-and-bounds pattern against free generation. It rests on the numeric-error results and on company practice.

| Rule | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Horizons | One day sprint and one week sprint, at most one active of each | The norm across Duolingo, Apple and Garmin |
| Enrollment | Offered in Today with an accept step, plus "Easier", "Harder" and "Not today" | Choice was the active ingredient in Duolingo's goal test and in the consent-language experiment |
| Difficulty | A level the person reached on roughly 60 to 85% of comparable recent days or weeks, framed as a range with the low end as success | Own-baseline trials and range goals. The band itself is an inference |
| Growth | A ceiling on any step up, a floor, and a reset after a pause. Targets come from the plan, not from beating last month | Apple's ratchet and the incentive to sandbag |
| Slack | Week sprints read "5 of 7 days" | The reserve experiment |
| Failure | The sprint expires silently. Nothing is lost and nothing is announced | Every product but Habitica, and the stretch-goal study |
| Reward | A stated, fixed bonus (+1 or +3). The surprise is which sprint appears and when, never the payout | The jackpot lottery failed and regulators target games of chance |
| Frequency | Day sprints on two or three irregular days a week and never two days running. A week sprint offered on Monday, with none in a down week or while paused | Habituation within two to four weeks. The numbers are an inference to tune |
| Notifications | None for day sprints. At most one optional push for the Monday offer, never Time Sensitive, with wording rotated | HeartSteps decay, and Duolingo's reminder model treats repeated wording as worn |

Training sprints need the tightest bounds. In a cohort of 5,205 runners, a single run more than 10% longer than the longest run of the previous 30 days carried a hazard ratio for overuse injury of 1.64, rising to 2.28 for runs more than double, while week-to-week change and the acute-to-chronic workload ratio did not predict injury ([Frandsen et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12421110/)). The study is observational and the injuries are self-reported, but it is the best current evidence and it points at the single session. So code must never offer a run longer than 110% of the longest run in the last 30 days, never add a hard session to a rest day or on top of the day's key session, and offer no training sprint while a pause, illness or injury flag is set. No comparable evidence exists for strength work, so a lifting sprint should only ever be "do the planned session", with no load or volume target. The safest sprints reward doing the plan as written, recovery habits, to-dos and journal lines.

The list of things a sprint may never ask is an inference from Guideline 1.4.5, the eating-disorder literature and the wellness rubrics, since no published checklist was found: fasting or skipped meals; calorie limits or "burn it off" targets; weight or body-measurement targets; training through pain, illness or injury; early-wake or late-night targets that cut sleep; dehydration or heat stress; maximum-effort or one-rep-max tests; back-to-back hard days beyond the plan; make-up sessions for missed ones; anything involving medication, supplements or alcohol; anything that needs the phone while moving in traffic; and exercise to save a run. Regression tests should replay a fixed set of synthetic user states on every prompt or model change, including no data, an illness flag, an injury note in the journal and a very high recent load, as Oura does for its advisor.

### Consent and disclosure: say what is sent, say it is the assistant, say it is not medical advice

If sprint generation sends habits, goals, journal text or training and Health data to a model provider, Guideline 5.1.2(i) requires an explicit in-app permission step before the first call, naming the third-party AI and what is sent. Developer commentary holds that a privacy-policy line alone is not enough. Orby's existing consent switches and log are the right home. Sprint generation must honor them (no health-derived facts unless the health switch is on) and record what was shown and agreed, which is also the record the FTC rule makes valuable. Data minimization points the same way as safety: send computed facts (counts, baselines, the candidate menu) and not raw journal entries or Health exports. The rule-based templates double as the full experience for anyone who declines.

The disclosure pattern used by comparable products has five parts: a label at first exposure and on each sprint that it was written by the assistant, the data categories used, who processes them and that they are not used for training or advertising, an off switch with a feedback and report control, and a wellness line. Strava's page for its generated summaries does the first four ([Strava Support](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401629-athlete-intelligence-on-strava)). For Orby the label is "From the assistant" in product copy, with the provider disclosure under the composer left exactly as written. One standing line near training sprints covers Guideline 1.4.1: "General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop if something hurts, and check with a doctor if you are unsure." The rewards layer needs its own plain disclosure on the one-screen rules page: Orbs are earned and cannot be bought, they have no cash value, nothing can be won, and here is what earns them. That page is also the cheapest proven lever in this report, since Duolingo's one-line explanation of its rule outperformed its animation work.

### Open risks

The largest risk is that the layer does little. The single-user case is unstudied, the proven recipes lean on competition or a partner, and the fair prior is a small lift that fades in months. Orby cannot run an A/B test with one user, so the check is personal: kept days per week before and after, and whether the owner still wants the Orbs on at week six, when the classroom data says novelty bottoms out. The off switch is the remedy if the answer is no.

Rewards can crowd out the reasons that matter. The undermining effect is strongest for activities a person already enjoys, and running is the likeliest casualty here. Paying for the plan kept and framing Orbs as a record of the person's own goals follows the one moderator with experimental support, but no long-run study of symbolic points in a fitness app exists to confirm that the framing is enough.

The run can still become the goal. Two passes a week and an any-Orb bar remove most token effort, yet the same generosity can drain the run of meaning: repaired streaks motivated less than intact ones, and Duolingo saw extra protection lengthen breaks. The pass count and the bar are the two dials to watch. Self-report is the related weakness: the caps and the Undo window are aimed at accidents, and one honest user can still inflate a collection by splitting tasks, at which point the Orbs stop being informative.

Model-written sprints carry confident-error risk that validation narrows and cannot remove. The menu, the bounds and the fallback keep an error from becoming an unsafe ask, but a wrong "why this" line would damage trust in the assistant across the app. The legal picture has gaps this research did not close: whether the AI Act applies to Orby at all, GDPR treatment of health data, Washington's My Health My Data Act and other state laws, and the App Store's notification rule 4.5.4, which was not re-read. None of this report is legal advice.

Several technical claims need a device before they are trusted: mute-switch behavior of Web Audio inside WKWebView, haptic latency through the plugin's unprepared generators, frame rate of the glow and burst on the oldest supported iPhone, and the conditions under which iOS suppresses haptics. Every duration and every Orb value above is a starting point. The injury bound rests on one large observational cohort of runners who mostly ran three or fewer times a week, and nothing comparable exists for lifting.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual order of work. The parts people picture first (the glow, the pop, the multiplier) have the weakest evidence and the smallest measured effects, and the parts that sound like housekeeping (what counts as a kept day, what happens on the morning after a miss, whether the rule fits in a line, whether the person chose the challenge) carry nearly all of the support. An Orb is worth collecting only if the run behind it survives an ordinary bad week, so the forgiveness rules are the product and the animation is the finish.

Orby also starts ahead of the products it would copy on the two points they reached late. Its training run already counts in weeks with rest and sick days kept, and its documents already forbid a zero as a headline. The larger change this report asks for is subtraction: one run in place of three kinds of streak, one small-integer currency in place of points and levels, fixed multipliers in place of a streak bonus, and sprints that expire without a word. A single-user app has no leaderboard to protect and nothing to sell, so it can afford the gentlest version of every mechanic. On the available evidence, the gentlest version is also the one most likely to still be switched on a year from now.
